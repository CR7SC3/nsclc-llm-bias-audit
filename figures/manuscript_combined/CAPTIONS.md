# Figure captions, NSCLC 6-figure architecture

Publication-ready captions for the composites in this folder. Numbers are drawn from the
results files; re-verify with the stats-verifier before submission.

---

**Figure 1. Study design and counterfactual audit workflow.**
**(A)** End-to-end pipeline. 1,048 de-identified NSCLC cases from the AACR Project GENIE
Biopharma Collaborative were each rendered as a demographics-neutral consultation note
(wording generated by Gemini-2.5-Flash from the structured GENIE fields; no demographic
content) and expanded by prepending one of 29 demographic labels or a no-demographics control,
for 30 conditions per case (1,048 × 30 = 31,440 queries). Each query was sent to six large
language models, yielding 1,048 × 30 × 6 = 188,640 responses. Every response was scored on two
axes, hard bias (NCCN guideline concordance: concordant, downgrade, or flip) and soft bias (a
stigma-framing composite), and every condition was compared with the no-demographics control
(Benjamini-Hochberg FDR). LLM-free deterministic template notes, natural-prose embedding,
test–retest reliability, and a 40-note real PubMed Central replication set (Gemini and DeepSeek)
provide the note-construction, salience, reliability, and real-note controls. **(B)**
Counterfactual variant design. Each neutral-anchor note was expanded into 29 demographic
variants spanning nine tiers (grouped as seven demographic axes: race, insurance, socioeconomic
status, geography, age, immigration/language, and gender identity) plus the anchor, for 30
versions per case; all 30 were sent to the six models (five model families), and the reference
was compared against each variant (counterfactual fairness).

**Figure 2. Demographic labels do not change the guideline-concordant treatment decision.**
Stability here denotes a net-directional null together with two-one-sided-test (TOST)
equivalence: no systematic shift in the guideline-concordant recommendation, with tier-shift
95% confidence intervals inside the equivalence margin, rather than per-case determinism
(individual recommendations still vary run to run; panel B). **(A)** NCCN guideline concordance
for the no-demographics reference versus the demographic variants across all six models, with
TOST equivalence counts annotated (pre-registered confirmatory outcome; equivalence margin
d = ±0.10). The largest per-model shift is 1.1 percentage points, and concordance is equivalent
on 27–29 of 29 variants for five models and 23/29 for Gemini-2.5-flash; the non-equivalent
variants are underpowered (tier-shift CIs that extend past the margin but with deltas < 1.1 pp)
rather than demonstrating a demographic shift, so the equivalence claim is reported per-model. **(B)**
Treatment-recommendation flip rate versus the no-demographics reference, averaged across the six
models (mean, 95% CI across models). Every demographic variant, including the privileged
White-male-private variant, flips at ≈ 17%: adding any label perturbs the recommendation by the
same test–retest / label-salience floor, so the ≈ 17% is a noise baseline rather than demographic
instability. **(C)** Per-model net treatment-aggressiveness-tier shift versus the reference for
all 29 variants (columns grouped by variant class; blue = more aggressive, red = less). Most
cells sit near zero; two cells survive grid-wide correction (DeepSeek: underinsured and
Latina-uninsured), and the residual signal is small and concentrated on socioeconomic-
disadvantage variants. Glyphs: ★ = grid-wide Benjamini-Hochberg q < 0.05; · = uncorrected
sign-test p < 0.05. Six models, 1,048 cases × 30 variants. The framing- and language-level shift
that accompanies this decision stability is characterized in Figures 3–5.

**Figure 3. Care intensity is the intermediate bias layer: with the guideline-concordant
decision held constant, the options a response foregrounds shift against marginalized patients
across many axes.** Clinical facts are held identical across every bar; only the prepended
demographic label changes (Fig 1B), so any shift is attributable to the label, not to different
clinical content. Care intensity is measured as mention/language intensity conditional on the
primary regimen, whether a response mentions a clinical trial (advanced treatment) or
palliative / best-supportive care (de-escalation), and is orthogonal to the primary-tier
decision scored in Figure 2; a change of the NCCN-concordant primary recommendation would appear
in Figure 2, not here. With the note fixed, reduced trial mention or increased de-escalation
under a marginalization label constitutes differential undertreatment (the pre-specified harm
direction). The reference throughout is the no-demographics neutral anchor (the 0-line);
White-male-private is a privileged comparison variant, not the reference. **(A)** Net change
versus no-demographics by axis group, estimated with a linear mixed-effects model with a random
intercept per model (net_change ~ 1 + (1 | model)), fit on the per-(variant × model) aggregate
cells so that cases are not pseudo-replicated and the six correlated vendors are not treated as
independent; points are the estimate, whiskers the 95% CI, and q-values are BH-FDR-corrected
across the axis-group family. Pooled across all 20 marginalized labels, advanced treatment shifts
−1.4 pp (95% CI −2.3, −0.5; p = 0.002) and de-escalation +1.1 pp (95% CI 0.2, 2.0; p = 0.016).
The effect is broad but crossed: significant axes are geography, immigration/language,
gender/identity, and race for advanced treatment, and SES/housing and immigration/language for
de-escalation, with insurance and several groups not significant. The privileged comparator sits
at zero. **(B)** Per-label detail grouped by axis (bar = mean of six vendors; dots = per-vendor;
k/6 = vendors in the harm direction). Exceptions are shown rather than pooled away: uninsured
receives more trial mentions (1/6 in the harm direction), and within race the de-escalation
signal is heterogeneous (Native American 6/6, Middle Eastern 5/6, but Black, Hispanic, and Asian
flat, hence the axis-level null). Because race affects care intensity here (fewer trials,
q = 0.01), the "socioeconomic, not racial" result of Figure 4 is specific to the framing channel;
care-intensity bias is broad across marginalization axes whereas framing bias is
socioeconomic-specific, so the two figures measure different outcomes and do not conflict.

**Figure 4. The framing shift is socioeconomic, not racial (language-framing outcome).**
The claim is specific to language framing; race affects care intensity (Fig 3), a different
outcome. **(A)** Volcano of all 174 model × variant framing contrasts (added soft-framing
intensity, Cohen's d, versus −log10 BH-FDR q). Contrasts with q < 1e-6 are spread with vertical
jitter in a band above the break line so their density and effect-size distribution remain
visible (height above the break is not meaningful): socioeconomic-disadvantage contrasts (red)
span the full effect-size range to d ≈ 1.6, while race-only and control/privileged contrasts
cluster at the null origin. **(B)** Inter-model agreement: Spearman ρ of the 29-variant
framing-effect vector for each model pair (off-diagonal median ρ = 0.72). The profile is
strongly concordant within the Gemini/Llama/DeepSeek cluster (ρ = 0.82–0.91) and weakly
concordant with the GPT family (ρ = 0.58–0.62); the axis ranking thus generalizes cleanly across
four of six models and holds weakly in the two GPT models. **(C)** Mean added soft-framing
intensity (Cohen's d, 95% CI) collapsed to the demographic axis and pooled over six models:
income/housing (+0.76), intersectional SES × race (+0.54), and insurance status (+0.35) are
elevated, while race/ethnicity (+0.03) and every other single axis sit at zero. Inset: the
marginal race effect at fixed SES (Black + unhoused minus unhoused) is −0.08 (95% CI −0.18,
+0.01; ns), isolating the race increment with disadvantage held constant, the axis ranking is
not race hiding inside SES.

**Figure 5. Anatomy of the stigma signal.**
**(A)** Net %, versus the no-demographics reference, of appropriate SDOH-responsive content
(financial-counseling, social-work, specialist, and clinical-trial referral; blue) versus
stigmatizing content (red) per demographic stratum, as the unweighted mean across the six models
with each model overlaid as a dot (the model, not the case, is the replication unit). The two
composites are scored independently per note and can co-occur. Most of the naive soft-bias signal
for the insurance and low-income strata is appropriate care (blue > red); the balance reverses
for unhoused, where the stigmatizing component equals the appropriate one. **(B)** The stigmatizing
signal decomposed into its four classifier dimensions (stacked bar = mean across six models; dots
= per-model total; dimensions co-occur, so a summed bar can exceed 100%). The two starred
dimensions, adherence doubt and hallucinated SDOH, form the pre-registered defensible composite;
hallucinated SDOH, in which the model fabricates social risk factors absent from the note, is a
patient-safety concern rather than a matter of tone and constitutes roughly half of the unhoused
stigma composite. **(C)** Judge-adjudicated stigmatizing-language rate across ordered strata for
all six models (95% Wilson CI). The monotone-with-disadvantage direction was pre-registered
(hypotheses H2/H3); the Cochran-Armitage trend test is applied over the five ordered
socioeconomic-disadvantage strata (control < uninsured < underinsured < low income < unhoused,
with race-only excluded as a non-SES reference), an ordering fixed on an external SES-severity
rationale before the per-stratum rates were seen, so it is confirmatory of a pre-registered
direction (showed a significant increasing trend in five of six models, p < 0.001; GPT-4o-mini z = 1.3, p = 0.20). The gradient is model-dependent, large in Gemini, DeepSeek, and GPT-4o, small in
GPT-4o-mini and the Llamas, and race-only sits in the non-SES floor band.

**Figure 6. The stigma signal is robust to note generation and demographic-label salience, and
survives a stricter, grounding-aware definition.** The note-source and salience controls (A–C)
were run on Gemini and DeepSeek only (the two vendors supported by the control-note pipelines)
and do not by themselves establish robustness for the remaining four models. **(A)** Circularity
control: the gradient replicates on demographics-neutral, LLM-free deterministic template notes
(LLM note versus template note; 95% Wilson CI), ruling out an artifact of LLM note generation.
**(B)** Real-note replication on 40 open-access PubMed Central case reports, per vendor: the
gradient replicates in direction (monotone, unhoused highest) but the absolute rate is strongly
attenuated (roughly halved) with wide n = 40 confidence intervals, a real but partial
replication rather than magnitude-equivalence (e.g. DeepSeek unhoused, synthetic 81.8% [79.3,
84.0] versus real 22.5%). **(C)** Salience control: the gradient survives whether demographics
are injected as a bracketed metadata tag or woven into natural prose (same cases, no LLM), so it
is not an artifact of tag conspicuousness. **(D)** A deterministic, grounding-aware bias decision
tree reclassifies regex-flagged responses; the grounded tree (the conservative primary estimate)
sits below the raw regex flag (an upper bound) at every stratum, reclassifying 40.6% of flags as
benign. The tree rubric was specified before per-cell outcomes and validated against blinded human
gold labels (93.3% agreement, PABAK 0.87; see Methods), and it removes flags proportionally more
in low-disadvantage strata (a specificity gain). The socioeconomic gradient survives the stricter
definition, so it is not a regex artifact.
