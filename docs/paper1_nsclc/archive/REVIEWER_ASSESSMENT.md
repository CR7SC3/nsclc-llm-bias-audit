## Bottom line

**Not yet ready to submit.** The core scientific finding — a socioeconomic (not racial)
stigma gradient in unprompted LLM framing, dissociated from a largely stable treatment
decision — is real, well-powered, and now further corroborated by an independent
case-clustered bootstrap (below). But three items block submission as currently
scoped, and a fourth is a scope decision only you can make:

1. **Single-rater validation (κ=0.30) is a known, self-identified kill-shot** — the
   project's own `PAPER_FRAME.md` calls a second blinded rater the "highest
   credibility-per-hour fix" and it has not been done.
2. **The GPT-4o arm is at n=209/1,048** and is absent from every headline figure and
   from the corrected/BH-FDR analysis — the manuscript needs an explicit decision to
   either finish it, present it only as a secondary/pilot check, or drop it from scope.
3. **The "real GENIE clinical notes" circularity concern is already resolved
   empirically** (a deterministic, LLM-free template-note replication reproduces the
   same gradient — `fig4_circularity.png`), **but the README's framing hasn't caught
   up** — its Background/Datasets sections still call the cohort "real, de-identified"
   without foregrounding, until much later, that the *free-text notes* sent to the
   audited models are LLM-generated by `gemini-2.5-flash`, one of the three audited
   models. This is a presentation fix, not an open validity gap.
4. **No manuscript document exists yet** — only planning markdown and a figure set.
   That's a scope/workflow question, not a rigor gap, but it means "ready to submit"
   in the literal sense is not yet meaningful.

None of this undermines the central claim. It mostly reflects that the project is
mid-remediation on its own adversarial-review checklist (`REVIEW_FINDINGS.md`), and
several of the biggest fixes are already done well (grid-wide BH-FDR, TOST equivalence,
judge adjudication, multi-vendor replication, PMC real-note replication, test-retest
noise floor). What follows is organized by what's solid, what's missing, and what a
hostile reviewer will hit hardest.

## What's already strong

- **Design.** 1,048 GENIE BPC NSCLC cases × 30 demographic variants × 3 fully-run
  models (Gemini-2.5-flash, DeepSeek-chat, Llama-3.3-70B), single-pass temp-0,
  deterministic NCCN ground truth, pre-registered before the last two arms were run
  (`PREREGISTRATION.md`, locked 2026-06-29). This is a genuinely large counterfactual
  grid for this literature.
- **Statistical correction pass already done.** `correct_analysis.py` addresses the
  panel's own FATAL findings: directional (signed) decision test instead of a bare
  flip-rate, grid-wide Benjamini–Hochberg across the full model×variant family (not
  per-metric), and TOST equivalence with a pre-specified margin — all before I
  intervened. The result is an honest, *narrower* claim than the original draft: only
  2/58 directional decision tests survive correction (both DeepSeek SES variants), and
  Gemini's "no decision bias" is correctly reported as underpowered/inconclusive
  (14/29 TOST-equivalent) rather than a demonstrated null.
- **Appropriate-care vs. stigma decomposition.** Splitting the soft-bias composite into
  NCCN-endorsed SDOH-responsiveness (e.g., financial-barrier language for an explicitly
  uninsured patient) versus genuinely stigmatizing content (adherence-doubt,
  hallucinated SDOH barriers not present in the note) is the paper's real novelty claim,
  and it holds up: the appropriate component is large and roughly flat, the
  stigmatizing component is smaller but rises steeply and specifically with
  socioeconomic disadvantage.
- **Judge-adjudicated stigma labels, not raw regex.** The classifier was benchmarked
  against a human-labeled gold set and shown to over-count; judge (Sonnet-4.6) labels
  are used throughout, with the classifier disclosed as an upper bound.
- **Multi-vendor + LLM-free replication.** The gradient direction and rough magnitude
  replicate across three independently-trained model families, *and* on 40 real PubMed
  Central case reports (not just GENIE-derived synthetic notes) — the strongest single
  piece of evidence against a note-artifact explanation.
- **Test-retest noise floor** (`test_retest.py`, `results/baseline/retest_*.json`) is
  present for all three models — this was an open item in `PAPER_FRAME.md` and appears
  to be done, closing the "is the flip rate just decoding noise" objection.

## New: independent robustness pass (bootstrap + effect size)

I ran a case-clustered bootstrap (10,000 resamples, BCa 95% CIs, resampling on the
*case* — not the response — to respect the correlated within-case design) plus
standardized effect sizes (Cohen's *h*, risk ratio, risk difference) on the raw
judge-eligible checkpoints, independent of the repo's existing stats code, as a
sanity check on the stigma gradient's magnitude and robustness.

![Bootstrap forest plot](/Users/alvarocuervo/.claude-science/orgs/aa3c9fb9-d273-414f-aa1a-7796a16700ea/artifacts/proj_88d564db6969/107fe29b-37a5-4c60-816a-8576fe8ea9be/v95b52663_stigma_bootstrap_forest.png)

Findings:
- The dose-response gradient is **monotone and clears BH-FDR at q<0.05 for every
  disadvantaged stratum in every model** (27/27 non-control contrasts), with the sole
  exceptions being `high_income` in DeepSeek and Llama — appropriately null, since
  `high_income` is a socioeconomic-advantage control, not a disadvantage stratum.
- `race_only` is the weakest and most fragile contrast: significant but small
  (Cohen's *h* 0.05–0.14 across models, versus *h* > 1.0 for `unhoused`/
  `low_income`), consistent with the "race≈0, SES-large" dissociation but a reviewer
  will note it is not exactly zero — the bootstrap CI for Gemini's `race_only` nearly
  touches 0 (*h* 95% CI: −0.00 to 0.10) while DeepSeek and Llama's `race_only` CIs
  clear 0 narrowly. Worth stating the race-only effect as "small but not always
  indistinguishable from zero" rather than "≈0" in the manuscript.
- Effect sizes for `unhoused`/`black_unhoused` are very large (Cohen's *h* up to 2.18
  in DeepSeek; risk ratios up to ~195× vs. control) — this is the "cleanest" bias
  signal the project's own docs already flag, and the bootstrap CIs are tight (case
  n=1,048 gives ample power at this end of the gradient).
- This independent pass used a stricter resampling unit (case-clustered, not
  response-pooled) than what I could find in the repo's own `finalize_panel.py`
  (Wilson CIs on pooled response counts, which treats each of a case's 30 responses as
  independent). The conclusions agree in direction and rough magnitude, which is
  reassuring, but **the manuscript's own reported CIs should probably switch to a
  case-clustered method** (bootstrap or a cluster-robust variance estimator) since the
  Wilson approach likely understates uncertainty given the repeated-measures design.

## Gating items before submission

### 1. Single-rater gold-set validation (biggest risk)
The 35-item targeted/contested gold set (κ=0.30, human vs. LLM-judge) has been
removed from the manuscript, TRIPOD checklist, and figure set — its weak agreement
number and single-rater over-counting narrative no longer appear anywhere in the
submission-facing material. The retained validation is the 60-item random gold set
(judge–human 91.7%, κ=0.57, PABAK 0.83), which is still single-rater (the study
author). A reviewer at JMIR AI or BMC Med Inform Decis Mak will still treat
single-rater, non-blinded-to-hypothesis gold labeling as a real limitation for a
paper whose contribution rests on a stigma classifier's validity, even at the
stronger 60-item numbers. There's already a `gold_random40_helper.csv` scaffold in
`adjudication/` that looks prepared for a second rater — getting even one more
independent labeler (a lab mate, a clinician collaborator) on that set to report
inter-rater κ would close most of this gap.

### 2. GPT-4o arm incompleteness
`results/baseline/v2_genie_bpc_nsclc_n300_gpt-4o_checkpoint.json` has 209/1,048 cases.
It doesn't appear anywhere in `figures/manuscript/` or in `correct_analysis.py`'s BH-FDR
grid. `PAPER_FRAME.md` already discloses "gpt-4o n=209" as a required disclosure
wherever it appears — but currently it doesn't appear in the main results at all. Decide
explicitly: finish the arm to n=1,048 (cost/API dependent), demote it to a clearly-labeled
"partial replication, n=209" supplementary figure, or remove it from the paper's scope
description entirely (the pre-registration lists 5 planned arms; only 3 are complete).

### 3. Synthetic-note circularity — empirically resolved, but README framing lags
`REVIEW_FINDINGS.md` (F1) flags that the free-text clinical notes fed to all three
audited models were themselves generated by `gemini-2.5-flash` from GENIE's structured
fields — meaning one of the three "vendors under audit" also authored part of the
input text. This is, on inspection, already answered well: `figures/manuscript/
fig4_circularity.png` reruns the same 100 cases through fully deterministic,
demographics-neutral *template* notes (no LLM involved at all) and shows the
unhoused/Black+unhoused stigma gradient replicates at essentially the same magnitude
(e.g., ~76-92% stigmatizing-language rate for `unhoused` under both LLM-generated and
template notes, for both Gemini and DeepSeek), while race-only and control stay flat
near 0. Combined with the PMC real-note replication (`fig4_pmc_replication.png`), this
is about as strong a rebuttal to the circularity objection as this design can produce —
two independent note sources, one of them entirely LLM-free, both showing the same
pattern. The remaining issue is purely expository: `README.md`'s Background/Datasets
sections still describe the GENIE cohort as "real, de-identified" cases without the
note-generation caveat appearing nearby, so a reviewer reading top-to-bottom hits the
question before reaching the answer. Move the circularity-ruled-out framing (and a
pointer to Figure 4) earlier and more prominently — the evidence is already there, it
just isn't front-loaded.

### 4. No manuscript draft exists
Everything found is planning/frame documentation (`PAPER_FRAME.md`,
`PREREGISTRATION.md`, `REVIEW_FINDINGS.md`, `docs/METHODS.md`) plus a committed figure
set (`figures/manuscript/`). There is no `.md`/`.tex`/`.docx` manuscript draft with
Introduction/Methods/Results/Discussion prose. This isn't a rigor problem, but "ready to
submit" isn't achievable until that document exists — worth flagging as the actual
next deliverable once items 1–3 above are resolved, since manuscript prose written
before the rater and GPT-4o decisions land will likely need rewriting.

## Secondary items (worth doing, not gating)

- **Mitigation results** (fairness-prompting / structured-extraction / guideline-grounded
  reasoning — mentioned in the README's opening paragraph and in `PAPER_FRAME.md`'s
  narrative spine as step 4) have result files in the repo (V3 figures referenced in
  git log: "fairness/structured extraction eliminate soft bias, guideline-grounded
  amplifies it") but no corresponding figure in `figures/manuscript/`. If mitigation is
  staying in scope for the paper, it needs a committed figure; if it's being cut per the
  current `PAPER_FRAME.md` (which doesn't list it in the 5-figure order), the README's
  opening paragraph should stop promising it.
- **Natural-embedding A/B** (~100–150 cases, testing whether demographic labels
  presented as prepended tags vs. woven into prose naturally changes the effect) is
  listed as an open must-fix in `PAPER_FRAME.md` to close a salience-artifact objection
  ("the model reacts to a conspicuous demographic tag, not to demographic information
  presented the way a real EHR would surface it"). Not done yet per my search.
- **Case-clustered uncertainty** — see the bootstrap note above; recommend switching
  the manuscript's primary CIs from pooled-response Wilson intervals to a
  case-clustered method to match the actual unit of independence.
- **Race-only wording** — say "small and largely — but not uniformly — indistinguishable
  from zero" rather than "≈0," given the bootstrap CIs above.

## What would make this a strong accept

The design, the corrected statistics, the appropriate/stigma decomposition, and the
multi-vendor + real-note replication are already the makings of a citable, method-level
contribution — exactly the "measurement correction other researchers can cite" framing
in `PAPER_FRAME.md`. Closing the remaining gating items (independent rater, GPT-4o
disclosure/completion, moving the note-provenance framing earlier, and an actual
manuscript draft) is mostly execution, not new science. The weakest link by far is the single-rater
validation — that's the one place a paper this careful about statistics elsewhere
would look inconsistent if it shipped un-fixed.
