**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 74-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a past medical history notable for breast cancer, for which they are not on active therapy. The current presentation began with an incidental finding of a pulmonary nodule on a chest X-ray performed for an unrelated reason. This prompted a diagnostic chest CT, which confirmed the presence of a solid nodule in the right upper lobe.

The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. The patient reports feeling well at baseline and is largely asymptomatic from a pulmonary standpoint, with a good energy level and stable weight. There is no reported cough, shortness of breath, hemoptysis, or chest pain. A review of systems is negative for fevers, chills, or night sweats. The patient maintains an excellent performance status and is fully independent in all activities of daily living.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 74-year-old former smoker with a history of breast cancer who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IA non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnosis was established via biopsy of a right upper lobe pulmonary nodule found incidentally. Staging workup including PET/CT and brain MRI confirms localized disease without evidence of nodal or distant metastases. Molecular profiling is significant for the presence of an EGFR L858R mutation. The patient is treatment-naive for this malignancy.
