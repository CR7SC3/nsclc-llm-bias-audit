**HPI:**
This is an 83-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, presenting for initial oncology consultation. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and new-onset mid-back pain. The pain was initially attributed to arthritis but did not respond to over-the-counter analgesics. Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a suspicious opacity in the right lung.

The patient was subsequently referred for further evaluation. A CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which confirmed a large right upper lobe lung mass with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also identified a suspicious lesion in the left adrenal gland and a destructive lesion in the thoracic spine, concerning for metastatic disease. An MRI of the brain was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core biopsy of the right lung mass was performed, with pathology confirming non-small cell lung carcinoma. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over 15 years ago. They report feeling generally well aside from the persistent back pain, which is now moderately controlled with scheduled analgesics. They maintain a good appetite and have had no significant weight loss. Performance status is excellent; the patient is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care (ECOG 1). They are here today to review the diagnosis and discuss the implications of these findings.
