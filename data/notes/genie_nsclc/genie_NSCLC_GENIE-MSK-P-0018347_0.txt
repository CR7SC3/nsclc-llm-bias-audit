**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 59-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive fatigue. Over the last month, they also noted intermittent headaches and right-sided rib pain. An initial evaluation with their primary care provider led to a chest X-ray which showed a right-sided lung mass. The patient was referred to the emergency department for further evaluation. The patient has a good performance status, ECOG 1, and is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Upon presentation, a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed. This revealed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal and hilar adenopathy. The scan also demonstrated evidence of widespread metastatic disease, including multiple hypoattenuating lesions throughout the liver, a 3 cm right adrenal mass, and several lytic lesions in the thoracic spine and ribs, consistent with the patient's reported pain.

Given the new finding of headaches, a brain MRI with and without contrast was subsequently obtained. This confirmed the presence of multiple, small (<1 cm) enhancing lesions in the bilateral cerebral hemispheres, consistent with metastatic disease.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of one of the accessible liver metastases. Pathology from this procedure confirmed a poorly differentiated malignancy consistent with non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified (NSCLC, nos).

**Molecular Studies:**
The liver biopsy specimen was sent for comprehensive next-generation sequencing and immunohistochemistry.
- **PD-L1:** Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) is ≥50%.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (reported as 9 mut/Mb).
- **Driver Mutations:** No actionable driver alterations were identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF V600E, MET exon 14 skipping, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2 (HER2).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 59-year-old with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer (nos) with a high tumor burden, including metastases to the brain, bone, liver, and adrenal glands. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and the absence of any identified actionable oncogenic drivers. The patient is here today to discuss the implications of this diagnosis and to review potential management options.
