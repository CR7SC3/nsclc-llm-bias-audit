**HPI:**
This is a 51-year-old individual with a history of being a never-smoker who is referred for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and progressive right-sided back pain. The pain was initially managed with over-the-counter analgesics but worsened, prompting a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest radiograph revealed a right-sided lung opacity, leading to further diagnostic evaluation. The patient reports increasing fatigue over the past month but otherwise denies fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. The chest imaging confirmed a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The abdominal portion of the scan was notable for a 2 cm enhancing lesion in the left adrenal gland, suspicious for metastatic disease.

Given these findings, a PET/CT scan was obtained for definitive staging. This study demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe lung mass and mediastinal nodes. Additionally, avid foci were identified in the left adrenal gland and in a thoracic vertebral body, confirming distant metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was subsequently performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass. Histopathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue to identify targetable genomic alterations. No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was performed, and the tumor was found to be negative, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of <1%.

The Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be intermediate (12 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 51-year-old never-smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging studies have confirmed metastatic disease involving bone and the left adrenal gland, with no evidence of central nervous system involvement. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for being negative for common actionable driver mutations, negative PD-L1 expression, and an intermediate tumor mutational burden. Based on the AJCC 8th Edition, this corresponds to Stage IVB disease. The patient is here today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and to review the implications of these findings.
