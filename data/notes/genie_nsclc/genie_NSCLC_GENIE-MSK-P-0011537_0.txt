**HPI:**
I had the pleasure of meeting this 68-year-old individual for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presents as a referral from their primary care provider after a diagnostic workup was initiated for a persistent, non-productive cough and mild dyspnea on exertion that began several months ago. The patient is a former smoker, having quit less than one year ago after a significant pack-year history. An initial chest X-ray ordered by the primary provider was abnormal, which prompted further investigation. The patient is otherwise feeling well, with a stable appetite and weight, and maintains an excellent performance status, fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest radiograph, a contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified]. This study revealed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy, with several nodes measuring up to 2.2 cm in short axis. No suspicious lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or osseous structures visualized on the scan.

To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was subsequently obtained. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity within the right upper lobe primary mass as well as in the right hilar and ipsilateral mediastinal lymph node stations, consistent with the findings on the diagnostic CT. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis and staging. A fine-needle aspiration (FNA) was performed on a subcarinal lymph node. Pathology from this specimen confirmed the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, with histologic features consistent with squamous cell carcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
The tissue obtained from the EBUS-FNA was sent for comprehensive molecular profiling and immunohistochemistry. Next-generation sequencing did not identify any actionable driver alterations; testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be intermediate, reported as 2–16 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 68-year-old, treatment-naive former smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of locally advanced, non-metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The diagnostic workup is consistent with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IIIA disease. The histology is squamous cell carcinoma. Molecular studies are notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%). The patient has an excellent performance status and is here today to discuss the diagnosis in detail.
