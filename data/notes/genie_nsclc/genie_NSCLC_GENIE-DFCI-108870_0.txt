**HPI:**
This is a 58-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to diagnosis, when they began to experience a persistent, dry cough and progressive fatigue. This was associated with an unintentional 15-pound weight loss over the preceding three months. The patient initially sought evaluation with their primary care provider, who ordered a chest radiograph that was suspicious for a right-sided lung mass. The patient is a former smoker with a 30 pack-year history who quit over one year ago. Functionally, the patient remains ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed for staging. This imaging confirmed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also revealed a 3.5 cm enhancing mass in the left adrenal gland, highly suspicious for a metastatic deposit.

To confirm the diagnosis and stage, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass on [De-identified Date]. Pathologic review confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

A subsequent staging PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right lung mass, multiple mediastinal lymph nodes, and the previously identified left adrenal mass, confirming distant metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. Analysis did not identify any actionable driver alterations. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations or fusions involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (12 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 58-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastatic disease to the left adrenal gland. Molecular profiling is notable for an absence of identifiable targetable driver mutations and an intermediate tumor mutational burden.
