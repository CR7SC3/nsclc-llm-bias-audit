**HPI:**
This 71-year-old is seen today in the Thoracic Oncology clinic for an initial consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker with a history of renal kidney cancer, status post prior treatment.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they began to develop a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. Symptoms initially were mild but have worsened, now limiting the ability to walk more than a few blocks. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider, who ordered initial imaging. The patient denies fevers, chills, hemoptysis, or significant weight loss. Performance status is estimated at ECOG 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a lung mass and a pleural effusion, which led to further staging studies.

A staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified Date]. This confirmed the presence of a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. A moderate-to-large right-sided pleural effusion was also identified. There was no evidence of contralateral lung nodules, or metastatic disease to the liver, adrenal glands, or bone. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided thoracentesis. Approximately 1 liter of serosanguinous fluid was removed, which provided significant symptomatic relief of dyspnea. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid confirmed the presence of malignant cells consistent with adenocarcinoma. The combination of a primary lung mass with a malignant pleural effusion establishes a diagnosis of metastatic disease.

Pathology from the pleural fluid cytology confirmed non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma subtype.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the cell block from the pleural fluid sample. The results are as follows:
- **Actionable Driver Mutations:** An **EGFR L858R** point mutation was detected. In addition, **high-level MET amplification** was identified.
- **Negative for:** ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2 rearrangements or mutations.
- **PD-L1 Expression:** Immunohistochemistry demonstrated a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the **1–49%** range.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Calculated as **intermediate** (2–16 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 71-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, metastatic to the pleura (cT_N_M1b). The patient is treatment-naive with a good performance status. Molecular profiling is notable for two co-occurring targetable alterations: a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation and high-level MET amplification. PD-L1 expression is low-positive. The patient has a remote history of renal cancer and is a former smoker. Today's visit is to discuss the diagnosis in detail and review the implications of these findings.
