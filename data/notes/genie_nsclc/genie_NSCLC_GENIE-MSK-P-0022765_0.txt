**HPI:**
This is a 55-year-old individual with a history of former tobacco use (quit >1 year ago) who is seen for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. These symptoms did not resolve with a course of antibiotics prescribed by their primary care provider. Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest radiograph was obtained, which revealed a right-sided pulmonary mass. This prompted an urgent referral for further diagnostic evaluation. The patient reports a 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past three months but denies fevers, night sweats, hemoptysis, or significant chest pain. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though their daily activities are somewhat limited by shortness of breath.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest x-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study characterized a 4.8 cm spiculated mass in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar adenopathy, with a right paratracheal lymph node measuring 2.5 cm in short axis. No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, or osseous lesions were identified.

A subsequent whole-body PET/CT scan was performed for staging. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the right hilar and right paratracheal lymph node stations (N2), consistent with the diagnostic CT findings. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease suggestive of distant metastases in the contralateral hemithorax, abdomen, pelvis, or skeleton.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also completed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS). Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) was performed on the right paratracheal (station 4R) lymph node. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, with an immunoprofile consistent with adenocarcinoma histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the EBUS-FNA.
- No actionable driver alterations were identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry (IHC) was negative, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 0%.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be low (<2 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 55-year-old, treatment-naive patient with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (clinical T2b N2 M0) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The patient has an ECOG performance status of 1. Staging workup, including PET/CT and brain MRI, has shown no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the absence of any targetable driver mutations and negative PD-L1 expression. The patient is seen today to discuss the diagnosis in detail and to review the implications of these findings.
