**HPI:**
This is the initial medical oncology consultation for a 67-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over a year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. Over the subsequent weeks, they also noted the onset of new, localized mid-thoracic back pain, which was constant and dull. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider. A chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a suspicious right upper lobe lung opacity. The patient reports some mild fatigue but has had no significant weight loss, hemoptysis, or fevers. They maintain an excellent performance status, continuing with all activities of daily living without restriction.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also identified multiple suspicious mediastinal lymph nodes, a lytic lesion in the T7 vertebral body consistent with a bone metastasis, and a 2 cm nodule in the right adrenal gland, suspicious for metastatic disease.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, several mediastinal lymph nodes, the T7 vertebral body lesion, and the right adrenal nodule. No other distant sites of FDG-avid disease were identified. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathology confirmed non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified (NOS) histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the lung biopsy.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified.
- Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was high, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was high at >16 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 67-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), NOS histology, with metastatic disease to bone and adrenal gland. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and high TMB, with no identified targetable driver mutations. The patient is treatment-naive and presents today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
