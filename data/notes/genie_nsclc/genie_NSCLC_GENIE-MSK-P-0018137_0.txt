**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 52-year-old with a recent diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit within the past year.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially managed as a presumed respiratory infection without resolution. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient noted progressive dyspnea on exertion and the onset of intermittent headaches. Due to the worsening headaches and a new episode of transient right-sided weakness, the patient presented to an outside emergency department for evaluation.

Initial workup included a chest CT, which revealed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with extensive mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. Given the neurologic symptoms, a brain MRI with contrast was performed, which demonstrated multiple enhancing lesions in the bilateral cerebral hemispheres, consistent with metastatic disease.

A subsequent staging PET/CT scan confirmed high FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and mediastinal lymph nodes. It also identified hypermetabolic activity in contralateral supraclavicular lymph nodes, confirming distant nodal metastatic disease (M1).

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass, which established the diagnosis. The patient is now referred to medical oncology for initial consultation and discussion of the diagnosis. The patient reports a good functional status, is fully ambulatory and capable of self-care, but is symptomatic from the disease (ECOG Performance Status 1).
