**HPI:**
This is a 71-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside facility approximately one month ago with several weeks of progressive shortness of breath on exertion and a persistent, dry cough. An initial chest X-ray was concerning for a left-sided pleural effusion and an underlying lung mass.

The patient was subsequently hospitalized for further evaluation. A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed, which confirmed a large mass in the left upper lobe and a significant, loculated left pleural effusion with associated nodular pleural thickening, highly suspicious for malignant involvement. No other sites of metastatic disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. Since the diagnostic workup began, the patient notes ongoing fatigue and dyspnea with minimal activity, but remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care. The current ECOG performance status is 1.

No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, osseous, or contralateral lung lesions were identified.

*   **Thoracentesis and Pleural Biopsy ([De-identified]):** The patient underwent a video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) for pleural fluid drainage and tissue sampling. Pathology from the pleural biopsy confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma. The morphology is consistent with a lung primary.
