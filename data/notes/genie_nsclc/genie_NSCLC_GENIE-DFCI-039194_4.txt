**HPI:**
This is a 70-year-old former smoker who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough. This was initially attributed to a seasonal illness, but due to lack of resolution and the new onset of mild exertional dyspnea, the patient sought evaluation from their primary care physician. They deny hemoptysis, fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. The patient quit smoking over a year ago. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which characterized a 4 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar adenopathy, concerning for nodal involvement. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was obtained. This showed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in multiple ipsilateral mediastinal lymph node stations, including the paratracheal and subcarinal regions. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease outside of the right hemithorax to suggest distant metastases. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis and definitive nodal staging. A transbronchial biopsy of the right upper lobe mass and fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node were performed. Pathology from both sites confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma. The positive lymph node confirmed nodal metastasis.

**Molecular Studies:**
The biopsy tissue was sent for next-generation sequencing. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations; results were negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 70-year-old with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, non-metastatic Stage IIIA (cT2aN2M0, AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. Molecular studies are negative for a targetable driver mutation. The patient has a good performance status and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
