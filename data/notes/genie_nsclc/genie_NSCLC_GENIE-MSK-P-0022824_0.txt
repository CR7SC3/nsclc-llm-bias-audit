**History of Present Illness**

This is a 73-year-old with a history of smoking (quit over one year ago) who is referred for initial evaluation and discussion of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and mild exertional dyspnea. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray showed a right-sided lung mass, which led to further cross-sectional imaging. The patient reports an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the last several months but denies fevers, chills, or significant chest pain. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though their symptoms are present with activity. ECOG performance status is 1. The patient is here today to understand the diagnosis and discuss the situation.

**Diagnostic Workup**

**Imaging:**
A diagnostic CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with intravenous contrast was performed. This study identified a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was associated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. No evidence of distant metastatic disease was identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or osseous structures.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT was subsequently obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in multiple right-sided mediastinal and subcarinal lymph node stations, consistent with locoregionally advanced disease. No FDG-avid lesions suggestive of distant metastases were seen.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastasis.

**Pathology:**
The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue acquisition. A fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node was performed. Final pathology confirmed invasive, poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

**Molecular Studies**

Comprehensive molecular profiling was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy.
*   **Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS):** No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
*   **PD-L1 Expression:** PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry (IHC) was negative (Tumor Proportion Score <1%).
*   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary**

In summary, this is a 73-year-old, treatment-naive former smoker with a new diagnosis of locally advanced Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer, squamous cell histology. Molecular profiling is notable for negative PD-L1 expression and no identified actionable driver mutations.
