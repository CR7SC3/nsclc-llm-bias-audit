**HPI:**
This is a 45-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, presenting for initial oncology consultation. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially managed symptomatically without resolution. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient developed new-onset, progressive mid-back pain, which was sharp, constant, and worsened with movement. Due to the severity of the pain and persistent cough, the patient sought further evaluation. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. They report a 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past three months. Despite the back pain, the patient remains fully ambulatory and is managing all activities of daily living, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest radiograph performed on [De-identified Date] was suspicious for a right upper lobe lung mass. A follow-up CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with intravenous contrast confirmed a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, with associated right hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The CT also revealed a lytic lesion within the T8 vertebral body, concerning for a metastasis.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT scan was performed. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, the mediastinal lymph nodes, and confirmed a solitary, intensely hypermetabolic focus in the T8 vertebral body, consistent with osseous metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was subsequently performed and showed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathological review of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the lung biopsy was submitted for comprehensive genomic profiling via next-generation sequencing.
- **Actionable driver mutation detected:** ERBB2 (HER2) exon 20 insertion.
- **Testing for other drivers was negative for alterations in:** EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, and NTRK. KRAS was wild-type.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (reported as 11 mutations/megabase).
- **PD-L1 expression:** Not assessed on the biopsy specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 45-year-old former smoker presenting with newly diagnosed Stage IV (cT2aN2M1b) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. Staging evaluation confirmed a primary right lung malignancy with mediastinal nodal involvement and a single site of distant metastatic disease in the thoracic spine. The patient has a good performance status (ECOG 1) but is symptomatic with back pain. Comprehensive molecular testing is notable for the presence of an activating ERBB2 exon 20 insertion mutation. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and to understand the implications of these findings.
