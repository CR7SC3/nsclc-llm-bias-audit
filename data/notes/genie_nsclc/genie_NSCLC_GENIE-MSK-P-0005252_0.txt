**HPI:**
This is a 79-year-old former smoker who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months ago, when they noted the onset of a persistent dry cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. These symptoms were associated with generalized fatigue and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the preceding three months. An initial outpatient chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass, prompting further diagnostic evaluation. The patient has a greater than 30-pack-year smoking history but quit over a decade ago. At present, they remain fully ambulatory and independent in all activities of daily living, though strenuous activity is limited by shortness of breath. ECOG performance status is 1. The patient is here today to discuss the findings of their workup.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified date]. This study identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. Additionally, multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules were noted, with several discrete nodules in the contralateral (left) lung suspicious for metastatic disease. No pathologically enlarged mediastinal or hilar lymph nodes were seen. The visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis were negative for metastatic disease to the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

To complete staging, a brain MRI with and without contrast was performed on [De-identified date] and revealed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass was subsequently performed on [De-identified date]. Histopathologic analysis confirmed poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung malignancy.

Based on the presence of a primary lung tumor with contralateral pulmonary nodules, the diagnosis is Stage IV (M1a) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue from the lung biopsy.
- An activating KRAS G12C mutation was detected.
- The tumor was negative for genomic alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 79-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (M1a) lung adenocarcinoma, characterized by a primary right lung mass with contralateral lung metastases. The patient is treatment-naive with an ECOG performance status of 1. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a KRAS G12C mutation.
