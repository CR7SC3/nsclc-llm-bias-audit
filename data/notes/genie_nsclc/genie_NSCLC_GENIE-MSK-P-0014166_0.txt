**HPI:**
This is a 59-year-old with a greater than 1-year history of smoking cessation who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to evaluation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild right-sided chest discomfort. These symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider, which led to an initial chest X-ray revealing a right upper lobe opacity. The patient reports mild fatigue but is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, maintaining an ECOG performance status of 1. They deny significant weight loss, fevers, or hemoptysis. The patient was referred to our service for further evaluation and discussion of management following a definitive diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This study characterized a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also revealed several enlarged ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes, the largest measuring 1.8 cm in the short axis, concerning for regional nodal involvement. No suspicious lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or other visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was obtained for staging, which demonstrated high FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the ipsilateral mediastinal nodal stations, consistent with the CT findings. Importantly, the PET/CT did not show any evidence of distant metastatic disease.

To complete staging, a brain MRI with and without contrast was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was successfully performed. Pathology confirmed the diagnosis of a moderately differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) of the tumor tissue was performed to assess for targetable alterations. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specifically, the tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1-49% range. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2-16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 59-year-old with a good performance status (ECOG 1) who presents with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, non-metastatic Stage IIIA (cT2bN2M0) non-small cell lung cancer, squamous cell histology. Molecular profiling is notable for PD-L1 expression (TPS 1-49%) with no identified actionable driver mutations. The patient is here for initial consultation and to discuss the diagnosis.
