**HPI:**
This is a 74-year-old individual with an excellent functional status who presents for initial medical oncology consultation to discuss a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months ago, when they noted the onset of a persistent dry cough and mild shortness of breath with exertion. These symptoms prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider, which included a chest radiograph that revealed a right-sided lung mass. The patient denies any associated chest pain, hemoptysis, fevers, night sweats, or unintentional weight loss. They remain fully independent with all activities of daily living and report an ECOG performance status of 0. Of note, the patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest x-ray, a diagnostic, contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study characterized a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the posterior aspect of the right upper lobe. There was also prominent ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy noted, concerning for nodal metastases.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT was obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the right paratracheal and subcarinal lymph node stations (stations 4R and 7), consistent with the CT findings. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the contralateral chest or at any distant sites, including the liver, adrenal glands, or skeleton. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic review of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

Based on the synthesis of these findings, the patient carries a diagnosis of clinical Stage IIIA (cT2aN2M0) non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue from the biopsy specimen. Molecular profiling did not identify an actionable driver alteration; the tumor was negative for mutations or fusions involving *EGFR*, *ALK*, *ROS1*, *BRAF*, *MET* exon 14, *RET*, *KRAS*, and *ERBB2*. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was evaluated and found to be high at >16 mut/Mb. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 74-year-old lifetime never-smoker with an excellent performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, non-metastatic Stage IIIA (cT2aN2M0) lung adenocarcinoma. Molecular studies are notable for a high tumor mutational burden without an identifiable oncogenic driver. The patient is here for initial consultation to review these findings.
