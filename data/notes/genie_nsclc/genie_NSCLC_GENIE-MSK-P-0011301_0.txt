**HPI:**
This is a 71-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who is seen today for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to an outside facility approximately two months ago with a persistent, dry cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, which prompted further investigation. The patient reports a 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past three months but denies fevers, night sweats, or hemoptysis. The patient remains active and independent with all activities of daily living, with an estimated ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified date]. This study identified a 5.1 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and right-sided supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, concerning for nodal metastases. No suspicious lesions were noted in the liver, adrenal glands, or osseous structures within the field of view.

To complete staging, a whole-body PET/CT scan was obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, as well as in multiple right-sided mediastinal and supraclavicular lymph nodes, consistent with the CT findings. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the contralateral chest or below the diaphragm. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified date], the patient underwent an interventional radiology-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
The biopsy specimen was submitted for comprehensive molecular profiling via next-generation sequencing. Analysis did not identify any actionable driver alterations; the tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range. The Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was classified as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 71-year-old former smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, non-metastatic Stage IIIB non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. The diagnostic workup confirmed a right upper lobe primary tumor with extensive ipsilateral mediastinal and supraclavicular nodal involvement, and no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling is notable for a PD-L1 TPS of 1-49% and the absence of an identifiable targetable driver mutation. The patient has a good performance status and is here to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
