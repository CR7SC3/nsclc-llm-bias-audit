**HPI:**
This is a 59-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild dyspnea on exertion. This was initially managed with outpatient symptomatic therapies without improvement. Due to worsening symptoms and the development of some right-sided chest discomfort, a chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a right upper lobe opacity.

The patient was subsequently referred for further evaluation. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest confirmed a large mass in the right upper lobe with associated bulky mediastinal and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. The patient denies significant constitutional symptoms such as fevers or night sweats but does report an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the last three months. Current performance status is excellent; the patient is fully ambulatory, capable of all self-care, and continues to manage light household activities (ECOG 1).

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Staging workup has been completed. A PET/CT scan performed on [De-identified Date] demonstrated a hypermetabolic primary mass in the right upper lobe. There was extensive, intensely hypermetabolic lymphadenopathy noted in the right hilar, paratracheal, and supraclavicular nodal stations, consistent with locoregionally advanced disease. Importantly, the PET/CT showed no evidence of distant metastatic disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed by Interventional Radiology on [De-identified Date]. Pathologic analysis of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of invasive, poorly differentiated non-small cell lung cancer with features most consistent with adenocarcinoma histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alterations were identified. Specific testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 59-year-old with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, locally advanced, unresectable Stage IIIB (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma subtype. Staging studies confirm disease is confined to the chest and regional lymph nodes. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations and high PD-L1 expression.
