**HPI:**
This is a 52-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. These symptoms were initially managed with a course of antibiotics for presumed bronchitis without resolution. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient noted the onset of dull, aching pain in the mid-back and a 10-pound unintentional weight loss. Due to the persistence of symptoms, the patient presented to their primary care provider who ordered initial imaging. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. The patient maintains a good performance status, fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though limited in physically strenuous activity due to dyspnea.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A chest CT with contrast was performed, which revealed a 4.8 cm spiculated mass in the left lower lobe with extensive mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also noted multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules, a 2.1 cm hypodense lesion in the right lobe of the liver, and a 3.0 cm left adrenal nodule.

For definitive staging, a PET/CT scan was obtained on [De-identified date]. This study confirmed high metabolic activity in the primary left lower lobe mass (SUV max 14.2) and in mediastinal, hilar, and supraclavicular lymph nodes. Additionally, multiple FDG-avid foci were identified consistent with widespread metastatic disease, including in the contralateral lung, the liver, the left adrenal gland, and multiple osseous sites including the T8 vertebral body and the right iliac crest. No evidence of brain metastases was seen on the CT portion of the study.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the left lower lobe mass was subsequently performed. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed invasive, moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing performed on the tumor tissue identified an actionable driver mutation, specifically an EGFR exon 19 deletion.
Additional molecular testing was negative for ALK and ROS1 rearrangements, as well as for mutations in BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2.
Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was performed, revealing a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range.
Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be high (>16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 52-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnostic workup confirms widespread metastatic disease involving contralateral lung, liver, adrenal gland, bone, and distant lymph nodes. Molecular profiling is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion, PD-L1 expression of 1-49% TPS, and high TMB.
