**HPI:**
This is a 58-year-old individual with a history of smoking, who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months ago, when they began to experience a persistent, dry cough and progressive shortness of breath with exertion. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider, which initiated a diagnostic workup. The patient reports some associated chest discomfort but denies hemoptysis, fevers, or significant weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and are managing all activities of daily living, though their exercise tolerance is now limited by dyspnea. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, leading to a follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest. This study confirmed the presence of a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The CT also demonstrated extensive and bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, concerning for locally advanced disease.

For comprehensive staging, a PET/CT was performed. This demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, as well as in the right hilar, multiple right-sided mediastinal, and right supraclavicular lymph node stations. No FDG-avid lesions suggestive of distant metastatic disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bone were identified. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic examination confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung malignancy. Based on the integrated imaging and pathology findings, the patient is staged as having Stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
The tissue from the diagnostic biopsy underwent comprehensive next-generation sequencing. Analysis for targetable driver mutations was performed. No actionable driver alteration was identified; testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, and NTRK. KRAS and ERBB2 were also found to be wild-type.

Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). A loss-of-function mutation in the KEAP1 gene was also identified. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on this specimen, as it was not standard of care at the time of the initial biopsy.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 58-year-old former smoker presenting with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive, Stage IIIB (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The disease is locally advanced, involving extensive regional lymph node basins, but the comprehensive staging workup, including PET/CT and brain MRI, is negative for distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the absence of any currently actionable driver mutations and the presence of a KEAP1 loss-of-function mutation. The patient is here for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis.
