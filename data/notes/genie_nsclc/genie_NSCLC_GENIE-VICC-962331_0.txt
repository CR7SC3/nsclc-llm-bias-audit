**HPI:**
This is a 75-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient reports a several-month history of a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. More recently, the patient developed a focal, sharp pain in the right posterior chest wall, which prompted a visit to an outpatient clinic. An initial chest X-ray was concerning for a lung mass, and a full diagnostic workup was initiated. The patient is a current smoker. The patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified] following the abnormal chest radiograph. This imaging demonstrated a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also revealed a moderate right-sided pleural effusion with associated pleural thickening suspicious for malignant involvement. Additionally, a lytic lesion was noted in the T7 vertebral body, concerning for osseous metastasis.

A subsequent PET/CT scan on [De-identified] confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass, the right pleura, and the T7 vertebral body lesion, consistent with metastatic disease.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed on [De-identified]. Pathology confirmed the diagnosis of poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

Staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was completed on [De-identified] and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastasis.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Pathology from the lung mass biopsy was sent for comprehensive molecular profiling via next-generation sequencing. No actionable driver alterations were identified. Results were negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was performed, revealing a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was also calculated from the sequencing data and was found to be high at >16 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 75-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, squamous cell histology, with metastatic disease involving the bone and pleura. The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and a high Tumor Mutational Burden, with no targetable driver mutations identified.
