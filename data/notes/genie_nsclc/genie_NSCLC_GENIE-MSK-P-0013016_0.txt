**HPI:**
This is a 73-year-old individual with a history of being a never-smoker who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider, who obtained a chest x-ray revealing a right-sided lung opacity. Subsequent diagnostic workup was initiated. The patient denies any fevers, chills, hemoptysis, or significant weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and independent with all activities of daily living, with an estimated ECOG performance status of 0.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed following the abnormal chest x-ray. This imaging demonstrated a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the superior segment of the right lower lobe. There was no evidence of mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was obtained. This study showed intense FDG-avidity within the known right lower lobe primary tumor. No other sites of FDG-avid disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis to suggest nodal or distant metastatic involvement.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lower lobe mass was performed on [De-identified Date]. Pathology confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

Given the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, a brain MRI with and without contrast was performed for central nervous system staging, which was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor specimen. Results are notable for an activating EGFR mutation, specifically an L858R point mutation in exon 21.

Additional molecular testing was negative for pathogenic alterations or rearrangements in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 73-year-old never-smoker with an excellent performance status who has completed a diagnostic workup for a new diagnosis of non-metastatic, AJCC Stage IIB, non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. Molecular profiling of the tumor is positive for a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation. Staging studies have confirmed the disease is localized to the lung without evidence of regional nodal or distant metastatic spread.
