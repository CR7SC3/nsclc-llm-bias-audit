**HPI:**
This is a 43-year-old individual with a past history of tobacco use (former smoker, quit >1 year ago) who presents for initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was followed by the onset of intermittent headaches and the self-discovery of a firm, non-tender nodule on the chest wall.

An initial evaluation in the emergency department at [De-identified Hospital] on [De-identified Date] included a chest CT scan. This revealed a primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, associated with a large malignant pleural effusion and multiple liver lesions concerning for metastatic disease. Given the finding of headaches, a brain MRI was subsequently performed, which confirmed the presence of multiple cerebral metastases. The patient's functional status is good, with an ECOG performance status of 1 (symptomatic but fully ambulatory).

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The patient underwent a biopsy of an accessible subcutaneous nodule on the anterior chest wall. Pathology from this procedure returned as adenocarcinoma, with an immunohistochemical profile (TTF-1 positive, Napsin-A positive) consistent with a lung primary.

Staging studies have been completed.
- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis with contrast:** Revealed a right upper lobe lung mass, a large right-sided pleural effusion with associated pleural thickening suggestive of carcinomatosis, multiple hypodense liver lesions consistent with metastases, and a subcutaneous metastatic deposit.
- **MRI Brain with and without contrast:** Confirmed multiple enhancing lesions in the cerebral hemispheres, consistent with metastatic disease.

These findings confirm a diagnosis of Stage IV NSCLC (AJCC 8th Edition).

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry were performed on the tumor tissue.
- **Actionable driver mutation:** An ALK gene rearrangement was detected.
- **Other drivers:** Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- **Immunotherapy markers:** PD-L1 expression was negative (Tumor Proportion Score <1%). Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was low at <2 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 43-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma with metastases to the brain, liver, pleura, and subcutaneous tissue. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of an ALK rearrangement. The patient is here for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and management options.
