**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS**
This is a 46-year-old individual referred for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a progressive, non-productive cough and increasing shortness of breath on exertion. Symptoms initially prompted a visit to an urgent care center, where a chest X-ray revealed a large left-sided pleural effusion.

The patient was subsequently referred to a pulmonologist for further evaluation. A diagnostic and therapeutic thoracentesis was performed, which drained a significant volume of fluid and provided temporary symptomatic relief. Cytology from the pleural fluid was suspicious for malignancy. A follow-up CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was obtained, which confirmed a primary mass in the left upper lobe, extensive pleural nodularity, and a re-accumulating pleural effusion. The patient has remained active with an excellent performance status (ECOG 0), continuing to work and perform all activities of daily living without restriction. They report no fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. There is no history of tobacco use.
