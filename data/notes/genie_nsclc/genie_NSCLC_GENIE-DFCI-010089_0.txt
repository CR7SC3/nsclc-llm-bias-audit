**HPI:**
A 61-year-old patient presents for initial oncology evaluation following a recent diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient first noted a persistent cough and mild shortness of breath approximately three months ago. These symptoms gradually worsened, prompting a visit to their primary care physician. Initial chest X-ray revealed a suspicious lung mass, leading to further diagnostic imaging. The patient reports an ECOG performance status of 1, able to perform all usual activities with some restriction of physically strenuous activity. No significant weight loss or other constitutional symptoms were reported.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Subsequent computed tomography (CT) of the chest confirmed a 4.5 cm mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, along with enlarged mediastinal and supraclavicular lymph nodes concerning for metastatic involvement. A CT-guided biopsy of the lung mass was performed, which confirmed the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Further staging workup included a positron emission tomography (PET)/CT scan, which demonstrated avid uptake in the primary lung lesion and confirmed extensive mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymph node involvement, consistent with locoregionally advanced disease. Importantly, the PET/CT scan showed no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain was performed and revealed no evidence of intracranial metastases. Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Molecular profiling was performed on the tumor tissue. Results indicate no actionable driver alterations, with testing negative for EGFR mutations, ALK rearrangements, ROS1 rearrangements, BRAF mutations, MET exon 14 skipping alterations, RET fusions, and NTRK fusions. PD-L1 expression analysis was not reported at this time.
