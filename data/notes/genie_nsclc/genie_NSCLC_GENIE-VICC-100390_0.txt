**HPI:**
This is a 72-year-old individual with a history of hypertension, presenting for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed persistent headaches and a dull, aching pain in the right hip. The headaches were intermittent but progressive, not fully relieved by over-the-counter analgesics. The hip pain was worse with weight-bearing.

These symptoms prompted an outpatient workup. An MRI of the brain was performed which revealed multiple enhancing intracranial lesions concerning for metastatic disease. A subsequent CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was obtained to identify a primary malignancy. This study demonstrated a spiculated mass in the left upper lobe of the lung, along with mediastinal lymphadenopathy and a lytic lesion in the right iliac bone. The patient was referred for pulmonology consultation and underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the lung mass, which established the diagnosis.

The patient is a former smoker, having quit approximately 10 years ago. Review of systems is notable for fatigue and an unintentional 10-pound weight loss over the past three months. The patient denies significant cough, shortness of breath, or hemoptysis. Performance status is ECOG 1; the patient is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity.

Based on the presence of a primary lung tumor with metastases to the brain and bone, the patient's disease is classified as AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV.

*   **Driver Mutations:** No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor is negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2.
