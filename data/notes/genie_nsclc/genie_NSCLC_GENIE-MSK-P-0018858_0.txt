**HPI:**
This is a 58-year-old individual with a history of hypertension who presents for initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they began to experience a progressively worsening, non-productive cough and increasing dyspnea on exertion. These symptoms prompted a visit to an urgent care center, where a chest X-ray was performed, revealing a right-sided lung opacity and a large pleural effusion. The patient was subsequently referred to the emergency department for further evaluation. The patient is a former smoker, having quit less than one year ago after a multi-decade history of tobacco use. Review of systems is notable for the aforementioned cough and dyspnea, as well as approximately 10 pounds of unintentional weight loss over the past two months and generalized fatigue. The patient denies fevers, chills, night sweats, hemoptysis, or focal neurological symptoms. Performance status is ECOG 1; the patient remains ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. Imaging revealed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, associated with mediastinal lymphadenopathy and a large, loculated right-sided pleural effusion with associated pleural thickening, suspicious for malignant involvement. There was no evidence of distant metastatic disease to the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis and to relieve symptoms, the patient underwent a video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) procedure with pleural biopsy and partial drainage of the pleural effusion. Pathologic analysis of the pleural biopsy specimen confirmed infiltration by a poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary. The diagnosis is therefore non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), adenocarcinoma histology. The presence of malignant cells in the pleura establishes a final diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV (M1a) disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive next-generation sequencing was performed on the pleural tumor specimen. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations, fusions, or amplifications involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.

Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate, at 2–16 mutations per megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 58-year-old, treatment-naive individual with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, metastatic to the pleura. The patient has a good performance status (ECOG 1). Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the absence of an identifiable oncogenic driver and for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%).
