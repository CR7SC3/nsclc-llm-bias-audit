**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest radiograph in the emergency department was suspicious for a right-sided lung mass, leading to further imaging. A subsequent CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed. This study identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated bulky mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy.

Given the new neurological symptoms, a contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was obtained. This revealed three distinct ring-enhancing lesions in the bilateral cerebral hemispheres, the largest measuring 1.5 cm in the left parietal lobe, consistent with metastatic disease.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic evaluation of the biopsy specimen confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

The combination of a primary lung mass with confirmed metastases to the brain establishes the diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
The lung biopsy tissue was sent for next-generation sequencing to assess for molecular alterations.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be high, at >16 mutations/megabase.
- PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen.
