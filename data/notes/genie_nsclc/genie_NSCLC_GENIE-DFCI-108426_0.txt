**HPI:**
This is a 52-year-old individual whom I am seeing today for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and was in their usual state of good health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation. At that time, they began to develop a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive right-sided pleuritic chest pain. Over the subsequent weeks, they noted increasing dyspnea on exertion and new, persistent pain in their lower back, which was not relieved by over-the-counter analgesics. These symptoms prompted a visit to an urgent care center on [De-identified Date], where a chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass and a moderate pleural effusion.

The patient was subsequently referred for further diagnostic evaluation. They have experienced an estimated 10-pound weight loss over the past two months and report significant fatigue, but otherwise deny fevers, chills, or hemoptysis. Their ECOG performance status is 1; they are fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but are restricted in physically strenuous activity. The patient presents to clinic today accompanied by family to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The patient completed a full staging workup prior to this consultation.

CT of the Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis with contrast demonstrated a 4.1 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. Associated findings included extensive malignant-appearing pleural thickening and a moderate-to-large right-sided pleural effusion. Multiple sclerotic lesions were identified in the thoracic spine and right iliac bone, suspicious for osseous metastases. No suspicious hepatic or adrenal lesions were identified.

MRI of the Brain with and without contrast was performed for neurologic staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed by Interventional Radiology on [De-identified Date]. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma. Cytology from a diagnostic thoracentesis was also positive for malignant cells consistent with adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. The following findings were reported:

-   **Negative for pathogenic alterations in:** ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, ERBB2
-   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (reported as 11 mutations/megabase)
-   **PD-L1 Expression:** Not performed, as testing was not standard at the time of initial sequencing.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 52-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, metastatic (Stage IV) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, with known metastatic disease to the pleura and bone. Molecular profiling is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion. The patient is here today to discuss the implications of this diagnosis.
