The patient is now here to discuss the findings and establish care.

An initial chest radiograph revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further imaging.

A subsequent CT of the chest confirmed the presence of a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe. There was associated adjacent pleural thickening but no definite chest wall invasion. Mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes appeared sub-centimeter and without overtly suspicious features.

A PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes on [De-identified Date]. This demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the known right upper lobe primary mass. There was no evidence of FDG-avid hilar or mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Crucially, there was no evidence of distant metastatic disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or skeleton. A dedicated MRI of the brain was obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathology from this procedure confirmed a diagnosis of invasive, moderately differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.
