**HPI:**
This is a 63-year-old individual with a history of former smoking (quit approximately 5 years ago) who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was followed by the onset of a constant, dull ache in the right posterior ribs. An initial evaluation with their primary care provider led to a chest X-ray which showed a left-sided lung mass, prompting further investigation. The patient reports a fair appetite but an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the past three months. They remain ambulatory and capable of self-care but are limited in more strenuous physical activity due to shortness of breath. ECOG performance status is 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified]. This study demonstrated a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the left lower lobe. There was associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. Additionally, the scan revealed multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules consistent with contralateral lung metastases, several hypodense lesions in the liver suspicious for metastatic deposits, and a lytic lesion in the right ninth rib corresponding to the patient's area of pain.

A subsequent PET/CT scan confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary left lower lobe mass, mediastinal lymph nodes, the multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules, the hepatic lesions, and the right rib lesion, consistent with widespread metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed for staging and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the left lower lobe lung mass was performed by Interventional Radiology on [De-identified]. The pathology returned adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Pathology confirmed non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with adenocarcinoma histology. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) of the tumor tissue was performed.
-   Actionable driver mutation detected: KRAS G12C
-   Negative for mutations/rearrangements in: EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, ERBB2.
-   Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was negative (Tumor Proportion Score < 1%).
-   Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was low at <2 mutations/Megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 63-year-old former smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup confirmed widespread disease involving the contralateral lung, liver, and bone, with no evidence of central nervous system involvement. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a KRAS G12C mutation and negative PD-L1 expression. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis in detail.
