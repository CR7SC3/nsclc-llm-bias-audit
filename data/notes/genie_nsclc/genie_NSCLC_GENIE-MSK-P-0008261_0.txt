**HPI:**
This is a 58-year-old for initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside facility approximately three weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and several weeks of intermittent, dull headaches. The patient is a former smoker, having quit within the last year after a significant pack-year history. Due to the nature of the headaches, an initial evaluation in the emergency department was pursued. The patient is otherwise active and independent with all activities of daily living, and reports a good appetite but an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the last two months. The patient's performance status is excellent, estimated at ECOG 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The diagnostic evaluation began with imaging of the head. An MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed which demonstrated multiple, well-circumscribed enhancing lesions in the right cerebellum and left frontal lobe, consistent with metastatic disease.

Subsequent staging scans, including a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast, were obtained. These studies revealed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the upper lobe of the right lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also confirmed the presence of a 2.5 cm enhancing nodule in the left adrenal gland, highly suspicious for a metastatic deposit. No other sites of distant disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic review of the biopsy specimen confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the lung biopsy. An actionable KRAS G12C mutation was detected. Further molecular analysis was negative for pathogenic mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. The tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 58-year-old with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnostic workup confirms metastatic disease involving the brain and the left adrenal gland. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a KRAS G12C mutation. The patient is a former smoker with an excellent performance status. The formal diagnosis is Stage IV NSCLC (AJCC 8th Edition).
