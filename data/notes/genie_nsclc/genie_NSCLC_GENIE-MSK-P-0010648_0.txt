**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 73-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who is referred for initial consultation and management of a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they began to develop a persistent, dull ache in their mid-back. This was initially managed conservatively without relief. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also developed a new, non-productive cough and a sense of fatigue. Due to the persistence of these symptoms, they presented to their primary care provider for evaluation. The patient maintains a good performance status (ECOG 1), remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though is limited in more strenuous activity by the back discomfort.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
An initial chest X-ray was obtained which was suspicious for a right-sided lung mass, prompting further investigation.

A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This study characterized a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The CT also revealed multiple sclerotic lesions in the thoracic spine and ribs, suspicious for metastatic disease.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was performed. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and mediastinal nodes. Furthermore, it confirmed multiple foci of hypermetabolism in the skeleton consistent with osseous metastases, confirming Stage IV disease. A contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Tumor tissue from the lung biopsy was sent for comprehensive next-generation sequencing. The results are as follows:
- **Driver Mutations:** No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
