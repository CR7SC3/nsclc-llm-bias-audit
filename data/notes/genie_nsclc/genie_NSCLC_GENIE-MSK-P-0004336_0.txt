**HPI:**
This is a 53-year-old individual with a history of being a never-smoker who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks ago, when they began to experience persistent headaches, mild dizziness, and a new, non-productive cough. The headaches were initially intermittent but became more frequent and were not fully relieved by over-the-counter analgesics. Due to the progressive nature of these symptoms, the patient sought medical evaluation.

An initial neurologic workup was pursued given the presenting symptoms. An MRI of the brain was performed, which unfortunately revealed several enhancing lesions suspicious for metastatic disease. This prompted a full staging workup to identify a primary malignancy. The patient reports a good performance status (ECOG 0-1), remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though they note increased fatigue over the past month. They deny any significant weight loss, hemoptysis, or focal weakness.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the brain MRI, the patient underwent comprehensive staging with a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast. This imaging identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe of the lung, with associated ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also confirmed the presence of a 1.8 cm lesion in the right adrenal gland, consistent with a distant metastasis.

A PET-CT scan was subsequently performed, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary left upper lobe lung mass, the mediastinal lymph nodes, the right adrenal lesion, and the multiple cerebral lesions identified on the prior MRI.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the left upper lobe lung mass. The procedure was well-tolerated. Pathologic analysis of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis.

**Molecular Studies:**
Pathology from the lung mass biopsy confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, non-small cell type. Comprehensive next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. This analysis identified an actionable driver mutation in the form of a **ROS1 rearrangement**.

The tumor tested negative for mutations or rearrangements in **EGFR, ALK, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2**. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be low (<2 mut/Mb). PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed, consistent with the standard of care at the time of the tissue acquisition.

**Problem Summary:**
1.  Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer, adenocarcinoma histology.
2.  AJCC Stage IV disease at diagnosis, with sites of metastasis including multiple brain lesions and a right adrenal gland lesion.
3.  Molecular profiling is notable for the presence of a ROS1 rearrangement.
4.  Patient is a never-smoker and is treatment-naive.
