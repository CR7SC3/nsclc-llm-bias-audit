**HPI:**
This is a 67-year-old patient with a good functional status who presents for an initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive shortness of breath with exertion. These symptoms were accompanied by vague right-sided chest and upper abdominal discomfort, as well as an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds. Due to the persistence of these symptoms, the patient sought evaluation with their primary care physician. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. They remain active and are able to perform all activities of daily living with minimal assistance, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray was abnormal, prompting a staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast. This imaging revealed a 4.5 cm primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung. The scan also demonstrated extensive metastatic disease, including multiple nodules in the contralateral (left) lung, a large malignant right-sided pleural effusion, and numerous hypodense lesions throughout the liver consistent with metastases. An additional 2.8 cm nodule was noted in the left adrenal gland, also suspicious for metastatic involvement.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent an interventional radiology-guided core needle biopsy of an accessible liver lesion. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed metastatic adenocarcinoma. Immunohistochemical stains were positive for TTF-1 and Napsin-A, consistent with a lung primary origin.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing and ancillary molecular testing were performed on the tissue obtained from the liver biopsy. The key findings are as follows:
- **Actionable Driver Mutation:** A ROS1 gene rearrangement was detected.
- **PD-L1 Expression:** Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) was high at ≥50%.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- **Other Markers:** Testing was negative for alterations in EGFR, ALK, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 67-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (M1) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The patient has a good performance status (ECOG 1). Staging workup confirms metastatic disease involving the contralateral lung, pleura, liver, and adrenal gland, with no evidence of brain metastases. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a ROS1 rearrangement and high PD-L1 expression. The patient is here for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and findings in detail.
