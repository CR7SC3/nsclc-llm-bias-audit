**HPI:**
This is a 65-year-old individual with a recent history of smoking (quit <1 year ago) who presents for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild right-sided chest discomfort. This prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, leading to further investigation. The patient reports good functional status, remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1. They deny significant weight loss, fevers, or hemoptysis.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date], which confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy, with the largest node measuring 2.8 cm in the right paratracheal station. No suspicious lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was performed on [De-identified Date]. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the known right upper lobe primary mass and in the right mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, consistent with the CT findings. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was also obtained as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient subsequently underwent an endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) with transbronchial needle aspiration of a right paratracheal lymph node (station 4R). Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

Based on this comprehensive workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IIIA (cT2bN2M0) non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the EBUS biopsy.
- No actionable driver alterations were identified, including in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, or ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was high, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be high at >16 mutations/megabase.
- A loss-of-function mutation in the STK11 gene was also identified.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 65-year-old with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, Stage IIIA non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%), high TMB, and an STK11 loss-of-function mutation, with no other targetable driver alterations. The patient is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis.
