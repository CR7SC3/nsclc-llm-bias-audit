**HPI:**
This is a 76-year-old individual with a significant smoking history who is referred for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to their primary care physician approximately six weeks ago with complaints of progressive shortness of breath on exertion and a persistent, dry cough. These symptoms had worsened over the preceding two months, limiting the ability to perform usual activities. There was no associated fever, chills, or hemoptysis. The patient reports being able to manage activities of daily living but is fatigued and spends more time resting. ECOG performance status is estimated at 1. The patient is a current smoker.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass and a large pleural effusion. This prompted a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis for staging. The CT scan confirmed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, along with extensive right-sided pleural nodularity and a large, complex pleural effusion. Several mediastinal lymph nodes were noted to be enlarged. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones. A subsequent PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, multiple mediastinal lymph nodes, and diffuse pleural thickening, consistent with metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A right-sided thoracentesis was performed by Interventional Radiology for both diagnostic and therapeutic purposes. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, and cell block pathology confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor specimen obtained from the pleural fluid. Molecular profiling did not identify an actionable driver alteration. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was performed, revealing a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of <1% (negative). Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 76-year-old current smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastatic disease confined to the pleura. The patient is treatment-naive. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of an actionable driver mutation and a PD-L1 TPS of <1%. The patient is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and establish oncologic care.
