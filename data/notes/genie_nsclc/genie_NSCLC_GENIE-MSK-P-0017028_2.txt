**HPI:** This 69-year-old with a history of pancreatic cancer presents for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The individual is a former smoker, having quit within the last year. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. These symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, leading to further cross-sectional imaging. The patient is otherwise active with a good performance status, reporting no significant weight loss, fevers, or hemoptysis.

**Diagnostic Workup:** A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study characterized a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, with the largest node measuring 2.8 cm in the paratracheal station. There was no evidence of contralateral or distant metastatic disease; the liver, adrenal glands, and visualized osseous structures were unremarkable.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was obtained on [De-identified Date]. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the right upper lobe primary mass as well as in the right hilar and right paratracheal lymph nodes, consistent with locoregional nodal metastases. No other sites of abnormal FDG uptake suggestive of distant metastatic disease were identified. A brain MRI performed on [De-identified Date] was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for definitive diagnosis and staging. Transbronchial needle aspiration (TBNA) was performed on the primary mass and a paratracheal lymph node. Pathology from both sites was consistent with invasive squamous cell carcinoma.

Based on this workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:** Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the lymph node biopsy. No actionable driver alterations were identified; the tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:** In summary, this is a 69-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, non-metastatic Stage IIIA squamous non-small cell lung cancer. Molecular profiling of the tumor did not reveal any actionable driver mutations. The patient has a notable past medical history of pancreatic cancer and presents with an excellent performance status for consideration of therapy.
