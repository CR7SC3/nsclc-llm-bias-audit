**HPI:**
This is an 80-year-old with a past medical history of prostate cancer who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation to discuss a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This prompted an evaluation by their primary care provider, which initiated a diagnostic workup. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. They report good energy levels and maintain full independence with activities of daily living, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1. They deny fevers, chills, night sweats, or unintentional weight loss.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, which led to a follow-up CT of the chest. The CT scan confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. For staging, a PET/CT was subsequently performed, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the known right upper lobe mass. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid mediastinal, hilar, or supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, and no findings concerning for distant metastatic disease. To complete the staging workup, a brain MRI with and without contrast was obtained, which was negative for intracranial metastases. The patient then underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic analysis of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. Results showed no actionable driver alteration identified. Specifically, the tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was negative (Tumor Proportion Score <1%). The tumor mutational burden (TMB) was low, at <2 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is an 80-year-old never-smoker with a good performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IIB (T2b N0 M0) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for being negative for any currently identified actionable driver mutations. PD-L1 expression is negative and tumor mutational burden is low. The patient is treatment-naive for this malignancy and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
