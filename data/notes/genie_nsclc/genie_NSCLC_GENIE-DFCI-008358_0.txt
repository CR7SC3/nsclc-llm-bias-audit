**History of Present Illness:**
The patient is a 54-year-old with a significant smoking history who presents for evaluation of newly diagnosed lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive exertional dyspnea. This was initially managed as a suspected respiratory infection without improvement. Due to worsening symptoms, including intermittent chest discomfort, a chest radiograph was obtained which revealed a right upper lobe opacity.

The patient is an active, current smoker. They report good functional status prior to the onset of these symptoms and currently maintain an ECOG performance status of 1, limited only by respiratory symptoms. The systemic review is otherwise negative for fevers, chills, significant weight loss, or focal neurologic deficits.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated prominent ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy, with the largest node measuring 2.8 cm. There was no evidence of distant visceral or bone metastases identified.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in multiple right-sided mediastinal lymph nodes (stations 4R and 7), consistent with nodal metastases. No FDG-avid lesions suggestive of distant metastatic disease were identified in the contralateral chest, abdomen, or pelvis. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)-guided fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

Based on the combination of a T2 primary tumor and N2 nodal involvement with no evidence of distant metastases (T2bN2M0), the patient is staged with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer.
