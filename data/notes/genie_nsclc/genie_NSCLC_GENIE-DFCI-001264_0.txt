**HPI:**
This is a 64-year-old individual with no significant past medical history who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they developed persistent, dull headaches and a sense of imbalance. Over the subsequent two weeks, they noted progressive weakness in their left arm and leg, which prompted an evaluation in an outside emergency department.

An initial head CT and subsequent brain MRI revealed multiple ring-enhancing lesions in the right cerebral and left cerebellar hemispheres, concerning for metastatic disease. A staging workup was initiated, including a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This imaging identified a 3.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, associated with bulky mediastinal lymphadenopathy. A lytic lesion was also noted in the T8 vertebral body. The patient was started on a course of dexamethasone with significant improvement in their neurologic symptoms. They are a lifetime never-smoker. At the time of this consultation, the patient is fully ambulatory and performing all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive diagnostic evaluation was completed prior to this visit.

Imaging studies have been reviewed:
- **MRI Brain with and without contrast:** Confirmed the presence of at least three discrete metastatic lesions, the largest in the right parietal lobe measuring 1.8 cm with surrounding vasogenic edema.
- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis with contrast:** Identified a 3.2 cm primary mass in the right upper lobe, extensive mediastinal adenopathy (short axis up to 2.5 cm), and a lytic lesion in the T8 vertebral body. No other sites of visceral metastatic disease were identified in the abdomen or pelvis.
- **PET/CT:** Performed for staging, this demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe mass, multiple mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, the known cerebral metastases, and multiple sites of osseous involvement including the T8 vertebra and a right rib.

Pathology:
- A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed on [De-identified Date].
- Histopathologic examination confirmed **invasive adenocarcinoma**, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the lung biopsy.
- An actionable driver mutation was detected: **EGFR exon 19 deletion (sensitizing)**.
- The panel was **negative** for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as **intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb)**.
- PD-L1 expression was **not tested**.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 64-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of **Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), adenocarcinoma histology**, with metastases to the **brain and bone** at the time of initial presentation. Molecular testing is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and establish a plan for management.
