**HPI:**
This is an 81-year-old individual with a recent history of a persistent, non-productive cough and new-onset mid-back pain, who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation. The patient was evaluated by primary care, and a chest X-ray revealed a suspicious right-sided lung mass. Subsequent staging with a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis confirmed a large primary mass in the right lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also identified multiple nodules in the contralateral (left) lung and a suspicious lesion in a thoracic vertebra.

The patient is a former smoker, having quit within the past year. At present, the patient reports ongoing fatigue and manageable back pain. Performance status is good, estimated at ECOG 1, as the patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of self-care, though symptomatic. There has been a small, unintentional weight loss over the last two months.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A PET/CT scan performed on [De-identified] for comprehensive staging demonstrated a hypermetabolic primary mass in the right lung, hypermetabolic mediastinal lymph nodes, multiple avid nodules in the contralateral lung, and an avid osseous lesion in the thoracic spine, confirming metastatic disease.

A subsequent MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and showed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right lung mass was performed on [De-identified]. Pathology confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, specifically adenocarcinoma histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) of the tumor tissue was performed. No actionable oncogenic driver mutations were identified; testing was negative for alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was negative (Tumor Proportion Score <1%). The Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

The molecular profile is notable for a loss-of-function mutation in STK11, which is associated with primary resistance to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. A concurrent loss-of-function mutation in KEAP1 was also identified, which is known to be associated with a reduced response to immunotherapy.

**Problem Summary:**
This is an 81-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT_N_M1b) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, metastatic to the contralateral lung and bone. The patient is treatment-naive. Molecular profiling is negative for targetable driver mutations and PD-L1 expression, but is positive for STK11 and KEAP1 co-mutations. The patient presents for evaluation and to discuss the diagnosis.
