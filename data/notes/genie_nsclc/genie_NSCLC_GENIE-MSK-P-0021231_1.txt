**HPI:**
This is a 54-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit within the last year) who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to evaluation, when they noted the development of a persistent dry cough and a new, firm, palpable nodule on their anterior chest wall. These symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray revealed a suspicious lung mass, leading to further workup with a staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This imaging confirmed the presence of a primary mass in the lung, with additional findings concerning for metastatic disease, including multiple nodules in the contralateral lung and several hypoattenuating lesions within the liver. The palpable subcutaneous nodule was also confirmed on imaging. The patient reports some mild fatigue but has had no significant weight loss, hemoptysis, or bone pain. They remain fully ambulatory and maintain an excellent performance status (ECOG 1).

To establish a definitive diagnosis, the patient underwent an image-guided core needle biopsy of the accessible subcutaneous chest wall lesion. Pathology from this specimen confirmed the diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma. A subsequent brain MRI was performed for staging and was negative for intracranial metastases. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and the results of the completed workup.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
- **Computed Tomography (CT) Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis with contrast, [De-identified Date]:** Revealed a primary lung mass. Findings consistent with metastatic disease were identified, including multiple contralateral pulmonary nodules, multiple hepatic lesions, and a distinct subcutaneous soft tissue nodule.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) Brain with and without contrast, [De-identified Date]:** Performed for central nervous system staging. No evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.
- **Biopsy of subcutaneous chest wall nodule, [De-identified Date]:** Histopathology confirmed poorly differentiated non-small cell carcinoma with features most consistent with squamous cell carcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry (IHC) were performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy.
- **Driver Mutations:** No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- **PD-L1 Expression:** PD-L1 IHC was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 1-49%.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was determined to be intermediate (2-16 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 54-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer of squamous histology. The diagnostic workup confirms metastatic disease involving the liver, contralateral lung, and subcutaneous tissue, with no evidence of central nervous system involvement. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a PD-L1 TPS of 1-49% and the absence of any identified targetable driver alterations.
