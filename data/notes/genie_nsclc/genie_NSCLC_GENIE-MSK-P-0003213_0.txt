**HPI:**
This is a 67-year-old individual who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to an outside emergency department approximately three weeks ago with several days of progressive headache, mild word-finding difficulty, and a new sense of imbalance. Due to the neurologic nature of these symptoms, a brain MRI was obtained. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and has no significant personal history of cancer. There is a reported 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past two months, associated with decreased appetite and generalized fatigue. The patient otherwise denies cough, hemoptysis, or significant shortness of breath, but does endorse a dull, persistent ache in the right hip.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The initial brain MRI with contrast revealed multiple ring-enhancing lesions in the bilateral cerebral and cerebellar hemispheres, highly suspicious for metastatic disease.

A subsequent staging evaluation was undertaken with a PET/CT scan from skull base to mid-thigh. This imaging identified a hypermetabolic 4.5 cm primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung. Widespread metastatic disease was evident, including multiple intensely FDG-avid hepatic lesions, numerous osseous metastases involving the axial and appendicular skeleton (including the right iliac bone, corresponding to the patient's pain), and extensive hypermetabolic lymphadenopathy in the mediastinum, supraclavicular, and retroperitoneal regions. A left adrenal metastasis was also noted.

To obtain a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of an accessible liver lesion. Pathologic examination of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of metastatic adenocarcinoma. Immunohistochemical stains were positive for TTF-1 and Napsin-A, consistent with a lung primary origin.

Based on the findings of a primary lung mass with distant metastases to the brain, liver, bone, and distant lymph nodes, the patient's cancer is formally staged as AJCC Stage IV.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the liver biopsy tissue specimen. Results are as follows:
- An actionable driver mutation was detected: EGFR L858R point mutation (sensitizing).
- The tumor was negative for rearrangements or mutations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 67-year-old lifetime never-smoker who presents for initial oncologic evaluation with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnostic workup has confirmed widespread metastatic disease involving the brain, liver, bone, and distant lymph nodes. The patient is symptomatic from this disease burden but maintains a good functional status, ECOG 1. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation.
