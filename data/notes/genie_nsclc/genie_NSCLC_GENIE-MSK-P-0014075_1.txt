**HPI:**
This is a 79-year-old with a past medical history notable for hypertension and hyperlipidemia, who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over a decade ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive lower back pain. The back pain was initially managed with over-the-counter analgesics with minimal relief. Due to the persistence of symptoms, they presented to their primary care provider who ordered initial imaging. A chest radiograph revealed a suspicious opacity in the right upper lung. This prompted a referral for further diagnostic evaluation. The patient reports mild fatigue but has maintained their weight and appetite. They remain fully ambulatory and independent in all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A staging PET/CT scan was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study demonstrated a hypermetabolic, spiculated 4.2 cm mass in the right upper lobe. There was also evidence of ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. Critically, the scan revealed multiple FDG-avid osseous lesions, most prominently a lytic lesion in the L3 vertebral body, consistent with metastatic disease. There was no evidence of metastatic disease to the liver or adrenal glands. A subsequent MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. An actionable driver mutation was identified in the form of a MET exon 14 skipping mutation. Additional molecular testing was negative for common driver mutations or rearrangements, including EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was performed, revealing a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 0% (negative). Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 79-year-old former smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastases to bone. The patient is treatment-naive with an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a MET exon 14 skipping mutation and negative PD-L1 expression.
