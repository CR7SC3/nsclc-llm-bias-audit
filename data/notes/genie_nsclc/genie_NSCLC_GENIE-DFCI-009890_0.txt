**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 62-year-old individual with a significant history of tobacco use (quit <1 year ago) who is referred for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months ago when they developed a persistent, dry cough. This was soon followed by progressive dyspnea on exertion and a vague, aching sensation in the right chest. After an initial trial of antibiotics for presumed bronchitis failed to provide relief, further imaging was pursued. The patient denies hemoptysis, fevers, or significant unintentional weight loss. Performance status is excellent, and the patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
An initial chest radiograph demonstrated a right upper lobe density, prompting a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest. This study confirmed the presence of a 4 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also revealed extensive ipsilateral mediastinal and right hilar lymphadenopathy.

For staging, a whole-body PET/CT was performed. This demonstrated high FDG avidity in the primary right lung mass as well as in the right paratracheal and subcarinal lymph nodes, consistent with locoregional nodal involvement. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones to suggest distant metastatic spread.

To complete staging and obtain a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and transbronchial needle aspiration of a right paratracheal lymph node. Pathology from this procedure confirmed adenocarcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung primary.

A staging MRI of the brain with contrast was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tissue obtained from the lymph node biopsy. No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

PD-L1 expression was not tested, as this was not standard of care at the time of the initial diagnostic workup. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
