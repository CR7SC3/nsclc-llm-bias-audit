**HPI:**
This is a 60-year-old individual with a history of former tobacco use who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months ago, when they began to experience a persistent dry cough and new, progressive mid-thoracic back pain. The back pain was initially managed with over-the-counter analgesics with minimal relief. Due to the persistent cough, a chest X-ray was performed by their primary care provider, which demonstrated a right-sided lung mass.

The patient was subsequently referred for staging imaging. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe and also identified multiple nodules in the contralateral (left) lung, enlarged mediastinal and supraclavicular lymph nodes, and a suspicious lytic lesion in a thoracic vertebral body. A subsequent PET-CT scan demonstrated high FDG-avidity in the primary right lung mass, the contralateral lung nodules, multiple nodal stations, and the thoracic spine lesion, confirming widespread metastatic disease. The patient then underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary lung mass, which established the diagnosis.

The patient reports ongoing moderate fatigue and persistent back pain, but remains fully ambulatory and capable of all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1. They have a 30 pack-year smoking history but quit over one year ago.
