**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass and a moderate pleural effusion. This prompted a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, which confirmed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated mediastinal adenopathy. The scan also characterized a moderate, complex right-sided pleural effusion. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A subsequent PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass, as well as in mediastinal lymph nodes and nodular thickening of the right pleura, consistent with malignant involvement. No other sites of distant FDG-avid disease were identified.

Given the advanced stage, a brain MRI with contrast was performed to evaluate for intracranial metastases, which was negative.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed. Pathology confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma. A diagnostic and therapeutic thoracentesis was also performed, and cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, confirming a malignant pleural effusion.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IV (M1a) non-small cell lung cancer.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**

- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was strongly positive with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
