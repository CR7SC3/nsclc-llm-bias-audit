**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 68-year-old individual with a recent diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, referred for initial oncologic evaluation. The patient has a past history of smoking but quit over one year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they began to experience a persistent, dull ache in the right upper back and flank, along with a non-productive cough. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also noted increasing fatigue, a decreased appetite, and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds. Due to the persistence of these symptoms, they presented for medical evaluation.

Initial workup included a chest CT scan, which revealed a large primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. The scan also demonstrated multiple hypoattenuating lesions throughout the liver, suspicious for metastatic disease. Given the patient's report of back pain, a subsequent bone scan was performed, which showed multiple areas of abnormal radiotracer uptake, including in the thoracic spine and a right-sided rib, consistent with osseous metastases. Staging was completed with an MRI of the brain, which was negative for intracranial metastases. The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core biopsy of a liver lesion to establish a tissue diagnosis.

The patient presents today for discussion of the diagnosis. They report ongoing back pain, managed with over-the-counter analgesics, and persistent fatigue. Performance status is ECOG 1; the patient is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity.
