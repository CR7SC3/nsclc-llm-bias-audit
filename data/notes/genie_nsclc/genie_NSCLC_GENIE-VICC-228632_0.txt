**HPI:**
This is a 76-year-old individual with a history of smoking who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they began experiencing persistent headaches and subtle word-finding difficulties. These symptoms prompted an evaluation in an emergency department, where a brain MRI was performed and revealed multiple intracranial lesions concerning for metastatic disease.

Subsequent staging workup was initiated. A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis demonstrated a primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, associated with a moderate-sized right-sided pleural effusion and a lytic lesion in the T7 vertebral body. The patient underwent a video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) for a pleural biopsy and talc pleurodesis. Pathology from this procedure confirmed the diagnosis.

The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. They report increased fatigue and a 10-pound weight loss over the past two months. Their performance status is fair; they are ambulatory and capable of all self-care but are restricted in physically strenuous activity. They deny significant shortness of breath, cough, or hemoptysis at this time. The patient is here today, accompanied by family, to discuss the diagnosis in detail.
