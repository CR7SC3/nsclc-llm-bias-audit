**HPI:**
This is a 74-year-old being seen for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a past medical history significant for breast cancer, treated in the past, and is a former smoker, having quit over a year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they began to experience progressive shortness of breath on exertion and a persistent, non-productive cough. These symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider, who ordered a chest X-ray. The radiograph revealed a large left-sided pleural effusion and a suspicious opacity in the left upper lobe.

The patient was subsequently referred to a pulmonologist and underwent a CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This confirmed the presence of a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe, extensive left-sided pleural thickening, and a large, loculated pleural effusion. No other distant metastatic disease was identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones. A dedicated MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a thoracentesis for both therapeutic and diagnostic purposes. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, consistent with adenocarcinoma. The patient reports some improvement in dyspnea following the fluid removal but remains easily fatigued. They are otherwise functional, with an ECOG performance status of 1, and are here today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options. The patient is treatment-naive for this lung cancer.
