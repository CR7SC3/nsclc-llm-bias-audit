**HPI:**
This is a 69-year-old individual with a history of former tobacco use who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months ago, when they began to experience a persistent dry cough and progressive shortness of breath with exertion. These symptoms were initially attributed to a seasonal illness but failed to resolve. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also noted mild right-sided chest discomfort and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds. This prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider, which initiated a diagnostic workup. The patient remains functionally well, carrying out all activities of daily living but is limited in strenuous physical activity due to dyspnea. Their ECOG performance status is 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray demonstrated a right upper lobe opacity, leading to a staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast performed on [De-identified date]. This study confirmed a 4.8 cm primary mass in the right upper lobe with associated mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. The scan also revealed multiple sites of distant disease, including several bilateral pulmonary nodules consistent with contralateral lung metastases, three distinct hypodense lesions in the liver suspicious for metastatic deposits, and a 2.5 cm enhancing nodule in the left adrenal gland.

To establish a definitive diagnosis and obtain tissue for molecular analysis, the patient underwent an interventional radiology-guided core needle biopsy of one of the accessible liver lesions on [De-identified date]. The pathology report confirmed metastatic adenocarcinoma. Immunohistochemical stains were positive for TTF-1 and Napsin-A, supporting a lung primary origin.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the liver biopsy was sent for molecular analysis. Comprehensive next-generation sequencing panel results are currently pending. An initial report indicates the Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) is low at <2 mut/Mb. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not tested on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 69-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging studies confirm widespread metastatic disease involving the contralateral lung, liver, distant lymph nodes, and the left adrenal gland, consistent with clinical stage cT2b N3 M1c. The patient has a good performance status (ECOG 1). Key molecular profiling is in process. The patient presents today to discuss the implications of this diagnosis and to establish care.
