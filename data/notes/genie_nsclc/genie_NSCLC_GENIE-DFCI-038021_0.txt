**HPI:**
This is a 54-year-old individual with a history of smoking, referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to their primary care provider approximately six weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. There was also a reported unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the preceding three months. The patient is otherwise active and fully ambulatory, with an ECOG performance status of 1. Past social history is notable for being a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting a follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest. This confirmed the presence of a primary lung mass, and the patient was subsequently referred to Pulmonology for a definitive diagnostic procedure.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed for initial staging. This identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe. There were also several prominent ipsilateral hilar lymph nodes, the largest measuring 1.5 cm. No suspicious lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or other visceral organs.

The patient underwent a bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and transbronchial needle aspiration of the right upper lobe mass and a station 11R (hilar) lymph node. Pathology from both sites returned poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung malignancy.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was performed. This demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the known right upper lobe mass and in the right hilar lymph nodes, consistent with the findings on CT. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere to suggest distant metastases. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was also obtained and was negative for any intracranial metastatic disease.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IIB non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained during biopsy.
- An actionable driver mutation was detected in **KRAS G12C**.
- The tumor was negative for alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of **1-49%**.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be **high (>16 mut/Mb)**.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 54-year-old with an excellent performance status and a new diagnosis of Stage IIB non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. The diagnostic workup has confirmed localized disease without evidence of distant metastases. Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a KRAS G12C mutation, positive PD-L1 expression, and high TMB. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and review potential treatment pathways.
