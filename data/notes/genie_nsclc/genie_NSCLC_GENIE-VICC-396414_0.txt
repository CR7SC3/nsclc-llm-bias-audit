**HPI:**
This is a 52-year-old individual who presents for initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient reports a several-month history of a persistent, dry cough and progressive shortness of breath, which prompted a visit to an urgent care facility. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass, leading to further evaluation.

The patient is a current smoker. The patient reports increased fatigue over the past few months but remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though strenuous activity is limited by dyspnea. Performance status is estimated at ECOG 1. The patient denies fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. There is no history of hemoptysis, headaches, or focal neurologic deficits.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed following the abnormal chest X-ray. This imaging demonstrated a large right upper lobe mass measuring approximately 6 cm, associated with extensive ipsilateral mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. There was no evidence of distant visceral or osseous metastatic disease.

A subsequent PET/CT scan confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe tumor and in the extensive mediastinal and supraclavicular nodal stations. No FDG-avid lesions suggestive of distant metastatic disease were identified. A dedicated MRI of the brain was performed for staging and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass was performed on [De-identified Date]. Pathology confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tumor tissue from the biopsy was sent for next-generation sequencing and immunohistochemistry.
- **PD-L1:** Expression by immunohistochemistry was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 30% (in the 1-49% range).
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Calculated as high at 18 mutations/megabase.
- **Driver Mutations:** Molecular profiling did not identify an actionable driver alteration. Results were negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 52-year-old with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer. The final diagnosis is Stage IIIB (AJCC 8th Edition) lung adenocarcinoma, based on the extent of nodal involvement without distant metastatic disease. Molecular studies are notable for the absence of an actionable driver mutation, a PD-L1 TPS of 30%, and high Tumor Mutational Burden. The patient has a good performance status (ECOG 1) and is here for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
