**REASON FOR VISIT:** Initial consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 58-year-old former smoker (quit >1 year ago) who presents for initial oncologic evaluation. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they began experiencing intermittent headaches and a sense of imbalance. These symptoms progressively worsened, prompting a visit to an emergency department at an outside facility.

An initial non-contrast head CT was performed, which was suspicious for intracranial pathology, leading to a follow-up MRI of the brain with contrast. This study revealed multiple enhancing lesions consistent with metastatic disease. A subsequent staging workup was initiated, including a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast. This identified a primary spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. A whole-body PET/CT scan confirmed FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe mass, mediastinal lymph nodes, and the previously identified intracranial lesions, with no other distant sites of FDG-avid disease.

The patient was referred to Interventional Radiology and underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass. Pathology from this procedure confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. The patient has not received any cancer-directed therapy to date. They report mild fatigue but are fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1. They present today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
