**HPI:**
This is a 64-year-old individual with a significant history of current tobacco use who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was followed by the gradual onset of mild dyspnea on exertion and intermittent right-sided chest wall discomfort. These symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider, which led to an initial diagnostic workup. The patient reports a good appetite and denies significant weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further imaging. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which characterized a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. This was associated with extensive ipsilateral mediastinal and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT scan was obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the primary right lung mass and the extensive regional nodal disease, consistent with locally advanced malignancy. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease at any distant sites to suggest metastasis. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass on [De-identified date]. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing and immunohistochemistry were performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy. Results are notable for the following:
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor is negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) is in the 1–49% range.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) is intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 64-year-old current smoker, treatment-naive, presenting with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer of squamous histology. The diagnostic workup has confirmed locally advanced, non-metastatic disease. Molecular profiling is negative for common actionable driver alterations, with a PD-L1 TPS of 1-49%. The patient is here today to discuss the implications of this diagnosis.
