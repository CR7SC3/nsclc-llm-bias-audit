**HPI:**
This is a 62-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, here for initial oncology consultation. The patient presented to an outside hospital approximately four weeks ago with several weeks of progressive dyspnea on exertion, a persistent non-productive cough, and unintentional weight loss. The patient is a current smoker. An initial chest X-ray showed a large right-sided pleural effusion, prompting further investigation. The patient is now minimally symptomatic after a diagnostic and therapeutic thoracentesis and is maintaining an excellent performance status (ECOG 0-1), able to perform all activities of daily living without assistance.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which identified a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right lower lobe. The scan also confirmed a large, loculated right pleural effusion with associated pleural thickening and nodularity, suspicious for metastatic involvement. There was no evidence of metastatic disease in the abdomen or pelvis.

A subsequent PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right lower lobe mass and extensive, diffuse FDG-avid pleural studding, consistent with pleural metastases. No other distant sites of FDG-avid disease were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bone.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed to complete staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

An ultrasound-guided thoracentesis was performed. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, and immunohistochemistry was consistent with a primary lung adenocarcinoma. This confirms a diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
The pleural fluid specimen was sent for comprehensive next-generation sequencing. Molecular profiling did not identify an actionable driver alteration. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as high (>16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 62-year-old with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, metastatic to the pleura. Molecular studies are notable for a high tumor mutational burden and no identified actionable driver mutations. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
