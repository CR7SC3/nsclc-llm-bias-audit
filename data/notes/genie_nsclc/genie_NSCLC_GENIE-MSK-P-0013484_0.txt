**HPI:**
This is a 66-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and new-onset, localized pain in the left posterior chest wall. These symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe lung opacity. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago. They maintain an excellent performance status (ECOG 0), remaining fully active and without constitutional symptoms such as weight loss or fevers.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This study characterized a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, associated with several enlarged mediastinal lymph nodes. The scan also identified a suspicious lytic lesion in the left posterior seventh rib, corresponding to the patient's area of pain. No metastatic lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or other visceral organs.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging, which confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass and the mediastinal lymph nodes. The previously noted lytic lesion in the left seventh rib was also highly FDG-avid, confirming metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed by Interventional Radiology. Pathology confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue.
- An activating EGFR exon 19 deletion (sensitizing) was detected.
- Testing was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 demonstrated a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was calculated as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 66-year-old with an excellent performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT2a N2 M1b) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology, metastatic to bone. Molecular profiling is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and the implications of these findings.
