**HPI:**
This is a 63-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside hospital approximately three weeks ago with progressive dyspnea on exertion and a persistent, non-productive cough. An initial chest X-ray revealed a large right-sided pleural effusion and a subtle right upper lobe opacity.

The patient was admitted for further evaluation. A CT scan of the chest confirmed a 3.5 cm speculated mass in the right upper lobe, extensive right-sided pleural thickening, and a large, loculated pleural effusion. The patient subsequently underwent a thoracentesis for both therapeutic relief and diagnostic purposes. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignancy.

The patient reports a significant improvement in breathing following the fluid removal, though some mild exertional dyspnea remains. Appetite and weight have been stable. The patient is fully ambulatory and managing activities of daily living without assistance. The patient is a former smoker, having quit approximately 5 years ago after a 30-pack-year history. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care. ECOG performance status is 1.

A PET/CT scan performed on [De-identified date] for staging demonstrated a hypermetabolic primary mass in the right upper lobe as described above. Pathology from the thoracentesis fluid obtained on [De-identified date] was reviewed. Cytologic examination and cell block preparation confirmed the presence of malignant cells consistent with adenocarcinoma, non-small cell type.
