**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS**
This is a 67-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. They also noted the appearance of a new, firm, non-tender nodule under the skin of their left anterior chest wall. They initially presented to their primary care provider, who ordered a chest X-ray that revealed a right-sided lung mass and a moderate pleural effusion.

The patient was subsequently referred for further evaluation. A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed which confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, extensive malignant-appearing pleural thickening and a large right-sided pleural effusion, and a 2 cm subcutaneous nodule in the left anterior chest wall consistent with the palpable finding. There was no evidence of metastatic disease in the abdomen or pelvis. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastasis.

The patient has experienced some fatigue and a 10-pound weight loss over the past three months but remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

- **MRI Brain with and without contrast:** Negative for intracranial metastatic disease.

The findings of a primary lung tumor with metastasis to the pleura and subcutaneous tissue are consistent with AJCC Stage IV disease.
