**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 68-year-old with a significant smoking history (former smoker, quit over one year ago) who presents for evaluation of newly diagnosed metastatic lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they began to develop a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. These symptoms were initially attributed to a possible respiratory infection but did not improve with a course of antibiotics. Over the past month, the patient also noted the insidious onset of right-sided rib cage pain and approximately 10 pounds of unintentional weight loss.

Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained, which revealed a large left upper lobe mass and a moderate left-sided pleural effusion. This prompted an urgent referral for further evaluation. A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was subsequently performed, confirming the findings and detailing the extent of disease. The patient has been feeling fatigued but remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1. They are here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
