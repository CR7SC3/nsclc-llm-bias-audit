**HPI:**
This is a 71-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is an active, current smoker who initially presented to primary care several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. An initial chest radiograph was concerning for a right-sided pulmonary mass, which prompted further evaluation. The patient reports some mild fatigue but denies hemoptysis, chest pain, or significant unintentional weight loss. Performance status is excellent, estimated at ECOG 1, with the patient remaining independent in all activities of daily living.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest x-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed. This study confirmed a 4 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The CT also revealed multiple enlarged ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes, raising suspicion for locoregional nodal involvement.

For comprehensive staging, a PET/CT scan was subsequently obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the right paratracheal and subcarinal nodal stations (N2 disease). No FDG-avid lesions suggestive of distant metastatic disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or skeleton were identified. A staging MRI of the brain with contrast was also performed and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and transbronchial needle aspiration (TBNA) of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified (nos).

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the biopsy specimen. Comprehensive molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations; the tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 71-year-old, treatment-naive patient with a new diagnosis of clinical Stage IIIA (cT2aN2M0) non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified. The diagnostic workup is consistent with locally advanced disease confined to the right hemithorax and mediastinum, with no evidence of distant metastatic spread. Molecular analysis reveals no targetable driver mutations. The patient presents today for initial consultation to discuss these findings.
