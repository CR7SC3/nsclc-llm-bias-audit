**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 58-year-old individual with an excellent baseline functional status who presents for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. The history was obtained from the patient and a comprehensive review of their medical records.

The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately three months ago, when they developed a persistent, dry cough. The cough did not improve with a course of antibiotics. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also noted the onset of mild fatigue and some dyspnea on exertion, which prompted a visit to their primary care provider. A chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass, leading to an expedited diagnostic workup. The patient denies any fevers, night sweats, hemoptysis, or unintentional weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and independent with all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 0.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
I have personally reviewed the patient's relevant imaging and pathology reports.

*   **CT of the Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis with contrast** on [De-identified date] revealed a 4.1 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was associated prominent ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy, with the largest node measuring 2.5 cm. No suspicious lesions were seen in the liver, adrenal glands, or osseous structures.

*   **PET/CT Scan** was performed for staging purposes. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in multiple right-sided mediastinal lymph node stations, consistent with locoregional nodal involvement. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere to suggest distant metastatic spread.

*   **MRI of the Brain with and without contrast** was completed as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

*   **CT-guided core needle biopsy** of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed by Interventional Radiology on [De-identified date]. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma.
