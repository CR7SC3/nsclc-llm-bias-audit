**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 77-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit within the last year. They presented to their primary care provider approximately one month ago with progressive shortness of breath on exertion and a persistent, dry cough. They were initially treated for a suspected respiratory infection without improvement. Due to worsening dyspnea, a chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a right-sided lung opacity and a large pleural effusion. The patient was subsequently referred for further diagnostic evaluation. They deny any fevers, chills, or significant weight loss but do report increasing fatigue that limits daily activities. Performance status is ECOG 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a CT of the chest with contrast was performed. This confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right lower lobe. The scan also demonstrated a large, complex right-sided pleural effusion with associated pleural thickening and nodularity, suspicious for malignant involvement. No other suspicious thoracic or upper abdominal abnormalities were noted on this scan.

To obtain a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided thoracentesis. Approximately 1.5 liters of serosanguinous fluid were removed, which provided significant symptomatic relief. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, consistent with adenocarcinoma.

Staging workup was completed with a PET/CT scan, which demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the known right lower lobe primary mass and extensive FDG-avid nodularity throughout the right pleura, confirming metastatic disease. No other sites of FDG-avid metastatic disease were identified in the neck, chest, abdomen, or pelvis. A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

**Molecular Studies:**
Pathology from the pleural fluid specimen confirmed non-small cell lung cancer with adenocarcinoma histology. The specimen was sent for comprehensive molecular profiling.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
- Next-generation sequencing (NGS) of the tumor DNA identified an activating KRAS G12C mutation.
- The tumor was negative for other targetable alterations, including mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was low (<2 mutations/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
1.  Newly diagnosed metastatic (Stage IV) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with malignant pleural effusion. AJCC Stage cT2b N0 M1a.
2.  Molecular profile notable for a KRAS G12C mutation and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%).
3.  Symptomatic from disease with dyspnea, managed with therapeutic thoracentesis.
4.  Former smoker with recent cessation.
