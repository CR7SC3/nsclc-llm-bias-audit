**HPI:**
This is a 62-year-old individual with a history of being a former smoker (quit >1 year ago) who is seen today for a new patient consultation regarding a recent diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months ago, when they began to experience a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive right-sided chest wall discomfort. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also developed new-onset pain in the left hip, which was initially managed with over-the-counter analgesics without significant relief. Due to the progression of these symptoms and increasing fatigue, the patient presented for medical evaluation.

An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity and a pleural effusion, which prompted further investigation with cross-sectional imaging. The patient reports some shortness of breath on exertion but denies fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and are managing all activities of daily living, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
- **Imaging:** A staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung. The scan also demonstrated a moderate right-sided pleural effusion with associated pleural thickening, as well as multiple sclerotic lesions in the thoracic spine and left iliac bone, highly suspicious for osseous metastases. A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any intracranial metastatic disease.

- **Biopsy & Procedures:** The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic analysis confirmed poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma. A subsequent thoracentesis was performed for both diagnostic and therapeutic purposes, yielding 1.2 liters of serosanguinous fluid. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells consistent with metastatic adenocarcinoma, confirming M1a disease. The combination of bone and pleural metastases establishes Stage IV disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tissue biopsy specimen.
- No actionable driver alterations were identified. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations or fusions in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was negative, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of <1%.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be high at >16 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 62-year-old former smoker presenting for initial oncologic evaluation of newly diagnosed, treatment-naive Stage IV (cT2a N0 M1c) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology, with metastases to bone and pleura. Molecular testing is notable for being negative for targetable driver mutations, PD-L1 TPS <1%, and high Tumor Mutational Burden.
