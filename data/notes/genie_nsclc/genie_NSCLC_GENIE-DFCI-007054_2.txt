**HPI:**
This is a 75-year-old patient with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, referred for initial oncology consultation and discussion of management. The patient was in a usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when a persistent, non-productive cough developed. This was later associated with new-onset right-sided chest wall pain and mild fatigue. An initial outpatient chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe mass, prompting further investigation. The patient denies hemoptysis, fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. Performance status is excellent; the patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1, limited only by some pain.

**PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:**
- Prostate Cancer, diagnosed approximately 10 years ago, treated with external beam radiation therapy with no evidence of recurrence.
- Hypertension, well-controlled on medication.
- Hyperlipidemia.

**PAST SURGICAL HISTORY:**
- None relevant.

**SOCIAL HISTORY:**
The patient is a never-smoker and reports minimal alcohol use. The patient lives at home and is independent with all activities of daily living.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated several small nodules in the left lung, consistent with contralateral metastatic disease. A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging, which showed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, multiple contralateral pulmonary nodules, and a solitary hypermetabolic focus in the right 7th rib, concerning for a bony metastasis.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathology returned as poorly differentiated non-small cell carcinoma, with immunohistochemical stains (p40 positive, TTF-1 negative) confirming a squamous cell carcinoma histology.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations, fusions, or amplifications in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be low (<2 mut/Mb).

**PROBLEM SUMMARY:**
In summary, this is a 75-year-old patient with an excellent performance status and a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV squamous non-small cell lung cancer, with metastatic disease to the contralateral lung and bone. The patient is a never-smoker. Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor did not identify an actionable oncogenic driver.
