**HPI:**
This is a 70-year-old individual with a history of being a former smoker (quit over one year ago) who is referred for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild dyspnea on exertion. These symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider, who obtained a chest X-ray that revealed a right-sided lung opacity. The patient was subsequently sent for further imaging. They report a good appetite and no significant weight loss. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 0-1, with the patient remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed as part of the initial workup. This study identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also revealed multiple enlarged ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, concerning for regional metastatic involvement. No suspicious lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or other visualized abdominal or pelvic organs.

Given these findings, a PET/CT was obtained for staging. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe mass and in multiple ipsilateral mediastinal lymph node stations, including the paratracheal and subcarinal regions, consistent with N2 nodal disease. Importantly, the PET/CT showed no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases.

To complete staging and to evaluate the mediastinum for a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS). Biopsies were successfully obtained from the right upper lobe mass and from an enlarged subcarinal lymph node. Pathology from the primary mass confirmed invasive, moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma. Cytopathology from the subcarinal lymph node fine-needle aspiration was positive for metastatic adenocarcinoma.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

Based on this comprehensive workup, the patient's cancer is clinically staged as AJCC 8th Edition Stage IIIA (cT2bN2M0) non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry were performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy. Key findings are as follows:
-   An actionable driver mutation was detected in **KRAS (G12C)**.
-   The tumor was **negative** for pathogenic alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2.
-   PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was **negative** (Tumor Proportion Score < 1%).
-   Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be **high** (>16 mutations/megabase).
