**HPI:**
This is a 69-year-old with a significant smoking history who is seen for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to an outside facility approximately one month ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and new-onset right-sided back pain. An initial chest X-ray was performed which revealed a large mass in the right upper lobe. The patient also reports an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds over the last three months, accompanied by fatigue. The patient denies hemoptysis, fevers, or chills. Given the initial imaging findings, a more comprehensive diagnostic evaluation was initiated. The patient is here today to review the results of the workup and discuss the diagnosis. The patient maintains an ECOG performance status of 1, remaining ambulatory and capable of all self-care but restricted in physically strenuous activity.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study confirmed a 5.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung. It also revealed multiple enlarged mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymph nodes. Several hypodense lesions were identified throughout the liver, consistent with metastatic deposits. Additionally, a lytic lesion was noted in the T7 vertebral body, corresponding to the patient's reported back pain.

A PET/CT scan was subsequently obtained for definitive staging. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe lung mass, as well as in mediastinal and supraclavicular lymph nodes. Avid uptake was also confirmed in multiple hepatic lesions and the lytic lesion in the thoracic spine, confirming widespread metastatic disease.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was performed as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass. Pathologic examination of the tissue revealed a poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.

Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be low at <2 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 69-year-old who presents for initial evaluation of a newly diagnosed metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The final diagnosis is Stage IV squamous cell carcinoma of the lung, with metastatic disease documented in the bone, liver, and distant lymph nodes. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and the absence of any identified targetable driver mutations. The patient is treatment-naive and is here to establish care.
