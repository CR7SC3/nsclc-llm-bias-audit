**HPI:**
This is a 64-year-old individual with a history of smoking who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation to discuss a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially attributed to a seasonal illness, but when symptoms failed to resolve, they sought evaluation with their primary care provider. An initial chest radiograph was notable for a right-sided pulmonary opacity, which prompted further investigation. The patient denies hemoptysis, fever, chills, or significant unintentional weight loss. They report mild exertional dyspnea but are otherwise fully ambulatory and managing all activities of daily living. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 1. Past medical history is otherwise non-contributory. Social history is notable for prior tobacco use, having quit over one year ago.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest x-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed. This study identified a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was no evidence of mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy. To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was obtained, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the known right upper lobe mass. Importantly, the PET/CT did not reveal any other sites of FDG-avid disease concerning for regional nodal involvement or distant metastases. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for intracranial metastatic disease.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Histopathologic examination of the biopsy specimen confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. This analysis identified an activating EGFR L858R point mutation. Further testing was negative for other actionable driver mutations, including rearrangements in ALK and ROS1, and mutations in BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 64-year-old with an excellent performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IIA (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma of the right upper lobe. The tumor is characterized by a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and establish a plan for management.
