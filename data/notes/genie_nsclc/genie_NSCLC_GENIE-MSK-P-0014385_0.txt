**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is an initial medical oncology consultation for a 61-year-old who presents for evaluation and management of newly diagnosed metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of excellent health until approximately two months ago when they developed a persistent, dry cough and progressive right-sided back pain. The symptoms were initially managed conservatively but did not improve. Over the past month, the patient also noted increasing fatigue and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and has no significant history of occupational or environmental exposures. Performance status is ECOG 1; the patient is fully ambulatory and capable of self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity by symptoms.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe lung mass, which prompted further investigation.

A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed, which characterized a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also identified multiple suspicious nodules in the contralateral (left) lung and a lytic lesion in the T8 vertebral body, concerning for metastatic disease.

A subsequent whole-body PET/CT scan was performed for staging. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass. Avid uptake was also demonstrated in several contralateral lung nodules and within the previously noted T8 vertebral body lesion, consistent with metastatic involvement. There was no other evidence of distant metastatic disease in the neck, abdomen, or pelvis.

An MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed for central nervous system staging and revealed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed. Pathology confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer with features most consistent with adenocarcinoma histology.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry were performed on the tumor tissue specimen.
- **Driver Mutations:** An ALK gene rearrangement was detected. Testing was negative for other actionable alterations, including EGFR, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, ERBB2, and KRAS.
- **PD-L1 Expression:** Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was low at <2 mutations/megabase.

**PROBLEM SUMMARY:**
In summary, this is a 61-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, AJCC 8th Edition. Staging studies have confirmed metastatic disease involving the contralateral lung and bone, with no evidence of central nervous system involvement. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of an ALK rearrangement and high PD-L1 expression. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and the implications of these findings.
