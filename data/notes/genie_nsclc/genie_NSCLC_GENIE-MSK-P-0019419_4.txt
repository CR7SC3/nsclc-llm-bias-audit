**HPI:**
This is an initial medical oncology consultation for a 52-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. These symptoms progressed, prompting a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, and the patient was referred for further diagnostic evaluation. The patient reports no significant weight loss, hemoptysis, or constitutional symptoms. They are fully ambulatory and maintain an excellent performance status.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed. This study confirmed the presence of a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The CT also identified several enlarged right-sided mediastinal lymph nodes, including in the paratracheal (4R) and subcarinal (7) stations, raising suspicion for nodal involvement.

To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the previously noted right paratracheal and subcarinal lymph nodes. Importantly, the PET/CT did not reveal any evidence of distant metastatic disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A brain MRI with contrast was also performed as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent an endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) with fine-needle aspiration of the right upper lobe mass and the subcarinal lymph node. Pathology from both sites was consistent with invasive adenocarcinoma, confirming the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specifically, the tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 52-year-old never-smoker who presents for initial evaluation of a newly diagnosed, treatment-naive, Stage IIIA (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnostic workup confirms locoregionally advanced disease involving the right upper lobe and ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes, with no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling of the tumor did not identify any actionable driver mutations. The patient has an excellent performance status and is here to discuss the diagnosis and potential management strategies.
