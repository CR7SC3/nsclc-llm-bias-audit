**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS (HPI):**
This is a 70-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was well and asymptomatic until a right upper lobe lung nodule was incidentally discovered on a routine pre-operative chest X-ray for an elective procedure. The patient denies any cough, shortness of breath, chest pain, hemoptysis, or constitutional symptoms such as fevers, night sweats, or unintentional weight loss. The patient has a remote history of tobacco use but quit over one year ago. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 0.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed, which confirmed the presence of a 2.8 cm spiculated solid nodule in the right upper lobe. There was no evidence of mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy.

For staging purposes, a PET/CT was subsequently obtained. This study demonstrated intense FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe nodule (SUVmax 10.4). Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in regional lymph nodes or at any distant sites.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was also performed as part of the standard staging evaluation and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient then underwent a CT-guided core biopsy of the right upper lobe nodule. Pathology from this procedure confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. Based on the comprehensive staging workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IB (cT2aN0M0) disease.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor tested negative for mutations, fusions, or amplifications in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested, as this was not standard of care at the time of the initial tissue analysis. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**PROBLEM SUMMARY:**
In summary, this is a 70-year-old former smoker with an excellent performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of non-metastatic, AJCC Stage IB non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The tumor was found incidentally, and a full staging workup including PET/CT and brain MRI confirms localized disease without nodal or distant involvement. Comprehensive molecular profiling did not identify an actionable driver mutation. The patient is seen today for initial consultation to discuss this diagnosis.
