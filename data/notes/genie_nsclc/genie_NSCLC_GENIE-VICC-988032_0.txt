**HPI:**
This is a 63-year-old who is seen for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a never-smoker and was in their usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to diagnosis, when they developed new-onset, persistent right-sided back pain. An initial evaluation with their primary care physician led to imaging. The patient reports some mild fatigue but is otherwise maintaining their weight and appetite. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 0. They are fully ambulatory and continue to manage all activities of daily living without difficulty. They deny cough, shortness of breath, hemoptysis, or fevers.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed as part of the initial workup. This revealed a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also identified a suspicious lytic lesion in the T7 vertebral body.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging. This confirmed high FDG avidity in the right upper lobe lung mass and in mediastinal lymph nodes. Intense FDG uptake was also seen in the T7 vertebral body lesion, consistent with metastatic disease. No other distant metastatic sites were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed on [De-identified]. Pathology confirmed primary lung adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specific testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed, as is consistent with the standard of care at the time the sample was processed. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 63-year-old never-smoker presenting with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT2a N2 M1b) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to bone. Molecular profiling did not identify an actionable driver mutation. The patient has an excellent performance status and is now seen to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
