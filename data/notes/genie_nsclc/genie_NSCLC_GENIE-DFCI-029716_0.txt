**HPI:**
This 70-year-old patient presents for initial oncology consultation following a recent diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to their primary care physician with a several-month history of persistent cough, increasing dyspnea on exertion, and unintentional weight loss. Initial evaluation included a chest X-ray, which revealed a suspicious lung mass.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Subsequent workup included a contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, which confirmed a primary mass in the lung and demonstrated regional lymphadenopathy in the mediastinum and hilum. A CT-guided biopsy of the lung mass was performed, confirming the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Further dedicated staging with a PET/CT scan showed uptake in the primary lung lesion and associated mediastinal lymph nodes, consistent with locoregional disease. Given the clinical presentation and for comprehensive staging, a brain MRI was also performed, which identified several brain metastases. Based on these findings, the patient's AJCC stage at diagnosis is IIIA, with synchronous brain metastases. The patient remains treatment-naive for their cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Molecular profiling of the tumor tissue was performed. Results indicated no actionable driver alteration, specifically negative for EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14 skipping, RET fusion, and NTRK fusion. PD-L1 expression testing results are not yet available.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 70-year-old patient with newly diagnosed non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, AJCC Stage IIIA with synchronous brain metastases. Molecular profiling revealed no actionable driver alterations (negative for EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK). PD-L1 status is pending. The patient is treatment-naive, and a comprehensive treatment strategy needs to be formulated.
