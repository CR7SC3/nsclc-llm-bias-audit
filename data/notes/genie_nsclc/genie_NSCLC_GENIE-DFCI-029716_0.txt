**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 70-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to their primary care provider several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea that had developed over the preceding two months. The patient is a current smoker but otherwise reports being active and maintaining a good functional status. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided pulmonary opacity, prompting further imaging.

A subsequent CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed for staging. This confirmed the presence of a primary lung mass and locoregional lymphadenopathy, but no evidence of distant metastatic disease. The patient was referred for a tissue diagnosis and subsequently to our clinic for consultation. The patient remains in good spirits, with an ECOG performance status of 1, fully independent with all activities of daily living.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast demonstrated a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was significant ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy noted in the right paratracheal (level 4R) and subcarinal (level 7) stations, with the largest node measuring 2.3 cm in short-axis diameter. The visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis, including the liver and adrenal glands, were negative for metastatic lesions.

A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass was performed on [De-identified]. Pathology confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin.

Based on this workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IIIA (cT2bN2M0) NSCLC.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor biopsy specimen. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations, fusions, or amplifications involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mutations/megabase). PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 70-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The patient is treatment-naive. Molecular profiling is negative for common actionable driver alterations. The patient is here for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
