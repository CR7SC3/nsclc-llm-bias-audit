**History of Present Illness**

This is a 59-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed persistent mid-back pain. The pain was initially intermittent but became constant and began to interfere with sleep. This prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray was suggestive of a right-sided lung opacity, leading to further cross-sectional imaging. A CT scan of the chest confirmed the presence of a right lower lobe lung mass and also identified a suspicious lesion in a thoracic vertebra. The patient was subsequently referred for a diagnostic biopsy. The patient reports ongoing back pain, which is partially controlled with over-the-counter analgesics, and a mild non-productive cough. They deny hemoptysis, fevers, or significant weight loss. Their energy levels are slightly decreased but they remain fully ambulatory and independent in all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1.
