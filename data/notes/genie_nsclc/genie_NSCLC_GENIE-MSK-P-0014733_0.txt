This is a 59-year-old individual who presents for an initial oncology consultation to discuss a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The history was obtained from the patient and a thorough review of available outside medical records, imaging, and pathology reports.

**History of Present Illness:**
The patient is a current smoker who was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was initially attributed to a respiratory infection, but symptoms failed to resolve with a course of antibiotics. Due to worsening shortness of breath and new-onset right-sided chest discomfort, the patient presented to an outside facility for evaluation.

A chest radiograph revealed a large right upper lobe opacity, prompting a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest. This study confirmed the presence of a large mass in the right upper lobe with associated bulky right-sided mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. To complete staging, a PET/CT was subsequently performed, which demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in ipsilateral mediastinal lymph node stations. Importantly, there was no evidence of distant metastatic disease. A brain MRI was also obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient was referred for bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis and nodal staging. Biopsies were successfully obtained from the primary mass and an involved subcarinal lymph node. Pathology confirmed squamous cell carcinoma.

The patient reports some fatigue but remains fully ambulatory and independent in all activities of daily living. They have had an approximate 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past three months. They deny fevers, chills, night sweats, or significant hemoptysis. They are here today to establish care and discuss the findings.
