**HPI:**
This is a 76-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to their primary care provider several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and new-onset, nagging low back pain. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, prompting further investigation. The patient reports mild fatigue but is otherwise maintaining a good performance status, continuing with all activities of daily living with minimal assistance. Review of systems is otherwise negative for fevers, chills, weight loss, or hemoptysis.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed. This imaging identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, along with mediastinal lymphadenopathy.

For definitive staging, a PET/CT scan was subsequently obtained. This demonstrated high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in mediastinal lymph nodes. Additionally, the scan revealed multiple foci of intense uptake in the axial skeleton, including a lytic lesion in the L4 vertebral body, consistent with osseous metastases. A contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for intracranial metastatic disease.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed on [De-identified]. Pathology review confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tumor tissue from the biopsy was sent for next-generation sequencing. The results are as follows:
-   **Actionable driver detected:** EGFR uncommon sensitizing mutation.
-   **Negative for:** ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2 (HER2) mutations or fusions.
-   **PD-L1:** Not tested.
-   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 76-year-old former smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastatic disease to bone. Molecular profiling is notable for the presence of an uncommon sensitizing EGFR mutation. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss these findings.
