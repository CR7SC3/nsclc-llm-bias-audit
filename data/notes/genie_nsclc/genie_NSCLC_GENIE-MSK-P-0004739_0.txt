**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 41-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, referred for initial oncology evaluation and management. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to diagnosis, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive headaches. The headaches were associated with intermittent word-finding difficulty, which prompted an evaluation in an outside emergency department.

An initial CT of the head revealed several lesions concerning for metastatic disease. This was followed by a staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, which identified a large primary mass in the right lung, suspicious mediastinal lymph nodes, and a nodule in the contralateral lung. The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided biopsy of the right lung mass, which established the diagnosis. A brain MRI and a PET/CT scan were completed for full staging, confirming the extent of disease.

The patient is a former smoker with a greater than 10-pack-year history, though they quit all tobacco use over one year ago. They report mild fatigue and some intermittent right-sided back pain but are otherwise maintaining their weight and appetite. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1.
