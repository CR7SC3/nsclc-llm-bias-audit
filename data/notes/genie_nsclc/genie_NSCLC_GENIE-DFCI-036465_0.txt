**HPI:**
This is a 47-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside facility approximately three weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. An initial chest radiograph demonstrated a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further investigation. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago, and reports a 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past two months. Otherwise, the patient feels well and maintains an excellent functional status, continuing with daily activities without significant limitation (ECOG Performance Status 0). There is no history of hemoptysis, chest pain, or neurological symptoms such as headache or focal weakness.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Staging workup was initiated, including contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This imaging revealed a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. Critically, the scan also identified a solitary 2.5 cm hypoattenuating lesion in the liver, highly suspicious for metastatic disease. A subsequent MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and showed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass on [De-identified Date]. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma with features consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry were performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the lung biopsy. Key findings are as follows:
- An actionable driver mutation was identified: **ALK rearrangement detected**.
- Testing was **negative** for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was **positive** (specific Tumor Proportion Score percentage is not available).
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be **intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb)**.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 47-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The disease is metastatic to the liver. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of an ALK gene rearrangement. The patient is treatment-naive with an excellent performance status.
