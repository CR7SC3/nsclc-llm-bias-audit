**HPI:**
This is a 75-year-old with a history of smoking, quit over one year ago, who is referred for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to primary care with a persistent, non-productive cough developing over the last several months. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further investigation. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was subsequently performed, which confirmed a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was obtained, which demonstrated FDG-avidity confined to the primary lung mass without evidence of mediastinal, hilar, or distant metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases. The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass, which established the diagnosis. The patient is functionally independent with an ECOG performance status of 1, limited only by mild dyspnea on exertion, and denies hemoptysis, fevers, or unintentional weight loss.
