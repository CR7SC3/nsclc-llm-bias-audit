**HPI:**
This is a 54-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when a persistent, non-productive cough developed. The cough did not resolve with a course of outpatient antibiotics, prompting a chest X-ray which revealed a right-sided pulmonary opacity. The patient has a history of former tobacco use, having quit over one year ago. The patient is otherwise active and reports a good energy level with no significant weight loss, hemoptysis, or chest pain.

Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed on [De-identified]. This study confirmed the presence of a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy, with nodes measuring up to 2.5 cm in the right paratracheal (4R) station.

A PET/CT scan was subsequently performed for staging purposes on [De-identified]. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the known right upper lobe primary mass (SUVmax 12.4) and in the right paratracheal (4R) and subcarinal (7) lymph nodes (SUVmax 9.8). No FDG-avid lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones to suggest distant metastatic disease.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was performed for central nervous system staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathology review confirmed the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer with features most consistent with adenocarcinoma histology.

Based on the combination of these findings, the patient's disease is staged as AJCC 8th Edition, clinical Stage IIIA.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the biopsy has been sent for comprehensive next-generation sequencing; these results are currently pending. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on the specimen. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) analysis was performed and was found to be low (<2 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 54-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, non-metastatic, Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup has confirmed disease confined to the right lung and ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes, with no evidence of distant metastatic disease. The patient has an excellent performance status and is here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
