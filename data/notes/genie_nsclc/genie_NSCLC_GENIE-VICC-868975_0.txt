**HPI:**
This is a 63-year-old individual who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately two months ago, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. These symptoms prompted an evaluation by their primary care provider. An initial chest radiograph was abnormal, leading to further cross-sectional imaging. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. They report good functional status, are fully ambulatory, and are capable of all self-care activities.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. Imaging demonstrated a primary mass in one lung with multiple nodules identified in the contralateral lung, consistent with metastatic disease. There was no evidence of metastatic involvement of the liver, adrenal glands, or bone.

A staging MRI of the brain was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary lung mass was performed for tissue diagnosis. Pathology confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with adenocarcinoma histology.

PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen.
