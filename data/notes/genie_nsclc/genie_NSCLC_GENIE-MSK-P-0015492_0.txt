**HPI:**
This is a 52-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially sought evaluation with their primary care provider several weeks ago due to a persistent dry cough, progressive fatigue, and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds over the last three months. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year prior. An initial chest x-ray demonstrated a right-sided lung mass, which prompted a full diagnostic workup. The patient is here today to discuss the findings and establish care. The patient maintains a good performance status, is fully ambulatory, and capable of all self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified date]. Imaging revealed a 4.2 cm primary mass in the right upper lobe. There was evidence of mediastinal and contralateral supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. A discrete 2.5 cm enhancing nodule was also identified in the left adrenal gland, concerning for a site of distant metastasis.

To complete staging, a brain MRI with and without contrast was performed and showed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic evaluation of the tissue confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary based on immunohistochemical staining.

Based on this workup, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IV disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue specimen.
- No actionable oncogenic driver mutations were identified. The tumor was negative for alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- A loss-of-function mutation was detected in the STK11 gene.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 52-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, metastatic to distant lymph nodes and the left adrenal gland. Molecular profiling is notable for PD-L1 expression (TPS 1-49%) and the presence of an STK11 loss-of-function mutation, with no other identified targetable driver mutations. The patient is treatment-naive and presents today to discuss the diagnosis in detail.
