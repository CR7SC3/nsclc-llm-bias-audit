**HPI:**
This is a 72-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, presenting for initial oncology consultation. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was associated with a 15-pound unintentional weight loss over the same period. An initial evaluation with their primary care provider led to a chest X-ray that revealed a right-sided lung mass. The patient is a former smoker, having quit approximately 15 years ago, with a prior 30-pack-year history. The patient remains ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is limited in strenuous activity, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This demonstrated a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also revealed a moderate-sized, malignant-appearing right pleural effusion and a 2.1 cm hypodense lesion in segment VI of the liver, suspicious for a metastasis. No other sites of distant disease were noted in the chest or abdomen.

A subsequent PET/CT scan confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, the right pleura, and the hepatic lesion. No other suspicious hypermetabolic foci were identified. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was performed for staging purposes and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass. Pathology from this procedure confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

Based on the presence of a primary lung tumor with metastases to the pleura and liver, the patient's cancer is classified as AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. No actionable driver alterations were identified; results were negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.

Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 72-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastases to the pleura and liver. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and the absence of identified targetable driver mutations.
