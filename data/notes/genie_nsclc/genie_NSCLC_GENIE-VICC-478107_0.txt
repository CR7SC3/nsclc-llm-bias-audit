**HPI:**
This is a 68-year-old who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks ago when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially managed symptomatically but did not resolve, prompting a chest X-ray which revealed a right-sided pulmonary opacity. The patient denies hemoptysis, fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. They report mild fatigue but remain fully active and ambulatory, with an ECOG performance status of 1. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed on [De-identified]. This confirmed the presence of a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. Additionally, a 1.5 cm nodule was identified in the contralateral left lower lobe, concerning for metastatic disease. No significant mediastinal or hilar adenopathy was noted.

A subsequent PET/CT scan on [De-identified] demonstrated high FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the contralateral left lower lobe nodule. No other sites of FDG-avid disease were identified in the abdomen, pelvis, or skeleton. An MRI of the brain with contrast was performed for staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core biopsy of the right upper lobe mass on [De-identified]. Histopathological analysis confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin.

Based on this workup, the patient carries a diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the core biopsy was sent for comprehensive next-generation sequencing. Analysis did not identify any actionable driver alterations; testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be low (<2 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested, as was consistent with standard practice at the time of the initial tissue analysis.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 68-year-old lifetime never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to the contralateral lung. The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of any identified targetable driver mutations and a low tumor mutational burden. The patient is here today to discuss the implications of this diagnosis and the overall goals of care.
