**HPI:**
This is a 68-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who is referred for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially treated as bronchitis without resolution. Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained which demonstrated a right upper lobe opacity. This prompted further evaluation with a contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest.

The patient is otherwise feeling well, denying fevers, chills, or unintentional weight loss. They report good energy levels and maintain full activity without significant limitation, corresponding to an ECOG performance status of 0.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive staging workup was undertaken following the initial abnormal imaging.

A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis confirmed a large, 6.1 cm spiculated mass in the posterior aspect of the right upper lobe. The mass was noted to abut the major fissure. No pathologically enlarged mediastinal or hilar lymph nodes were identified. The visualized portions of the abdomen, including the liver and adrenal glands, were unremarkable.

A full-body PET/CT scan was performed for staging. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity within the known right upper lobe mass. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in regional lymph nodes or in any distant sites to suggest metastatic disease.

Given the risk of occult central nervous system involvement, a dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed, which was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathology from this procedure returned as invasive, moderately differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 68-year-old former smoker with an excellent performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IIB (cT3N0M0) non-small cell lung cancer of squamous cell histology. The diagnosis was established via CT-guided biopsy of a right upper lobe mass. Staging workup, including PET/CT and brain MRI, is negative for nodal or distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations. The patient is treatment-naive and presents for initial oncology consultation.
