**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 73-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks ago, when they began to experience a progressively worsening, non-productive cough and shortness of breath with exertion. These symptoms prompted an evaluation in the emergency department.

Initial imaging with a chest X-ray revealed a large left-sided pleural effusion and suspicion for an underlying parenchymal mass. The patient was admitted for further workup.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed the presence of a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe. A moderate-to-large, loculated left pleural effusion was also noted, with associated pleural thickening suspicious for malignant involvement. No suspicious mediastinal or hilar adenopathy was identified. The visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis were negative for metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was subsequently performed and revealed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a thoracentesis for both therapeutic and diagnostic purposes. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, consistent with adenocarcinoma. Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV disease. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and maintains an excellent performance status, ECOG 1.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the cell block from the pleural fluid.
- **Actionable driver mutation:** An EGFR L858R point mutation was detected.
- **Additional testing:** The tumor was negative for ALK and ROS1 rearrangements, as well as for mutations in BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- **Immunohistochemistry:** PD-L1 expression was negative.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**PROBLEM SUMMARY:**
In summary, this is a 73-year-old never-smoker with an excellent performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, characterized by an EGFR L858R sensitizing mutation. The patient is here today to discuss the implications of this diagnosis and to establish care.
