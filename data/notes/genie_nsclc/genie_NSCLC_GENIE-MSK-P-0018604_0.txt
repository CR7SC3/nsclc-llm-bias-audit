**HPI:**
This is a 59-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately three months prior to evaluation, when they developed a persistent dry cough. When the cough failed to resolve with conservative measures and was later associated with intermittent, scant hemoptysis, the patient sought evaluation with their primary care provider. The patient is a current smoker. A review of systems is notable for some generalized fatigue but is otherwise negative for fevers, chills, night sweats, or unintentional weight loss. The patient maintains a good appetite and remains fully ambulatory and independent in all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest radiograph revealed a right upper lobe opacity, which prompted a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest. This study confirmed the presence of a 4.8 cm spiculated mass in the apical segment of the right upper lobe. There was no associated pleural effusion or definite chest wall invasion. Staging evaluation proceeded with a PET/CT scan from skull base to mid-thigh. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the known right upper lobe mass. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the mediastinal or hilar lymph nodes, nor were there any findings suggestive of distant metastatic disease in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis. A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer with squamous cell features.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tumor tissue from the biopsy was sent for next-generation sequencing to assess for molecular alterations. No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 59-year-old with a new diagnosis of clinical Stage IIB non-small cell lung cancer, squamous cell carcinoma histology. A comprehensive staging workup has confirmed disease localized to the lung without evidence of regional lymph node involvement or distant metastatic spread. Molecular analysis did not identify an actionable driver mutation. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and establish a plan for care.
