**HPI:**
This is a 57-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, referred for initial consultation. The patient initially presented to an outside facility approximately one month ago with several weeks of progressive, dull ache in the right hip and lower back, which was initially attributed to musculoskeletal strain. Due to lack of improvement and increasing pain severity that began to limit ambulation, the patient sought further evaluation.

An X-ray of the pelvis revealed a suspicious lytic lesion in the right iliac bone. This prompted a staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast, which identified a 4.5 cm primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal adenopathy. The scan confirmed the destructive osseous lesion in the right ilium, consistent with metastatic disease. A subsequent MRI of the brain was performed for staging purposes and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient reports significant fatigue and ongoing pain related to the bone metastasis, managed with intermittent analgesics. Appetite is fair with a recent unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds. The patient is a former smoker with a 30-pack-year history, having quit over 10 years ago. Performance status is ECOG 1, with the patient fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but restricted in physically strenuous activity. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
