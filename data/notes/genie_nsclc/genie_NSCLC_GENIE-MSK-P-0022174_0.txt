**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 58-year-old, treatment-naive patient with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and right-sided pleuritic chest pain. This was followed by progressive fatigue and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds. Initial evaluation with their primary care provider led to a chest radiograph which showed a right upper lobe opacity. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest confirmed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, associated with a moderate right-sided pleural effusion and an adjacent lytic lesion in the T7 vertebral body.

The patient has maintained good functional status despite symptoms, with an ECOG performance status of 1. They deny fevers, chills, or night sweats. They report mild dyspnea on exertion but are able to perform all activities of daily living.
