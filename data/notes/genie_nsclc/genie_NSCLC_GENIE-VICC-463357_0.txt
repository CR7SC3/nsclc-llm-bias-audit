**HPI:**
This is a 64-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially sought medical attention approximately one month ago for a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive shortness of breath on exertion. An initial chest X-ray at an outside facility revealed a right-sided lung mass, which prompted further diagnostic evaluation. The patient reports associated fatigue but remains ambulatory and capable of all self-care. There has been an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the past two months. The patient is a current smoker. Performance status is ECOG 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with intravenous contrast was performed. This confirmed the presence of a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated multiple nodules in the contralateral (left) lung, suspicious for metastatic disease. A moderate right-sided pleural effusion was identified. There was no evidence of metastatic disease in the abdomen or pelvis. A subsequent MRI of the brain was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass on [De-identified Date]. Histopathologic examination confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma. A diagnostic and therapeutic thoracentesis of the right-sided pleural effusion was also performed, and cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, confirming M1a disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tissue obtained from the core biopsy. No actionable driver alterations were identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as high (>16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 64-year-old with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging studies confirm metastatic disease involving the contralateral lung and pleura. Molecular profiling is notable for a high Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) but is negative for targetable driver mutations. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis in detail and to review the findings.
