**HPI:**
This is a 76-year-old individual with a past medical history notable only for well-controlled hypertension, who presents for initial medical oncology evaluation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough. This was initially treated as bronchitis without resolution. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient noted the onset of mild dyspnea on exertion, which prompted a visit to their primary care provider. The patient denies hemoptysis, chest pain, or significant weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and independent in all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1. Notably, the patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided pulmonary mass, which led to a staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast. This imaging confirmed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also identified a distinct 1.5 cm nodule in the contralateral left lower lobe, suspicious for metastatic disease. No other suspicious lesions were noted in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A subsequent whole-body PET/CT scan was performed, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the contralateral left lower lobe nodule, consistent with metastatic disease. No other distant sites of FDG-avid malignancy were identified.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma.

Given the diagnosis of metastatic disease, a brain MRI with and without contrast was performed for central nervous system staging, which was negative for any intracranial metastases.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue specimen.
-   **Driver Mutations:** No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
-   **PD-L1 Expression:** PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry (IHC) was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range.
-   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 76-year-old lifetime never-smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, of squamous histology, with metastasis to the contralateral lung. The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of an actionable driver mutation and a PD-L1 TPS of 1-49%.
