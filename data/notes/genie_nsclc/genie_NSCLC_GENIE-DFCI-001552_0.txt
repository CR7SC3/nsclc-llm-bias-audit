**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 71-year-old former smoker who presents for an initial medical oncology evaluation following a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and a new onset of mild exertional dyspnea. These symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest radiograph revealed a right-sided lung opacity, leading to further diagnostic imaging. The patient reports a 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past three months but otherwise denies fevers, chills, night sweats, or hemoptysis. **DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
Following the abnormal chest x-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This study characterized a 5 cm mass in the right upper lobe and demonstrated significant ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy.

For definitive staging, a PET/CT scan was subsequently obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe mass and in right-sided mediastinal (paratracheal and subcarinal) lymph nodes. No FDG-avid lesions suggestive of distant metastatic disease were identified in the contralateral chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

To complete the staging workup and rule out central nervous system involvement, a brain MRI with contrast was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis. A fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node was performed. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung primary.
