**HPI:**
This is a 47-year-old individual with a history of former tobacco use (quit >1 year ago) who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately six weeks prior to evaluation, when they noted the onset of a persistent dry cough and a sense of chest pressure. These symptoms were initially managed conservatively but progressed, prompting a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, which led to further diagnostic evaluation. The patient reports mild fatigue but denies hemoptysis, fevers, chills, or significant unintentional weight loss. Performance status is excellent; the patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though is limited in strenuous activity by dyspnea (ECOG 1).

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which identified a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. This scan also revealed extensive and bulky mediastinal and right-sided supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, concerning for advanced nodal involvement. No discrete distant visceral or osseous metastases were identified.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was obtained. This study confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the extensive mediastinal (paratracheal, subcarinal) and right supraclavicular nodal stations. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease below the diaphragm or in the contralateral hemithorax to suggest distant metastatic spread. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (EBUS-FNA) of a subcarinal lymph node for tissue diagnosis. Pathologic examination of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tumor tissue from the biopsy was sent for comprehensive next-generation sequencing and biomarker analysis.
- Molecular profiling did not identify an actionable driver alteration. Results were negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 1–49%.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 47-year-old former smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of unresectable, non-metastatic Stage IIIB (AJCC 8th edition) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging is based on a large primary tumor with extensive N3 nodal involvement and no evidence of distant metastatic disease (M0). Molecular studies are notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations and a PD-L1 TPS of 1-49%. The patient has an excellent performance status and is here today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
