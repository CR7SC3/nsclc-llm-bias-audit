**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 60-year-old individual with a history of prior tobacco use who is referred for initial consultation and management of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. **Diagnostic Workup:**
The initial presentation prompted a chest X-ray which revealed a right-sided pulmonary mass. Subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed for staging. This confirmed a primary mass in the right upper lobe with associated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. No suspicious lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones to suggest distant metastatic disease.

A PET/CT scan was then obtained, which demonstrated high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe tumor and the adjacent mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, consistent with locoregional disease. No other sites of FDG-avid disease were identified. A brain MRI with contrast was performed to complete staging and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary non-small cell lung cancer.

- **Other Drivers:** Testing was negative for EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14 skipping mutations, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2 mutations.
