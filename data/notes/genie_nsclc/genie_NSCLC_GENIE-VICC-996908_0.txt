**HPI:**
This is an initial consultation for a 59-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit within the last year. The patient initially presented to an outside facility several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild dyspnea on exertion. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, prompting further evaluation.

A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed the presence of a primary mass in the right upper lobe and also demonstrated prominent mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. To complete staging, a PET/CT was obtained which showed intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe mass and in ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones to suggest distant metastatic spread. A brain MRI was also performed and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

The patient was referred for a bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis and nodal staging. Biopsies of the primary mass and mediastinal lymph nodes were obtained. The patient has tolerated the workup well and presents today to establish care and discuss the findings. The patient is active and independent with an ECOG performance status of 1, limited only by mild respiratory symptoms.
