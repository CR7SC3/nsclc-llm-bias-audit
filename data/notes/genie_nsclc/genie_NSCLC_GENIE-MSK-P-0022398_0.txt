**HPI:**
This is a 25-year-old individual who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a history as a former smoker. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they began to develop a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive fatigue. These symptoms prompted a visit to an urgent care center, where a chest X-ray revealed a suspicious right-sided pulmonary opacity. The patient was referred for further diagnostic evaluation. At the time of this consultation, the patient reports ongoing fatigue and a mild cough but is fully ambulatory and managing activities of daily living with some effort. ECOG performance status is 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, the patient underwent a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis on [De-identified Date]. This imaging demonstrated a large, irregular 5.5 cm mass in the right lower lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also revealed multiple hypodense lesions throughout the liver, consistent with metastatic deposits, with the largest measuring 4.2 cm. An additional 1.8 cm nodule was noted in the contralateral left upper lobe, also suspicious for metastasis.

A subsequent PET/CT scan on [De-identified Date] was performed for staging. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right lower lobe mass, the mediastinal lymph nodes, the multiple liver lesions, and the contralateral lung nodule. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed as part of the standard metastatic workup and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core biopsy of a right-sided liver lesion on [De-identified Date]. Pathological analysis of the specimen confirmed metastatic carcinoma. Immunohistochemical staining was consistent with a lung primary, and the final diagnosis was rendered as non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified (NSCLC).

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive molecular profiling via next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the liver biopsy tissue.
- An actionable **ALK gene rearrangement** was detected.
- Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of **≥50%**.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be **intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb)**.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 25-year-old former smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, NOS histology. The diagnosis was established via biopsy of a liver metastasis. Staging workup confirmed a primary right lung mass with metastatic disease involving the liver and the contralateral lung. Brain MRI was negative for metastatic disease. Molecular analysis is significant for the presence of an ALK rearrangement and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%). The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
