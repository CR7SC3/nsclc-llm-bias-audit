I had the pleasure of meeting this 77-year-old individual in clinic today for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.

These symptoms gradually progressed, prompting a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a suspicious opacity in the right lung, leading to a more comprehensive diagnostic workup. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. **DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This imaging identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. Multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules were also noted, with the largest in the contralateral left lung measuring 1.5 cm, concerning for metastatic spread. The scan also demonstrated bulky mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy.

A subsequent whole-body PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes. This study confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, the contralateral lung nodules, and in multiple lymph node stations including the right supraclavicular fossa, consistent with M1 disease.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin.

As part of the standard staging workup for metastatic NSCLC, a contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was performed, which was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.
