**HPI:**
This is a 68-year-old individual with a history of smoking (former, quit over one year ago) who presents for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. Initially, this was attributed to a possible respiratory infection. However, symptoms worsened over the subsequent weeks, and the patient began to experience right-sided chest discomfort and a feeling of fullness. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider.

An initial chest radiograph was obtained which revealed a large right-sided pleural effusion. The patient was referred for further urgent evaluation, including cross-sectional imaging. The patient reports some fatigue and a decreased exercise tolerance but has maintained their weight and appetite. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of self-care, though their activities are limited by shortness of breath. Performance status is ECOG 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with intravenous contrast was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study identified a 4 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. Additionally, there was a large, loculated right-sided pleural effusion with associated nodular pleural thickening, highly suspicious for malignant pleural disease. No suspicious mediastinal or hilar adenopathy was definitively identified. The visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis were negative for metastatic disease.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) for pleural biopsy and partial drainage of the effusion. Pathologic examination of the pleural tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma. Cytology of the pleural fluid was also positive for malignant cells.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma) with malignant pleural effusion.
