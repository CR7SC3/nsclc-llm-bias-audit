**HPI:**
This is a 53-year-old individual with a history of former smoking (quit within the last year) who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild right-sided chest discomfort. These symptoms were initially attributed to a respiratory infection but did not resolve with a course of antibiotics. Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained, which revealed a right upper lobe opacity. The patient reports mild fatigue but denies fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed on [De-identified]. This study characterized a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, concerning for nodal involvement.

For definitive staging, a PET/CT scan was subsequently performed. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the mediastinal lymph nodes (stations 4R and 7). Critically, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, bones, or elsewhere to suggest distant metastases. A staging MRI of the brain with contrast was also completed and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed by Interventional Radiology on [De-identified]. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma, a subtype of non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. The results are notable for the following:
- An actionable driver mutation was detected: an uncommon sensitizing EGFR mutation.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry (IHC) was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was high at >16 mutations/megabase.
- The tumor was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 53-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (non-metastatic) squamous non-small cell lung cancer of the right lung with mediastinal nodal involvement. The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling is significant for an uncommon sensitizing EGFR mutation, high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%), and a high tumor mutational burden.
