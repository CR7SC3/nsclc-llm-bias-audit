**HPI:**
This is a 52-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately three months ago, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and progressive right-sided chest and back pain. The symptoms initially did not resolve with a course of antibiotics. Due to the persistence of symptoms and new-onset shortness of breath with exertion, the patient presented for further evaluation.

A chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass and a large pleural effusion. The patient was subsequently referred for a staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This imaging confirmed the presence of a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, a large, loculated right-sided pleural effusion with associated pleural thickening, and a destructive osseous lesion in the thoracic spine. A staging MRI of the brain was obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided thoracentesis for both diagnostic and therapeutic purposes. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. The patient reports feeling some improvement in their breathing since the fluid was drained but continues to have chest wall pain and fatigue. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. Performance status is ECOG 1; the patient is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
*   **CT Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis with contrast, [De-identified Date]:** Revealed a 5.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. Associated large right pleural effusion with nodular pleural enhancement. A 1.8 cm lytic lesion was identified in the T8 vertebral body. No evidence of metastatic disease in the liver or adrenal glands.
*   **MRI Brain with and without contrast, [De-identified Date]:** No evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.
*   **Thoracentesis and Pleural Fluid Cytology, [De-identified Date]:** Fluid analysis was positive for malignant cells. Cell block pathology confirmed adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary. Immunohistochemical stains were supportive of this diagnosis.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the pleural fluid cell block specimen with the following results:
*   **Actionable driver mutation detected:** EGFR L858R point mutation (sensitizing)
*   **Negative for other actionable alterations:** ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, ERBB2 rearrangements/mutations were not detected.
*   **PD-L1 Immunohistochemistry (IHC):** Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) <1% (Negative).
*   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 52-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup confirms metastatic disease to the pleura (malignant pleural effusion) and bone. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation and negative PD-L1 expression. The patient is treatment-naive and presents for discussion of findings and initiation of therapy.
