**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 64-year-old individual referred for initial oncology evaluation of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to their primary care provider several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough. **Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest x-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed. This imaging confirmed the presence of a 4 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also revealed prominent ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, concerning for regional metastatic disease.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT was subsequently obtained. This study demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the primary right upper lobe mass and the right-sided mediastinal lymph nodes. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

To obtain a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)-guided transbronchial needle aspiration of a right paratracheal lymph node. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung cancer primary.
