**HPI:**
This is a 57-year-old who is seen in our Thoracic Oncology clinic for an initial consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The history was obtained from the patient and through a review of outside medical records.

The patient was in a usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when a persistent, dry cough developed. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also began to experience nagging right-sided hip and lower back pain, which was initially attributed to a muscle strain. The pain gradually worsened, was present at rest, and began to interfere with sleep. This prompted a visit to an urgent care center.

A chest X-ray performed at that time was abnormal, leading to a referral for further imaging. The patient reports a 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past three months but denies fevers, chills, or night sweats. Appetite is described as fair. The patient is a former smoker, having quit approximately five years ago after a 20-pack-year history. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 1, with the patient remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, limited only by the hip discomfort.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
I have personally reviewed the patient's relevant imaging and pathology reports.

A diagnostic CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified]. This revealed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung. There was also evidence of mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes. This confirmed high FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and associated mediastinal lymph nodes. Additionally, a focus of intense FDG uptake was identified in a lytic lesion within the right iliac bone, consistent with osseous metastatic disease.

A contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was performed as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. The key findings are as follows:
-   An activating EGFR mutation, specifically an L858R point mutation in exon 21, was detected.
-   Testing was negative for other actionable driver alterations, including rearrangements in ALK and ROS1, and mutations in BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
-   Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression revealed a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 0% (negative).
-   Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 57-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT2a N2 M1b) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to bone. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation and negative PD-L1 expression. The patient is treatment-naive and has a good performance status.
