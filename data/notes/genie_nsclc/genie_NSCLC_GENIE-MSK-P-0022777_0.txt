**HPI:**
The patient is a 54-year-old individual presenting for an initial oncology consultation following a recent diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient reports experiencing a persistent cough and increasing shortness of breath over the past three months, which prompted evaluation by their primary care physician. They also note mild fatigue but deny significant weight loss or hemoptysis. Performance status is excellent, with the patient maintaining full activity and independence.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The diagnostic journey commenced with a chest X-ray, which revealed a left upper lobe mass. This was followed by a comprehensive staging workup. A subsequent CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis confirmed a primary mass measuring approximately 4.5 cm in the left upper lobe, along with evidence of significant mediastinal lymphadenopathy. A PET/CT scan demonstrated hypermetabolic activity corresponding to the primary lung lesion and the involved mediastinal lymph nodes, with no evidence of distant metastatic disease. An MRI of the brain was performed to rule out intracranial involvement, which demonstrated no evidence of brain metastases. Biopsy of the left upper lobe mass, obtained via bronchoscopy, confirmed adenocarcinoma histology. Based on these findings, the patient was diagnosed with non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, AJCC Stage IIIA (cT2b N2 M0).

**Molecular Studies:**
Molecular profiling was performed on the biopsy tissue to identify actionable genomic alterations. Results revealed an EGFR exon 19 deletion, which is a sensitizing mutation. Testing for other common driver mutations was negative, specifically ALK rearrangement, ROS1 rearrangement, BRAF V600E mutation, MET exon 14 skipping mutation, RET fusion, and NTRK fusion. PD-L1 expression status was not reported at the time of this consultation.

**Problem Summary:**
The patient is a 54-year-old individual with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, AJCC Stage IIIA. The disease is characterized by a primary left upper lobe mass with mediastinal lymph node involvement, and no evidence of distant metastases, including no brain metastases. Molecular testing has identified an EGFR exon 19 deletion. The patient is now presenting for initial oncology evaluation and discussion of treatment options.
