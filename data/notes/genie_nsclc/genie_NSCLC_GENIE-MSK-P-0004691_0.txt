**HPI:**
This is a 66-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks ago, when they developed a persistent, dull ache in the right posterior ribs that was progressive and not relieved by over-the-counter analgesics. This was accompanied by a mild, non-productive cough and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds over the last three months. Due to the persistent pain, the patient presented to their primary care provider, who ordered initial imaging. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. Performance status is excellent; the patient is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though activities are limited by the rib pain.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe lung mass and a suspicious lytic lesion in a posterior rib. This prompted a full staging workup.

A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis performed on [De-identified] confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, associated with bulky mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also characterized a destructive lesion in the right seventh rib, consistent with a bone metastasis. No other distant visceral metastases were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or elsewhere in the abdomen/pelvis.

A subsequent PET/CT scan on [De-identified] demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, the mediastinal lymph nodes, and the solitary bone lesion in the right seventh rib. No other sites of FDG-avid metastatic disease were noted.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was performed on [De-identified] and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathology review confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) performed on the tumor tissue revealed the following:
- Actionable driver mutations detected: EGFR L858R point mutation and high-level MET amplification.
- The tumor was negative for alterations in: ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14 skipping mutations, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression was not tested.
- Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be low (<2 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 66-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (AJCC 8th Edition) squamous non-small cell lung cancer, metastatic to bone. Molecular profiling is notable for a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation and concurrent high-level MET amplification. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss the implications of these findings.
