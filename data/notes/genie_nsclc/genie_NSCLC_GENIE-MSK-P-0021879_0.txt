**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 61-year-old individual with a history of former tobacco use who is seen today for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they began to experience a persistent, dry cough and progressive shortness of breath with exertion. These symptoms were initially attributed to a benign cause, but due to their persistence and the subsequent development of fatigue and an unintentional 15-pound weight loss, the patient sought medical evaluation.

An initial chest X-ray ordered by their primary care provider demonstrated a right-sided lung mass. This prompted a referral for further staging studies. A diagnostic CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed, which identified a 4 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated bulky mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Critically, the scan also revealed a 1.2 cm nodule in the contralateral left upper lobe, concerning for metastatic disease. There was no evidence of metastatic involvement of the liver, adrenal glands, or other abdominopelvic organs.

To complete the initial staging workup, a whole-body PET/CT scan was obtained. This study confirmed intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the primary right upper lobe mass, multiple mediastinal lymph nodes, and the contralateral left lung nodule, consistent with metastatic disease. A subsequent MRI of the brain was performed to evaluate for central nervous system involvement and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe mass. Pathologic review of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. The patient has a remote 35 pack-year smoking history but quit over 10 years ago. They are currently active and independent with all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1.
