**HPI:**
This is a 59-year-old with a history of smoking (quit <1 year ago) who is seen for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to an outside facility several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, prompting further investigation. The patient is otherwise active and independent with all activities of daily living, with an estimated ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This imaging demonstrated a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe measuring approximately 5 cm, with associated bulky mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also identified a suspicious 1.5 cm nodule in the left lower lobe, concerning for contralateral metastatic disease.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was obtained for staging, which confirmed high FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe mass, the mediastinal lymph nodes, and the contralateral left lower lobe nodule. No other distant sites of FDG-avid disease were identified in the neck, abdomen, or pelvis.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and fine-needle aspiration (FNA) of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathology from this specimen confirmed poorly differentiated non-small cell carcinoma with features consistent with squamous cell histology.

A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastasis.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IV disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the biopsy specimen. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specific testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was negative (<1% tumor proportion score).

Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 59-year-old, treatment-naive former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer of squamous cell histology, with metastatic disease to the contralateral lung. The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of an actionable driver mutation, negative PD-L1 expression, and an intermediate tumor mutational burden. The case was discussed in detail.
