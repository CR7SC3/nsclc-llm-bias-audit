**HPI:**
This is a 57-year-old individual with a significant history of tobacco use who is referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside facility approximately three weeks ago with a chief complaint of progressive shortness of breath on exertion and a persistent, dry cough. These symptoms had been worsening over the prior two months. An initial chest radiograph was abnormal, prompting a more detailed diagnostic evaluation. The patient reports some pleuritic chest discomfort but denies hemoptysis, fevers, or significant weight loss. Performance status is good, estimated at ECOG 1, with limitations only in strenuous physical activity due to dyspnea.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified]. This imaging revealed a 4.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated extensive right-sided pleural thickening and nodularity, along with a moderate-sized, complex pleural effusion, findings highly concerning for pleural metastatic disease. There was no evidence of metastatic disease in the liver, adrenal glands, or other visceral organs.

A subsequent PET/CT scan on [De-identified] confirmed high FDG avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass and in the extensive right-sided pleural deposits. No other sites of distant FDG-avid disease were identified. A staging MRI of the brain performed on [De-identified] was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic examination confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma. Concurrently, a thoracentesis was performed for both diagnostic and therapeutic purposes, with removal of 1.2 liters of serosanguinous fluid. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, consistent with metastatic adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the core biopsy was sent for comprehensive genomic and biomarker profiling. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed, and results are as follows:
-   No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
-   Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
-   Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be high at >16 mutations/megabase.
-   A loss-of-function mutation in the KEAP1 gene was also detected on the sequencing panel.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 57-year-old current smoker who presents for initial evaluation of a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, Stage IV (cT_N_M1a) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The disease is metastatic to the pleura, confirmed by imaging and positive pleural fluid cytology. Staging workup is negative for brain or other distant visceral metastases. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and high TMB, without an identifiable targetable driver mutation. A KEAP1 loss-of-function mutation is also present. The patient is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
