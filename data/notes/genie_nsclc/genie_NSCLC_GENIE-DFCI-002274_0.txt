**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 60-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker who quit over a year ago and was in a usual state of good health until approximately three months prior to presentation. At that time, the patient developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially treated as bronchitis without resolution. Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a mass in the right upper lobe. The patient reports some mild fatigue but denies fever, chills, night sweats, hemoptysis, or significant weight loss. The patient maintains a good performance status, ECOG 1, and continues with activities of daily living without major limitation. Following the abnormal chest X-ray, the patient was referred for further diagnostic evaluation.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which confirmed the presence of a 6.1 cm spiculated mass in the apical segment of the right upper lobe. The mass was noted to abut the parietal pleura without definitive evidence of chest wall invasion. No mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy was identified. There was no evidence of distant visceral or osseous metastatic disease.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was performed. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the known right upper lobe primary tumor (SUVmax 12.8). No FDG-avid hilar, mediastinal, or supraclavicular lymphadenopathy was seen. No evidence of FDG-avid distant metastatic disease was identified.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was performed for central nervous system staging and was negative for metastatic disease.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass was subsequently performed. Pathologic analysis of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2-16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 60-year-old with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive, clinical Stage IIB (cT3N0M0) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology, with no actionable driver mutations identified on molecular profiling.
