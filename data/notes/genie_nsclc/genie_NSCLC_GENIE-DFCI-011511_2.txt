**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for an 80-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive right hip pain. The pain was initially managed with over-the-counter analgesics but worsened, limiting mobility. The patient has a remote history of prostate cancer, managed with local therapy many years ago and with no evidence of recurrence. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over a decade ago. Due to the constellation of symptoms, a diagnostic workup was initiated by their primary care provider. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care. Performance status is estimated at ECOG 1; the patient is ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in strenuous activity due to hip discomfort.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a suspicious mass in the right lung. This was followed by a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis which confirmed a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also identified a destructive lytic lesion in the right iliac bone, suspicious for metastasis.

A subsequent whole-body PET/CT scan was performed for staging, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe lung mass, multiple mediastinal lymph nodes, and the previously identified lytic lesion in the right iliac bone. No other distant sites of FDG-avid disease were identified. A contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed on [De-identified]. Pathology confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified (nos).

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested as part of this panel.
