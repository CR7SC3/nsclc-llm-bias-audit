**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 69-year-old individual referred for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to their primary care provider several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was associated with mild fatigue and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the past two months. The individual is a current smoker. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further diagnostic evaluation. The patient is now seen in our clinic for comprehensive evaluation and discussion of management. The patient has a good performance status, ambulatory and capable of self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date], which confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated extensive mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, including bulky right paratracheal and subcarinal nodes.

A subsequent PET/CT scan on [De-identified Date] demonstrated high metabolic activity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in multiple nodal stations, including right hilar, subcarinal, and contralateral mediastinal lymph nodes. No evidence of distant metastatic disease was identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis. A dedicated MRI of the brain was performed for staging and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and fine-needle aspiration (FNA) of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathologic examination of the specimen confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

Based on the combination of a primary lung tumor with contralateral mediastinal lymph node involvement (N3) and no evidence of distant metastases (M0), the diagnosis is Stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, per AJCC 8th edition staging.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the EBUS-FNA. No actionable driver alteration was identified; results were negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be low at <2 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 69-year-old current smoker who is treatment-naive, presenting with a new diagnosis of unresectable, locally advanced Stage IIIB non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) with no identified actionable driver mutations. The patient is here today for initial oncologic consultation to review these findings.
