**HPI:**
This is a 53-year-old individual with a history of being a lifetime never-smoker who is seen today for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and a new sensation of mild shortness of breath with strenuous activity. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider. The patient denies fevers, chills, hemoptysis, or significant weight loss. They remain fully active, performing all routine daily activities without limitation and maintaining an excellent performance status (ECOG 0).

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest radiograph revealed a right upper lobe opacity, leading to a referral for further imaging. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study confirmed the presence of a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated extensive ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy, involving the right paratracheal (station 4R) and subcarinal nodal stations, with the largest conglomerate of nodes measuring approximately 3 cm. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or other visceral organs.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT scan was subsequently performed. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the right hilar and mediastinal lymph nodes, consistent with the CT findings. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease below the diaphragm or elsewhere to suggest distant metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also completed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

Following the imaging workup, the patient underwent a bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and transbronchial needle aspiration of a right paratracheal lymph node. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive next-generation sequencing was performed on the tissue obtained from the EBUS-guided biopsy. No actionable driver alterations were identified. Specifically, the tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was low at <2 mutations/megabase. PD-L1 expression was not tested, as this was not standard practice at the time of tissue acquisition.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 53-year-old lifetime never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The disease is localized to the right hemithorax with extensive mediastinal nodal involvement but no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling of the tumor is negative for any identified oncogenic drivers. The patient is treatment-naive and presents with an excellent performance status for consideration of therapy.
