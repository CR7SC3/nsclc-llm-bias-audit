**HPI:**
This is an 80-year-old individual with a history of being a lifetime never-smoker, who is referred for initial oncology consultation to discuss a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and a new onset of mild dyspnea on exertion. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider. The patient denies hemoptysis, chest pain, or significant weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and independent with all activities of daily living, though they note some increased fatigue.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, which led to a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis for further characterization and staging. The CT scan identified a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated a solitary 1.2 cm nodule in the contralateral left lower lobe, highly suspicious for metastatic disease. Several mediastinal lymph nodes were noted to be enlarged. No metastatic lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones. A subsequent MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and showed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe mass on [De-identified date]. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the biopsy specimen was sent for comprehensive next-generation sequencing. The results of this molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mutations/megabase). PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on the specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is an 80-year-old, treatment-naive individual with an excellent performance status presenting with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with a single site of distant metastatic disease in the contralateral lung. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of any identified targetable driver mutations and an intermediate tumor mutational burden. The patient is here today for an initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and its implications.
