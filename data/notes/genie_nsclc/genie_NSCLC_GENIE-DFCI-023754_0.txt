**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 62-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation to discuss a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a history of a former smoking habit, having quit over one year ago.

The diagnosis was prompted by an incidental finding. The patient underwent a screening chest X-ray which revealed a small, spiculated nodule in the right lung periphery. The patient was asymptomatic at the time and denies any cough, dyspnea, hemoptysis, chest pain, or constitutional symptoms such as fever, chills, or unintentional weight loss. Performance status is excellent; the patient maintains a fully active lifestyle without restriction (ECOG 0).

Following the abnormal chest X-ray, the patient was referred for further evaluation.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed, which confirmed the presence of a 1.8 cm solid, spiculated nodule in the apical segment of the right upper lobe. There was no evidence of hilar or mediastinal lymphadenopathy.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes. This demonstrated focal, intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the right upper lobe nodule. No other sites of abnormal hypermetabolism were identified in the chest or visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis to suggest nodal or distant metastatic disease.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was completed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung nodule. Pathology from this procedure confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin.

Based on this comprehensive workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IA (cT1b N0 M0) non-small cell lung cancer.
