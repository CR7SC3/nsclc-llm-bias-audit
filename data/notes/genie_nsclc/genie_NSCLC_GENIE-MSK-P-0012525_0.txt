**HPI:**
This is a 42-year-old individual with a limited past medical history who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks ago when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and nagging mid-back pain, which prompted a visit to their primary care provider. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. Initial evaluation with a chest X-ray revealed a suspicious opacity in the right lung, leading to further diagnostic imaging. The patient reports mild fatigue but is otherwise fully ambulatory and managing all activities of daily living. They deny fever, chills, hemoptysis, or significant weight loss. The patient is here today to understand the diagnosis and discuss the situation.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This study identified a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. Multiple small nodules were also noted in the contralateral left lung, the largest measuring 1.2 cm, suspicious for metastatic spread. The scan also revealed a lytic lesion within the T8 vertebral body, concerning for osseous metastasis.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe mass. The pathology report confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, moderately differentiated.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT was performed. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, the contralateral lung nodules, and the previously noted T8 vertebral body lesion, confirming metastatic disease. No other sites of FDG-avid disease were identified in the neck, abdomen, or pelvis.

Given the diagnosis of metastatic NSCLC, a staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was obtained, which was negative for intracranial metastases.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy. Key findings are as follows:
- **Actionable driver mutation:** A ROS1 gene rearrangement was detected.
- **Negative for other drivers:** Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Low (<2 mutations/megabase).
- **PD-L1 Expression:** Testing for PD-L1 was not performed.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 42-year-old never-smoker presenting with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT2a N0 M1b) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnostic workup confirms metastatic disease involving the contralateral lung and bone. Molecular analysis of the tumor is significant for the presence of a ROS1 rearrangement.
