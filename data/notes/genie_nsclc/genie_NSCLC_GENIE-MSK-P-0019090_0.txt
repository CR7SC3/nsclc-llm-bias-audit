**HPI:**
This is an initial medical oncology consultation for a 66-year-old individual referred for management of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to their primary care provider approximately six weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, prompting further investigation. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago, and is otherwise active with a good performance status.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date], which confirmed the presence of a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was also evidence of ipsilateral hilar lymphadenopathy. No suspicious lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or other visualized abdominopelvic organs.

A subsequent PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in adjacent hilar lymph nodes, consistent with the chest CT findings. Importantly, the PET/CT did not show any evidence of distant metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and transbronchial needle aspiration of the primary mass. Pathologic analysis of the tissue specimen confirmed the diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma.

Based on this workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IIB (T2aN1M0) non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive molecular profiling was performed on the biopsy specimen.
-   Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
-   Next-generation sequencing did not identify any actionable driver alterations. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
-   Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 66-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, non-metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient maintains an excellent performance status and is fully ambulatory and independent.

The key diagnostic findings are:
1.  **Diagnosis:** Stage IIB non-small cell lung cancer, squamous cell carcinoma histology.
2.  **Staging:** Disease is localized to the right lung and regional lymph nodes, with no evidence of distant metastatic disease based on PET/CT and brain MRI.
3.  **Biomarkers:** PD-L1 positive (TPS ≥50%). No actionable driver mutations were identified on molecular profiling.
