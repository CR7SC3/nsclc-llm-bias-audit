**HPI:**
This is a 56-year-old individual with a significant smoking history, referred for an initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside hospital emergency department approximately three weeks ago with complaints of persistent headaches, intermittent dizziness, and a sense of cognitive fogginess that had been progressing over several weeks. An initial workup was initiated at that time. The patient maintains a good performance status, is fully ambulatory, and is capable of all self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Given the presenting neurological symptoms, a contrasted MRI of the brain was performed. This study revealed several enhancing cerebral lesions, highly suspicious for metastatic disease.

Subsequent staging evaluation with a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was completed to identify a primary malignancy. This scan demonstrated a spiculated 3.5 cm mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. No other sites of distant metastatic disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma. The overall findings are consistent with AJCC Stage IV disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor biopsy specimen. Molecular analysis identified an actionable KRAS G12C mutation. Further testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be high at >16 mut/Mb. PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 56-year-old current smoker presenting with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to the brain. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a KRAS G12C mutation and a high tumor mutational burden. The patient is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis in detail.
