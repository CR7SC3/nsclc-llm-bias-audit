**HPI:**
This is a 59-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit within the past year.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dull ache in the right hip and lower back, which progressively worsened and became refractory to over-the-counter analgesics. Over a similar timeframe, the patient also noted the appearance of a new, firm, non-tender palpable nodule on the skin of their anterior chest. Due to the intractable pain, the patient presented to an outside emergency department for evaluation.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Initial workup included plain films of the pelvis, which demonstrated a suspicious lytic lesion in the right iliac crest. This prompted a staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast, performed on [De-identified date]. This study revealed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, with associated bulky mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan confirmed multiple osseous metastatic lesions, including the right iliac lesion, as well as additional lytic deposits in the thoracic spine and ribs. A 2 cm enhancing subcutaneous nodule was also visualized in the left anterior chest wall, corresponding to the patient's palpable finding. A subsequent MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent an ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy of the accessible left chest wall subcutaneous nodule. Pathology from this procedure confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified (NSCLC, nos).

**Molecular Studies:**
The biopsy specimen was sent for comprehensive molecular profiling via next-generation sequencing. No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was performed and was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 1–49%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 59-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified histology. Staging is consistent with AJCC Stage IV disease, with metastatic involvement of bone and subcutaneous tissue. Brain imaging is negative for central nervous system involvement. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations and a PD-L1 TPS of 1-49%. The patient is treatment-naive and presents today to discuss the diagnosis and management options.
