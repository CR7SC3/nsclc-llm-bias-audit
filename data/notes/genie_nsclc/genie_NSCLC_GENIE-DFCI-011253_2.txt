Initial evaluation with a chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further investigation.

A diagnostic CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study characterized a 4.8 cm spiculated mass in the apical segment of the right upper lobe. No suspicious lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or other visualized intra-abdominal organs.

For definitive staging, a PET/CT was subsequently performed on [De-identified Date]. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the right hilar and paratracheal nodal stations. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases. A staging MRI of the brain was also obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma. Based on the integrated clinical and radiologic findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IIB disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alteration was identified; testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested, as this was not standard of care at the time the sequencing was performed. The tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
