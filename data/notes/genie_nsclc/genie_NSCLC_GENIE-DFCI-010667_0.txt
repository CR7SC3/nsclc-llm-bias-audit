**HPI:**
This is a 56-year-old individual with a recent history of tobacco use (quit <1 year ago) who is referred for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to an outside provider several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and intermittent right-sided chest wall discomfort. These symptoms had been progressive over the prior two months. The patient denies significant weight loss, hemoptysis, or fevers. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, which prompted further evaluation. The patient is otherwise active and independent with an excellent performance status.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which demonstrated a large, spiculated 5.2 cm mass in the right upper lobe. This was associated with extensive, bulky mediastinal and right hilar lymphadenopathy, with a conglomerate of subcarinal nodes measuring up to 3.5 cm. There were no suspicious lesions identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was obtained. This confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and the extensive mediastinal nodal stations, consistent with the CT findings. No FDG-avid lesions suggestive of distant metastatic disease were identified. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was also performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed by Interventional Radiology on [De-identified Date]. Pathologic review of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
The biopsy specimen was sent for comprehensive molecular profiling. Next-generation sequencing did not identify any actionable driver alterations; results were negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was performed, showing a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of <1%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) analysis was also completed and was found to be high (>16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 56-year-old former smoker presenting for initial evaluation of a newly diagnosed, treatment-naive, Stage IIIB (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup confirms locally advanced disease with extensive mediastinal lymphadenopathy and no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations, a PD-L1 TPS of <1%, and a high Tumor Mutational Burden. The patient has an excellent performance status and is here to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
