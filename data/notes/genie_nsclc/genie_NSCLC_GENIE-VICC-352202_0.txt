**HPI:**
This is a 56-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of excellent health until approximately six weeks prior to this evaluation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This prompted a visit to an urgent care center, where a chest X-ray revealed a large left-sided pleural effusion.

The patient was subsequently referred to a pulmonologist for further evaluation. A CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which identified a primary lung mass in the left lower lobe as well as extensive pleural nodularity and a large, complex pleural effusion. The patient reports being a lifetime never-smoker. They remain fully ambulatory and independent with activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis on [De-identified] demonstrated a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the left lower lobe. The study also revealed extensive left-sided pleural thickening and nodularity, with an associated large, loculated pleural effusion. There was no evidence of mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy. The liver, adrenal glands, and visualized bones were free of metastatic lesions. These findings are consistent with M1a disease.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed to complete staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent a video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) for a pleural biopsy and drainage of the effusion with talc pleurodesis. Pathologic analysis of the pleural tissue confirmed the diagnosis.

**Molecular Studies:**
Pathology from the pleural biopsy confirmed poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue with the following results:
- **Actionable Mutations:** EGFR exon 19 deletion (sensitizing) detected.
- **Negative for:** ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, ERBB2.
- **PD-L1 Expression:** Not tested.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 56-year-old never-smoker with an excellent performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma) with pleural metastases, notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion. The patient is treatment-naive and is here to discuss the diagnosis and management options.
