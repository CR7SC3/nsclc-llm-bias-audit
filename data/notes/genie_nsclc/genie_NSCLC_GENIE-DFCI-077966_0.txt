**HPI:**
This is a 61-year-old individual who presents for an initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and was in a usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to diagnosis, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. Initial evaluation with a primary care provider led to a course of antibiotics without improvement. Subsequent chest X-ray showed a right-sided lung mass and a pleural effusion, prompting an urgent referral for further evaluation. The patient reports some mild constitutional symptoms including fatigue but has maintained their weight and has a good performance status, remaining fully ambulatory and capable of self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A diagnostic workup was initiated following the abnormal chest radiograph. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified]. This study confirmed the presence of a large primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The imaging also revealed a moderate right-sided malignant pleural effusion, multiple suspicious osseous lesions consistent with bone metastases, and enlarged contralateral supraclavicular lymph nodes.

Given the imaging findings, a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed. Pathology from this specimen confirmed a diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma.

Due to the high risk of central nervous system involvement, a brain MRI with and without contrast was obtained. This study demonstrated multiple small parenchymal lesions consistent with brain metastases.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy.
- An actionable sensitizing mutation was detected: EGFR exon 19 deletion.
- Testing was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression showed a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 61-year-old never-smoker presenting with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, squamous cell histology. The diagnostic workup has confirmed widespread metastatic disease involving the brain, bone, pleura, and distant lymph nodes. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion, with PD-L1 expression of 1-49%. The patient is treatment-naive and presents for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and management options.
