**HPI:**
This is an initial consultation for a 75-year-old patient recently diagnosed with non-small cell lung cancer. The patient first sought medical attention several weeks ago due to a persistent cough and gradually increasing shortness of breath, which had been present for approximately three months. There were no other significant constitutional symptoms such as unexplained weight loss, fevers, or night sweats. An initial chest X-ray performed by their primary care physician revealed an abnormality in the left lung, prompting further imaging with a CT scan of the chest. This CT scan identified a solid lung mass and suggested involvement of ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes, but no clear evidence of distant metastatic disease was observed. The patient reports maintaining good energy levels and independence with all activities of daily living, with an estimated ECOG performance status of 0-1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive staging workup was subsequently completed. A whole-body PET/CT scan confirmed increased metabolic activity within the primary left lung mass and in several mediastinal lymph nodes, consistent with locoregional disease. Crucially, the PET/CT demonstrated no evidence of hypermetabolic lesions elsewhere in the body that would suggest distant metastatic spread. To thoroughly assess for intracranial disease, a brain MRI with contrast was also performed, which revealed no evidence of brain metastases. The diagnosis was histologically confirmed by a CT-guided core biopsy of the primary lung lesion, which yielded adenocarcinoma. Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, AJCC Stage IIA.

**Molecular Studies:**
Molecular profiling of the tumor tissue was conducted. Results from next-generation sequencing detected an ALK rearrangement. Further testing for other common oncogenic drivers was negative, including EGFR mutations, ROS1 rearrangement, BRAF mutations, MET exon 14 skipping alterations, RET fusions, and NTRK fusions. PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was not reported.

**Problem Summary:**
This 75-year-old patient presents for initial oncology evaluation with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging has confirmed AJCC Stage IIA disease, with no evidence of distant metastases, including no brain metastases. Tumor molecular profiling revealed the presence of an ALK rearrangement. The patient maintains a good performance status.
