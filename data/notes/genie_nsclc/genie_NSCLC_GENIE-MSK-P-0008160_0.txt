**HPI:**
This 63-year-old individual with a history of smoking (former, quit over one year ago) presents for initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately six weeks ago, when they began to experience progressive shortness of breath with exertion and a persistent dry cough. These symptoms prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider. An initial chest radiograph demonstrated a right lung mass and a large pleural effusion, leading to an urgent referral for further evaluation.

The patient reports ongoing dyspnea, which limits strenuous activity, but denies chest pain or hemoptysis. They note mild fatigue but have had no significant unintentional weight loss, fevers, or night sweats. Their appetite remains stable. The patient is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified date]. This study confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also revealed a large, complex right-sided pleural effusion with associated nodular pleural thickening, highly suspicious for malignant involvement. No suspicious mediastinal or hilar adenopathy was definitively identified. The visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis, including the liver, adrenal glands, and bones, were negative for evidence of metastatic disease.

A staging MRI of the brain was performed and showed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified date], the patient underwent a thoracentesis for both diagnostic and therapeutic purposes. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells. The final pathology confirmed adenocarcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor cell block from the pleural fluid specimen. Comprehensive genomic profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations. Specifically, the tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be in the intermediate range (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested, consistent with the standard of care at the time of sequencing (pre-2017).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 63-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT_x_ N_x_ M1a) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnosis is established by a primary right lung mass with metastatic disease to the pleura, confirmed by a malignant pleural effusion. Staging studies are negative for distant metastases to the brain or other visceral sites. Molecular profiling of the tumor did not identify a targetable oncogenic driver.
