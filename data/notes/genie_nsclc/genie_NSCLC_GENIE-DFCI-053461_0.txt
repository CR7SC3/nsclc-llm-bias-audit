**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 43-year-old individual with an excellent baseline performance status who presents for an initial oncology evaluation following a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially managed as a respiratory infection without resolution. Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a right-sided lung opacity. This prompted a follow-up contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest.

The patient reports feeling well otherwise, with no fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. They maintain full activity without limitation. Energy levels are good. There is no hemoptysis, chest pain, or dyspnea at rest.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive staging evaluation was pursued following the initial imaging findings.

**Imaging:**
- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis:** Confirmed a 4.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was associated prominent ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy, concerning for nodal involvement. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or other visceral organs.
- **PET/CT:** Performed for staging, this demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the right-sided mediastinal and subcarinal lymph nodes, consistent with the CT findings. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases.
- **MRI Brain:** Performed for central nervous system staging, this was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

**Pathology:**
- **Bronchoscopy with EBUS-FNA:** The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound-guided biopsy of the right upper lobe mass and sampling of a subcarinal (station 7) lymph node.
- **Final Diagnosis:** Pathology from both sites was consistent with squamous cell carcinoma, non-small cell type.
