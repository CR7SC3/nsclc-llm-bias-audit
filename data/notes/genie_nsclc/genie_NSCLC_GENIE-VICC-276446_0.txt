**HPI:**
This is a 79-year-old, previously in a good state of health, who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

The patient reports approximately three months of progressive dyspnea on exertion and a mild, non-productive cough. These symptoms initially were attributed to age but worsened to the point of limiting routine activities. This prompted a visit to a primary care provider, who ordered a chest X-ray. The imaging revealed a right-sided lung opacity and a significant pleural effusion.

The patient was subsequently referred for further evaluation. A CT scan of the chest confirmed a primary lung mass and a large pleural effusion. To establish a diagnosis and alleviate symptoms, the patient underwent a right-sided thoracentesis. The patient reports some improvement in breathing following the procedure. The patient's performance status is ECOG 1; symptomatic but fully ambulatory and capable of light activity. Review of systems is otherwise negative for fevers, chills, night sweats, significant weight loss, or neurological changes such as headache or focal weakness.
