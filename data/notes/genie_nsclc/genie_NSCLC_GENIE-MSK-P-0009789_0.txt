**HPI:**
This is a 71-year-old individual with a history of tobacco use (former smoker, quit >1 year ago) who is referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was associated with mild fatigue and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds. The patient initially sought evaluation with their primary care provider, which led to outpatient imaging. A chest CT scan revealed a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, along with mediastinal lymphadenopathy and a suspicious nodule in the contralateral lung. The patient was subsequently referred for further diagnostic workup.

The patient is currently feeling reasonably well, with the cough persisting but manageable. Appetite is fair. The patient remains fully ambulatory and independent with all activities of daily living, with an estimated ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A PET/CT scan was performed on [De-identified Date] for staging. This demonstrated a hypermetabolic 4.2 cm primary mass in the right upper lobe. There was associated hypermetabolic mediastinal lymphadenopathy. A distinct 1.5 cm hypermetabolic nodule was identified in the left lower lobe, consistent with M1 disease. No other sites of distant metastatic disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, with features of adenocarcinoma histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry were performed on the biopsy specimen.
-   **Driver Mutations:** An actionable KRAS G12C mutation was detected. Testing was negative for alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2.
-   **PD-L1 Expression:** Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 1–49%.
-   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 71-year-old former smoker presenting with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to the contralateral lung. The diagnosis is confirmed by biopsy. Molecular testing is notable for the presence of a KRAS G12C mutation and PD-L1 expression with a TPS of 1-49%. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis.
