**HPI:**
This is a 69-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit over one year ago) who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially sought medical attention several weeks ago for a persistent, non-productive cough. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided pulmonary opacity, prompting a follow-up CT scan of the chest. The patient reports mild fatigue but is otherwise active and maintaining their weight and appetite. They deny chest pain, hemoptysis, or significant dyspnea. The patient's ECOG performance status is 1. They are here today to discuss the completed diagnostic workup and establish care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which identified a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also revealed bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy, with the largest node measuring 2.8 cm in short axis. No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, or osseous lesions were identified.

A subsequent whole-body PET/CT scan was performed for staging. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the mediastinal and subcarinal lymph nodes. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) of the primary mass and a station 7 (subcarinal) lymph node. Pathology from both sites confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

Based on the T2b N2 M0 classification, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the biopsy specimen was sent for next-generation sequencing. Molecular analysis identified a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion. Further testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 69-year-old, treatment-naive patient with a good performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (T2b N2 M0) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The disease is locoregionally advanced, confined to the right hemithorax, with no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion. The patient is seen in consultation today to review these findings.
