**REASON FOR VISIT:** Initial Oncology Consultation
**CHIEF COMPLAINT:** Newly diagnosed Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer

**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 66-year-old with a recent diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presents for initial oncologic evaluation and discussion.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive fatigue. Over the subsequent weeks, they also noted the onset of new, persistent mid-back pain, which was dull and aching in character. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider.

Initial workup included a chest X-ray which revealed a suspicious left-sided lung mass. A subsequent CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed for staging. This confirmed a large primary mass in the left upper lobe with mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also revealed multiple suspicious lesions throughout the liver. Due to the persistent back pain, a bone scan was performed which showed abnormal radiotracer uptake in the thoracic spine and right iliac crest. An MRI of the brain was also obtained as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient has a history of tobacco use, having quit within the last year. They are currently functional with an ECOG performance status of 1, able to perform all self-care but restricted in physically strenuous activity by fatigue and back pain.

- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis with contrast on [De-identified date]:** Revealed a 4.8 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe with associated mediastinal adenopathy. Multiple hypoenhancing lesions were identified throughout the liver, consistent with metastatic disease.
- **Bone Scan on [De-identified date]:** Showed multiple foci of increased uptake in the thoracic spine and the right iliac crest, highly suspicious for osseous metastases.
- **MRI Brain with and without contrast on [De-identified date]:** No evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.
- **CT-guided core biopsy of the left upper lobe lung mass on [De-identified date]:** Pathology confirmed invasive, moderately differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung primary.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor biopsy specimen.
- **Actionable driver mutation:** KRAS G12C mutation detected.
- **Negative for other drivers:** No pathogenic variants were identified in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, or ERBB2.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- **PD-L1 Immunohistochemistry:** Not performed, as this was not standard practice at the time of initial tissue sequencing.

**PROBLEM SUMMARY:**
This is a 66-year-old former smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastases to bone and liver. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a KRAS G12C mutation. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation.
