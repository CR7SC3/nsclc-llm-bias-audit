**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 59-year-old individual with an excellent performance status (ECOG 0) who is referred for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately one month prior to presentation when they developed a mild, persistent dry cough. This did not resolve with conservative measures, prompting a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest radiograph was obtained which demonstrated a suspicious opacity in the right upper lung field. The patient denies any associated hemoptysis, dyspnea, chest pain, fevers, or unintentional weight loss. They remain fully active and without constitutional symptoms.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed. This confirmed the presence of a 3.8 cm spiculated solid mass in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe. No significant mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy was identified.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT scan was subsequently obtained. This study demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the known right upper lobe primary tumor. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in regional lymph nodes or at any distant sites.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed as part of the standard staging evaluation and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass on [De-identified]. Histopathologic review of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy. Molecular analysis identified an actionable driver mutation, specifically an EGFR exon 19 deletion.

Further testing was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**PROBLEM SUMMARY:**
In summary, this is a 59-year-old lifetime never-smoker who presents for initial oncology evaluation of a newly diagnosed, treatment-naive, Stage IIA (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnosis was confirmed via biopsy of a right upper lobe mass. Comprehensive staging evaluation, including PET/CT and brain MRI, has shown no evidence of regional or distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion. The patient is asymptomatic with an excellent performance status.
