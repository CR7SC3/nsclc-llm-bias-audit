**HPI:**
This is a 56-year-old individual with a recent diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, presenting for initial oncology consultation. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially managed with over-the-counter remedies without relief, which prompted a visit to their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, leading to a follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis for further characterization. This imaging confirmed a primary lung mass and also identified a suspicious lesion in the left adrenal gland. To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was performed, which demonstrated high FDG-avidity in the lung mass, mediastinal lymph nodes, and the left adrenal mass, consistent with metastatic disease. A dedicated brain MRI was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient remains in good functional status with an ECOG performance status of 1, primarily limited by mild fatigue and the persistent cough. They report a stable appetite and deny fevers, night sweats, or significant unintentional weight loss. They are a lifetime never-smoker.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 56-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), not otherwise specified. Staging studies confirm metastatic disease limited to a single site in the left adrenal gland. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a high tumor mutational burden and is negative for all tested targetable driver alterations.
