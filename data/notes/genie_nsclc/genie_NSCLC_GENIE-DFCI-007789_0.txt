**HPI:**
This is a 60-year-old individual referred for initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside emergency department approximately three weeks ago with a two-month history of persistent, non-productive cough, progressive fatigue, and new-onset headaches. The patient also reports an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds over the past three months. Past medical history is notable for a long-term smoking history, having quit less than one year ago. The patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity, with an estimated ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray performed in the emergency department was suspicious for a right-sided lung mass, prompting further investigation.

A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed for staging. This study identified a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also revealed multiple hypoattenuating lesions in the liver, the largest measuring 2.8 cm in segment VII, and a 2.1 cm left adrenal nodule, all highly suspicious for metastatic disease.

Given the presenting neurological symptoms, a contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was obtained. This revealed several small, ring-enhancing intraparenchymal lesions in the bilateral cerebral hemispheres, consistent with metastatic deposits.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe lung mass. Pathology review confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the lung biopsy. No actionable driver alteration was identified; results were negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested, as this was not standard of care at the time of tissue acquisition. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was found to be high at >16 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 60-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup has confirmed metastatic disease involving the brain, liver, and left adrenal gland. Molecular profiling of the tumor is negative for common actionable driver mutations but demonstrates a high tumor mutational burden. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis in detail and establish care.
