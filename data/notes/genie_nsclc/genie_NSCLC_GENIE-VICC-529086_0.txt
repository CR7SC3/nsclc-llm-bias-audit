**HPI:**
The patient is a 58-year-old individual presenting for initial oncology consultation regarding a recent diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient first noted a persistent, dry cough approximately three months ago, which gradually progressed and was accompanied by mild exertional dyspnea and a general sense of fatigue. Over the past several weeks, the patient also reported a modest, unintentional weight loss of approximately 8 pounds. These symptoms prompted evaluation by their primary care physician, leading to further diagnostic workup. The patient reports a good functional status, able to perform all usual activities without significant limitation, with an estimated ECOG Performance Status of 0-1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Initial evaluation included a chest X-ray which revealed a suspicious mass in the upper lobe of the right lung. This was followed by a contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, which confirmed a primary pulmonary mass measuring approximately 4.5 cm in the right upper lobe. The CT also demonstrated ipsilateral hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy, with several enlarged lymph nodes measuring up to 2.0 cm in the right hilar and subcarinal stations. Importantly, there was no evidence of distant metastatic disease identified in the abdomen (e.g., liver, adrenal glands) or other visualized sites. A subsequent PET/CT scan corroborated these findings, showing hypermetabolic activity corresponding to the primary lung lesion and the involved regional lymph nodes, without any other metabolically active foci suggestive of distant spread. Biopsy of the lung mass, performed via bronchoscopy with transbronchial biopsy, confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. Given the extent of disease, a dedicated MRI of the brain was performed, which showed no evidence of intracranial metastatic disease. Based on this comprehensive staging workup, the patient's disease is classified as AJCC Stage IIB.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive molecular profiling was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alterations were identified. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14 skipping, RET, and NTRK fusions. PD-L1 expression was not reported at the time of this consultation.

**Problem Summary:**
This 58-year-old patient is newly diagnosed with treatment-naive non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, AJCC Stage IIB. The disease involves a primary lung lesion with regional lymph node involvement, and there is no evidence of distant metastatic disease, including no brain metastases. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations.
