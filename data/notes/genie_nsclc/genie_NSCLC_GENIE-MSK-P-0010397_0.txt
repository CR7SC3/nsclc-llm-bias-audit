**HPI:**
This is a 72-year-old with a history of recently discontinued tobacco use who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation to discuss a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they began to experience a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive shortness of breath with exertion. These symptoms were initially managed as a possible respiratory infection without improvement. Over the past month, the patient noted worsening dyspnea, particularly when lying flat, and the development of left-sided pleuritic chest discomfort. This prompted a visit to an urgent care center, from which they were referred for further imaging. The patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1. They deny any fevers, hemoptysis, or unintentional weight loss.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The initial diagnostic evaluation began with a chest radiograph which demonstrated a large left-sided pleural effusion and an ill-defined opacity in the left lung. This was followed by a staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with intravenous contrast. Imaging confirmed a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe, associated with ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy. A large, loculated left pleural effusion was also identified. No suspicious metastatic lesions were seen in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or osseous structures.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

To obtain a tissue diagnosis and for symptomatic relief, the patient underwent an ultrasound-guided thoracentesis. Approximately 1.2 liters of serosanguinous fluid were removed. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid confirmed the presence of malignant cells, and immunohistochemical staining was consistent with a primary lung adenocarcinoma.

The combination of a primary lung mass with a confirmed malignant pleural effusion establishes a diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the cell block obtained from the pleural fluid. The analysis did not identify any actionable driver alterations. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Consistent with the standard of care at the time of diagnosis, PD-L1 expression was not tested. The Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 72-year-old former smoker with a good performance status who presents with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastatic disease confined to the pleura. Comprehensive molecular profiling did not reveal an actionable oncogenic driver. The patient is here today for initial consultation to review these findings and establish oncologic care.
