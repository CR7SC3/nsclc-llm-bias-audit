**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 54-year-old referred for evaluation and management of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside facility several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, which prompted further investigation. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. The patient reports feeling well otherwise, with no fevers, chills, weight loss, or hemoptysis. Performance status is excellent; the patient is fully ambulatory and carrying out all normal activities without restriction.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed. This study characterized a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe. There was no evidence of mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy.

For staging, a PET/CT scan was obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the known right upper lobe mass. No other sites of abnormal hypermetabolism were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis to suggest nodal or distant metastatic disease. A contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was also performed as part of the staging workup and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic review of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Tissue from the biopsy specimen was sent for next-generation sequencing. An actionable driver mutation was identified in **KRAS G12C**.

Further molecular analysis was negative for pathogenic alterations in **EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2**.

PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as **intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb)**.
