**HPI:**
This is a 60-year-old former smoker (quit >1 year ago) who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed progressive dyspnea on exertion, a non-productive cough, and persistent headaches. Over the two weeks prior to admission, the patient's family noted increasing episodes of confusion and word-finding difficulty, prompting presentation to the emergency department at [De-identified] Hospital. At the time of evaluation, performance status is estimated at ECOG 2, limited by dyspnea and neurologic symptoms.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial workup was initiated in the inpatient setting.
- **Chest X-ray** revealed a large right-sided pleural effusion and a suspected right upper lobe lung mass.
- **CT of the Head** without contrast was performed due to neurologic symptoms and demonstrated multiple parenchymal lesions concerning for metastatic disease.
- **CT of the Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis** with contrast was subsequently performed for staging. This confirmed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. A large, loculated right-sided pleural effusion was again noted. A lytic lesion in the T7 vertebral body was identified, suspicious for osseous metastasis. No other distant visceral metastases were identified in the abdomen or pelvis.
- **MRI of the Brain** with contrast was performed to further characterize the intracranial findings, which confirmed at least five discrete enhancing lesions in the cerebral and cerebellar hemispheres, consistent with metastatic disease.
- **Thoracentesis** of the right pleural effusion was performed. Cytology and cell block of the pleural fluid confirmed the presence of malignant cells. Pathology was consistent with adenocarcinoma of lung primary origin.

The combination of a primary lung mass with confirmed metastases to the brain, bone, and pleura establishes a diagnosis of Stage IV disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the pleural fluid cell block specimen.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (10 mutations/megabase).
- PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 60-year-old with a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The patient has a significant burden of disease with metastases to the brain, bone, and a malignant pleural effusion. Molecular profiling did not identify any targetable driver alterations. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
