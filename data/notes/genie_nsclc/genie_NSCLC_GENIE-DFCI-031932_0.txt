**HPI:**
This is a 55-year-old individual who presents for an initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when they developed a persistent, dry cough and mild dyspnea on exertion. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider. The patient is a former smoker, having quit less than one year ago after a significant pack-year history. They continue to maintain a good performance status, carrying out all routine activities without significant limitation.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, leading to a follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. The CT scan confirmed the presence of a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy, concerning for locoregional nodal involvement. No suspicious lesions were identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones to suggest distant metastatic disease.

Given the findings of locally advanced disease, a PET/CT was performed for staging, which confirmed high FDG avidity in the right upper lobe mass and the mediastinal lymph nodes, with no evidence of distant metabolic activity. A brain MRI was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and biopsy of a mediastinal lymph node. Pathology from this procedure confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary. The overall workup is consistent with clinical Stage IIIA (cT2bN2M0) disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. Results revealed the presence of high-level MET amplification. Further molecular testing was negative for common driver mutations, including EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14 skipping mutations, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was negative, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of <1%. The Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 55-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (cT2bN2M0) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The tumor is characterized by high-level MET amplification and is PD-L1 negative (TPS <1%). The patient has a good performance status and is here to discuss the diagnosis and establish a plan for management.
