**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 55-year-old who presents for evaluation of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit within the last year. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately three months prior to diagnosis, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. These symptoms were initially attributed to a respiratory infection, but when they failed to resolve, the patient sought further evaluation. An initial chest radiograph demonstrated a right-sided lung opacity, which prompted further investigation. The patient has a good performance status, reporting being ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though notes fatigue.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. Imaging revealed a 4.5 cm mass in the right upper lobe. There was extensive associated ipsilateral mediastinal and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, concerning for N3 disease. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones to suggest distant metastatic disease.

To confirm staging and evaluate for occult metastases, a PET/CT was obtained. This study demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, as well as in multiple mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymph node stations. The scan did not reveal any other sites of FDG-avid disease consistent with distant metastases.

A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

For tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)-guided fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathology from this specimen confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the lymph node biopsy. Key findings are as follows:
- **Actionable Driver Mutation:** An EGFR exon 19 deletion was detected.
- **Other Drivers:** Testing was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- **PD-L1 Expression:** Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was negative.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was low (<2 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 55-year-old with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, locally advanced, unresectable Stage IIIB (cT2N3M0, AJCC 8th edition) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion and negative PD-L1 status. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
