**HPI:**
This is a 67-year-old individual with a history of hypertension and hyperlipidemia, and a former smoking history (quit over 10 years ago), who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. Initial treatment with a course of antibiotics by their primary care physician did not result in improvement. Subsequent chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further investigation. The patient denies fevers, chills, night sweats, or significant weight loss. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 0-1, with the patient remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive staging workup was undertaken.

A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast demonstrated a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. In addition, there was prominent right-sided mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy, with the largest node measuring 2.2 cm in short axis. No suspicious liver, adrenal, or bone lesions were identified.

A subsequent whole-body PET/CT scan confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass and in multiple ipsilateral mediastinal nodal stations (subcarinal, right paratracheal), consistent with the CT findings. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was performed for central nervous system staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS). Biopsy of the right upper lobe mass and fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node were performed. Pathology from the primary mass confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma. The subcarinal lymph node aspirate was positive for metastatic adenocarcinoma, confirming N2 disease.

The combination of these findings is consistent with a clinical stage of IIIA (T2aN2M0).

**Molecular Studies:**
Tumor tissue from the biopsy was sent for comprehensive molecular profiling via next-generation sequencing.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 67-year-old former smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (T2aN2M0) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup has confirmed locoregionally advanced disease confined to the chest without evidence of distant metastases. Molecular studies are notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations and positive PD-L1 expression (TPS 1-49%). The patient is treatment-naive and presents today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
