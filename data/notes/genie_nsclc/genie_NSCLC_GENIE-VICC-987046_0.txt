**HPI:**
This is a 72-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to their primary care physician approximately one month ago with complaints of a new, persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was initially managed as a respiratory infection without improvement, prompting further investigation. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and maintains a good functional status, continuing with daily activities with some limitation due to shortness of breath. ECOG performance status is estimated at 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity and a moderate left-sided pleural effusion. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis on [De-identified] confirmed a 3.5 cm speculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also characterized the large left pleural effusion and noted several small, non-specific subpleural nodules in the left lung. There was no evidence of adrenal, hepatic, or osseous metastases.

A PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes, which demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass and within the left pleural space, consistent with a malignant effusion. No other sites of distant hypermetabolic disease were identified.

Given the concern for metastatic disease, a brain MRI with contrast was obtained and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

To obtain a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a left-sided thoracentesis on [De-identified]. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, consistent with adenocarcinoma. This finding confirms Stage IV disease.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 72-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (M1a) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, confirmed by malignant pleural effusion. Staging studies including brain MRI are negative for distant organ metastases. The patient has a good performance status. Molecular profiling is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion, high PD-L1 expression, and a high tumor mutational burden. The patient is here today to discuss these findings and the implications for management.
