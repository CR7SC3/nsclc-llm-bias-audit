**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 48-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a significant history of tobacco use and is a current smoker.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they began to develop a persistent, dry cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. These symptoms were associated with right-sided pleuritic chest discomfort and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds over the past three months. The patient initially sought care at an urgent care clinic and was treated with a course of antibiotics without improvement.

Due to worsening shortness of breath, the patient presented to an outside emergency department. A chest X-ray performed at that time was abnormal, showing a large right-sided lung mass and a pleural effusion. A subsequent staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was obtained, which further characterized the extent of disease. The patient then underwent a CT-guided biopsy of the primary lung mass, which confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. A dedicated brain MRI was also performed as part of the staging workup.

The patient is now referred to our clinic for evaluation and to establish care. At today's visit, the patient reports ongoing dyspnea, particularly with ambulation, but is able to perform all activities of daily living. ECOG performance status is 1.
