**HPI:**
This is a 59-year-old treatment-naive patient presenting for initial oncology consultation regarding a recent diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to their primary care physician with a several-month history of persistent cough and mild exertional dyspnea. Initial workup included a chest X-ray which demonstrated a right upper lobe mass, prompting further imaging. The patient reports a generally good functional status with an ECOG performance status of 0-1, maintaining most usual activities without significant limitations. They deny significant weight loss, fevers, chills, or night sweats.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Subsequent CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis revealed a primary mass measuring approximately [plausible size, e.g., 4.5 cm] in the right upper lobe, along with several enlarged ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes. There was no evidence of distant metastatic disease in the abdomen or pelvis. A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass was performed, which confirmed the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. For comprehensive staging, a PET/CT scan was performed, which showed hypermetabolic activity in the primary right upper lobe lesion and the enlarged ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes, consistent with locoregional disease. There was no evidence of hypermetabolic activity suggestive of distant metastases. An MRI of the brain was also performed, which showed no evidence of intracranial metastases. Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Molecular profiling was performed on the tumor biopsy specimen. Results indicate no actionable driver alterations, with testing negative for EGFR mutations, ALK rearrangements, ROS1 rearrangements, BRAF V600E mutations, MET exon 14 skipping mutations, RET fusions, and NTRK fusions. PD-L1 expression testing results are not yet reported.
