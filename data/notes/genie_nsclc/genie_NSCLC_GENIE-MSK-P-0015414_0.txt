**HPI:**
This is a 56-year-old individual with a significant smoking history who is referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside facility approximately one month ago with a chief complaint of progressive shortness of breath and a persistent dry cough over the preceding 2-3 months. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass and an associated pleural effusion.

The patient was subsequently evaluated by a pulmonologist. A diagnostic CT of the chest confirmed the presence of a right upper lobe lung mass with extensive pleural involvement. The patient remains symptomatic with dyspnea on moderate exertion but is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care. Performance status is ECOG 1. The patient is a current smoker.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive staging workup has been completed.

A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis revealed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. This was associated with diffuse, nodular right-sided pleural thickening and a moderate malignant pleural effusion. No other suspicious visceral or osseous lesions were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

A subsequent PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the right upper lobe primary tumor. Diffuse, intensely FDG-avid pleural studding was also noted, confirming malignant pleural disease. No other sites of hypermetabolic activity suggestive of distant metastatic disease were seen.

An MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed for central nervous system staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided biopsy of a pleural deposit. Histopathologic analysis confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor specimen.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry (IHC) was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as high (>16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 56-year-old who presents with newly diagnosed Stage IV (cT_N_M1a) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. Metastatic disease is documented in the pleura. Brain imaging is negative for metastatic disease. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations, positive PD-L1 expression (TPS 1-49%), and a high tumor mutational burden. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation to discuss these findings.
