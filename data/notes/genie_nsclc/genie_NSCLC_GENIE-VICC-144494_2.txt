**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 59-year-old with a history of former tobacco use (quit >1 year ago) who presents for initial oncologic evaluation after a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. After an initial course of antibiotics from their primary care provider failed to resolve symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a suspicious right upper lobe opacity.

A follow-up CT scan of the chest confirmed the presence of a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, measuring approximately 4 cm, with associated prominent mediastinal and right hilar lymphadenopathy. Given the concern for malignancy, a comprehensive staging evaluation and tissue diagnosis were recommended.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
A full staging evaluation was pursued. A PET/CT scan demonstrated high FDG avidity in the known right upper lobe primary mass and in multiple mediastinal nodal stations, including the paratracheal and subcarinal regions. Importantly, the PET/CT showed no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for definitive diagnosis and staging. Biopsies were successfully obtained from the primary mass and from an enlarged subcarinal (station 7) lymph node. Pathology from both sites returned invasive adenocarcinoma, confirming a non-small cell lung cancer primary. The nodal biopsy confirmed metastatic involvement, consistent with locally advanced disease.

The patient has tolerated the diagnostic workup well and maintains a good performance status, remaining fully ambulatory and capable of self-care. They report a stable appetite and a 5-pound weight loss over the past three months.
