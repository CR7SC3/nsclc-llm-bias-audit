**HPI:**
This is a 30-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, who presents for initial oncology consultation. The patient was in their usual state of excellent health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent dry cough. This was initially managed with outpatient symptomatic treatments without improvement. Approximately one month ago, the patient began to experience progressive, dull headaches and an episode of right-sided arm weakness, which prompted an evaluation in the emergency department at an outside hospital. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago, with a prior 5 pack-year history. They are otherwise active and maintain a good performance status, though they report increased fatigue over the past several weeks.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The diagnostic evaluation began in the emergency department. A CT of the head without contrast, followed by a contrasted study, revealed several ring-enhancing lesions within the cerebral hemispheres, concerning for metastatic disease.

Subsequent staging imaging was pursued. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis demonstrated a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, with associated bulky mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also identified multiple lytic and blastic osseous lesions, most prominent in the thoracic spine and pelvis.

A PET/CT scan was performed to further delineate the extent of disease. This study confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe lung mass, multiple mediastinal lymph node stations, numerous sites of osseous metastasis, and the previously identified intracranial lesions.

An MRI of the brain was subsequently performed, which better characterized the intracranial disease, confirming at least five distinct metastatic deposits in the bilateral cerebral hemispheres with associated vasogenic edema.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed on [De-identified Date]. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
The lung biopsy tissue was sent for comprehensive molecular profiling. Next-generation sequencing and immunohistochemistry (IHC) were performed.
-   **Actionable Driver Mutations:** A sensitizing **EGFR exon 19 deletion** was detected.
-   **Additional Markers:** The tumor was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
-   **PD-L1 Expression:** PD-L1 IHC was strongly positive with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of **≥50%**.
-   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was found to be **intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb)**.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 30-year-old individual presenting with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastases to the brain and bone. The patient is treatment-naive. Molecular analysis of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion and high PD-L1 expression. The patient is here today for initial consultation to review these findings.
