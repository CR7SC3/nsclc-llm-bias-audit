**HPI:**
This is an 80-year-old who is seen for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they began to experience progressive dyspnea on exertion and a persistent, non-productive cough. This prompted an evaluation in the emergency department.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass and a moderate pleural effusion. A subsequent CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed for staging. This confirmed the presence of a 4 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also characterized a large, right-sided pleural effusion with associated pleural thickening, suspicious for malignant involvement. There was no evidence of distant metastatic disease in the abdomen or pelvis. A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided thoracentesis was performed. Cytology from the pleural fluid confirmed the presence of malignant cells consistent with adenocarcinoma, establishing a diagnosis of Stage IV disease (M1a) based on malignant pleural effusion.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the biopsy specimen was sent for next-generation sequencing. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was low at <2 mutations/megabase. PD-L1 expression was not tested, as this was not standard at the time of sequencing.

**Problem Summary:**
This is an 80-year-old, treatment-naive, never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastasis to the pleura. Molecular studies are notable for the absence of any identified actionable driver mutations and a low tumor mutational burden. The patient has a good performance status and is here today to discuss the diagnosis.
