**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 63-year-old individual referred for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit within the last year. The patient initially presented to an outside facility approximately one month ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, which prompted further investigation. The patient reports feeling generally well otherwise, with stable weight and appetite. Performance status is excellent, estimated at ECOG 1, with the patient maintaining all activities of daily living and continuing to work part-time. Past medical history is notable for a history of cervical cancer, which was treated definitively in the past and is considered in remission. The patient is here today, accompanied by family, to discuss the diagnosis and establish a plan for oncologic care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with intravenous contrast was performed. This study characterized a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the apical segment of the right upper lobe. The scan also revealed bulky ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy, with a conglomerate of nodes in the right paratracheal station (4R) measuring up to 2.5 cm in short-axis diameter.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT was obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the right paratracheal (4R) and subcarinal (7) nodal stations, consistent with nodal metastases. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease below the diaphragm or in the contralateral hemithorax, and no suspicious osseous lesions were identified.

To complete the staging workup and to rule out central nervous system involvement, a contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (EBUS-FNA) of the right paratracheal (4R) lymph node. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the EBUS-FNA.
- No actionable driver alterations were identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, and NTRK. KRAS and ERBB2 were also wild-type.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was negative, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 0%.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was evaluated and found to be high, at >16 mutations/megabase.
- A loss-of-function mutation was identified in the STK11 gene.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 63-year-old former smoker with an excellent performance status (ECOG 1) who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging is based on a primary right upper lobe tumor with ipsilateral mediastinal lymph node involvement (N2) and no evidence of distant metastatic disease (M0). Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations, negative PD-L1 expression, a high tumor mutational burden (TMB-high), and the presence of an STK11 loss-of-function mutation. The patient is treatment-naive and presents today for initial consultation and to establish care.
