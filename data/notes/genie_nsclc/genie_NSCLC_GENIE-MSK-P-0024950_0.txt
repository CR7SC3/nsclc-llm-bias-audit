**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 59-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who is referred for initial consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was asymptomatic and underwent a routine low-dose screening chest CT, which revealed a suspicious solid nodule in the right lung.

A subsequent diagnostic contrast-enhanced chest CT was performed, which characterized a 1.7 cm spiculated nodule in the right upper lobe. There was no evidence of hilar or mediastinal lymphadenopathy. To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was obtained. This study demonstrated FDG-avidity isolated to the known right upper lobe nodule, with no uptake in regional lymph nodes or at any distant sites to suggest metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

Given these findings, the patient proceeded with a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe nodule, which established the diagnosis. The patient presents today to discuss the findings and establish oncologic care. They report an excellent functional status, are fully active, and deny any constitutional symptoms such as fevers, weight loss, or fatigue.
