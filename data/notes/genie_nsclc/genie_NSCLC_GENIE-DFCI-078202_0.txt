**HPI:**
This is a 79-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they noted the development of a non-tender lump on the anterior chest wall. This was associated with a new, persistent dry cough and intermittent headaches. These symptoms prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider, which initiated a diagnostic workup. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. They remain fully ambulatory and self-caring, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest CT scan was performed which revealed a large primary mass in the left upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also confirmed the presence of a 2.5 cm subcutaneous soft tissue nodule in the right anterior chest wall.

Given the patient's report of headaches, a contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was subsequently performed. This study demonstrated multiple enhancing lesions in the cerebral and cerebellar hemispheres, consistent with metastatic disease.

A PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes, which confirmed high metabolic activity in the primary left upper lobe lung mass, mediastinal lymph nodes, the right chest wall subcutaneous nodule, and the known brain lesions. No other sites of distant metastatic disease were identified.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent an ultrasound-guided core biopsy of the accessible right chest wall subcutaneous nodule. Pathology confirmed a poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

The cumulative findings of a primary lung tumor with metastases to the brain and subcutaneous tissue establish a diagnosis of AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the biopsy specimen was sent for comprehensive genomic and protein expression profiling.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
- Next-generation sequencing did not identify any actionable driver alterations. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- A loss-of-function mutation was detected in the KEAP1 gene.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 79-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV squamous cell non-small cell lung cancer, metastatic to the brain and subcutaneous tissue. The patient is treatment-naive and presents with a good performance status. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and the presence of a KEAP1 loss-of-function mutation, with no identified targetable driver mutations. The patient is seen today for initial consultation to review these findings and discuss the diagnosis.
