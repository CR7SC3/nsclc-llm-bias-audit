**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 77-year-old with a past medical history of breast cancer, who is seen in consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This prompted an evaluation by their primary care provider, which included a chest X-ray that was suspicious for a lung mass. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over a year ago. On review of systems, the patient reports the cough but denies hemoptysis, chest pain, or significant shortness of breath. They note a mild decrease in energy but remain fully ambulatory and independent in all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed on [De-identified Date]. This confirmed the presence of a 3.1 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was no evidence of pleural effusion or definite mediastinal lymphadenopathy.

No other sites of FDG-avid disease were identified to suggest regional nodal involvement or distant metastases. A brain MRI with and without contrast was also obtained as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass on [De-identified Date]. Pathology from this specimen confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma.

Based on this comprehensive workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IIA non-small cell lung cancer.

Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor biopsy specimen.
- Actionable driver mutation detected: EGFR L858R point mutation (sensitizing).
- The tumor was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was low (<2 mut/Mb).
