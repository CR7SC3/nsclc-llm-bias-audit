**History of Present Illness**
The patient is a former smoker who was in their usual state of health until approximately three months ago, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. These symptoms did not resolve with a course of antibiotics prescribed by their primary care provider. Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained, which revealed a right upper lobe opacity.

This prompted a follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, which confirmed the presence of a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, associated with prominent right hilar and ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy. There was no evidence of distant organ involvement on this initial scan.

The patient was subsequently referred for a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathology from this procedure confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma.

Staging workup was then completed, including a PET/CT scan and a brain MRI. The PET/CT demonstrated high FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes, consistent with nodal involvement. No FDG-avid lesions suggestive of distant metastatic disease were identified. A brain MRI was performed for central nervous system staging and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient presents to clinic today for discussion of these findings. They report a stable cough but deny hemoptysis, fevers, or significant weight loss. Their performance status is excellent; they remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care (ECOG 1).
