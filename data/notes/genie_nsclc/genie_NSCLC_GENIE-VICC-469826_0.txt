**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
Following the abnormal chest x-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed the presence of a 5.5 cm solid mass in the right upper lobe. The mass appeared to abut the major fissure, but there was no definitive evidence of chest wall or mediastinal invasion. No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, or other distant visceral lesions were identified. A few small, sub-centimeter mediastinal lymph nodes were noted but were not definitively pathologic by size criteria.

For definitive staging and tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a PET/CT scan which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity within the known right upper lobe mass. There was no evidence of FDG-avid hilar or mediastinal lymphadenopathy, and no distant metastatic disease was identified. A subsequent MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed for central nervous system staging and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass was performed. Pathologic analysis revealed poorly differentiated non-small cell carcinoma with features most consistent with squamous cell carcinoma.

Based on the combination of imaging and pathology, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IIB (cT3N0M0) non-small cell lung cancer.
