**REASON FOR CONSULTATION:** Initial evaluation of newly diagnosed non-small cell lung cancer.

**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 60-year-old individual with a history of former tobacco use (quit >1 year ago) who is referred for oncologic consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient initially presented to an outside facility approximately one month prior with a persistent, non-productive cough. An initial chest radiograph was abnormal, prompting a follow-up CT scan of the chest. This imaging identified a suspicious mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy.

A subsequent staging workup was initiated. A PET/CT scan confirmed the hypermetabolic right upper lobe mass and mediastinal nodal involvement but did not reveal distant extracranial disease. For comprehensive staging, a brain MRI was performed, which revealed the presence of multiple small intracranial lesions consistent with metastatic disease. A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass was then performed, establishing the diagnosis.

At present, the patient reports a mild, stable cough but denies hemoptysis, chest pain, or significant dyspnea. The patient is neurologically asymptomatic, denying headaches, dizziness, seizures, or focal motor or sensory changes. The patient maintains an excellent performance status, is fully ambulatory, and is managing all self-care.
