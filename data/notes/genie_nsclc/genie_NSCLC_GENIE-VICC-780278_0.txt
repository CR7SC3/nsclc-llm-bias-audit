**HPI:**
This is a 51-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who is referred for initial medical oncology evaluation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside hospital approximately one month prior with a several-week history of a persistent, dry cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. An initial chest radiograph was abnormal, prompting further investigation. The patient is otherwise active and maintains a good functional status, with an ECOG performance status of 1, though notes fatigue related to symptoms.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed which revealed a large, approximately 5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. This was associated with extensive and bulky mediastinal and right hilar lymphadenopathy. Notably, there was involvement of contralateral mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymph nodes, concerning for locoregionally advanced disease. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or other visceral organs.

A subsequent staging PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the extensive nodal stations previously identified on CT, including the right hilar, subcarinal, contralateral mediastinal, and right supraclavicular regions. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease below the diaphragm to suggest distant metastatic spread. A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic review confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified (NOS) histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor biopsy specimen. No actionable driver alteration was identified; the tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as high at >16 mut/Mb. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on this sample.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 51-year-old former smoker with newly diagnosed, unresectable, AJCC Stage IIIB (cT2bN3M0) non-small cell lung cancer, NOS histology. The patient is treatment-naive with an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling is negative for common driver mutations and notable for a high tumor mutational burden. Brain imaging is negative for metastatic disease.
