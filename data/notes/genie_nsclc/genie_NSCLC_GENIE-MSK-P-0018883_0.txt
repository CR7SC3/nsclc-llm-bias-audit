**HPI:**
This is a 67-year-old former smoker (quit over one year ago) who presents for initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months ago, when they developed a persistent cough and progressive shortness of breath with exertion. An initial chest X-ray ordered by their primary care provider revealed a right-sided lung mass.

For further evaluation, a staging PET/CT scan was performed. This demonstrated a large, hypermetabolic primary mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also revealed extensive FDG-avid lymphadenopathy within the mediastinum and in the right supraclavicular region. Importantly, there was no evidence of distant metastatic disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones. A subsequent MRI of the brain was performed for staging purposes and was negative for intracranial metastases.

Given these findings, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for definitive tissue diagnosis and staging. Biopsies were obtained from a subcarinal lymph node, which confirmed the diagnosis. The patient has been referred for discussion of the diagnosis and management options. They report a good performance status (ECOG 1), remaining fully ambulatory and capable of self-care, though limited in strenuous activity by dyspnea.
