**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 65-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when a chest CT, performed as part of a cardiac workup, incidentally revealed a solid pulmonary nodule in the right upper lobe. The patient denies any significant associated symptoms such as cough, shortness of breath, hemoptysis, or chest pain. There has been no unintentional weight loss, fevers, or night sweats. The patient maintains an excellent performance status, continuing with all usual activities of daily living without limitation. Following the imaging finding, the patient was referred to Pulmonary for further evaluation and subsequently to our clinic for oncologic consultation after a definitive diagnosis was made.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A diagnostic chest CT with contrast on [De-identified] confirmed a 3.5 cm spiculated solid nodule in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe. There was no associated mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy.

To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was performed on [De-identified]. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity within the known right upper lobe nodule, consistent with malignancy. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the mediastinal or hilar lymph nodes, nor were there any findings suggestive of distant metastatic disease in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

A contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was performed on [De-identified] to complete staging and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma. Based on this comprehensive workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IB (T2aN0M0) non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor biopsy specimen. No actionable driver alterations were identified. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, and NTRK. The tumor was also negative for KRAS and ERBB2 mutations.

Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 65-year-old former smoker with an excellent performance status who presents for initial evaluation of a new diagnosis of clinical Stage IB (T2aN0M0) invasive adenocarcinoma of the right lung. The diagnostic workup is negative for nodal or distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for an absence of common actionable driver mutations.
