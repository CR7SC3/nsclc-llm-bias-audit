**HPI:**
This is a 77-year-old with a history of hypertension and hyperlipidemia, who is referred for evaluation of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to an outside facility with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea developing over the past several months. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further investigation.

A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This study confirmed a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. It also incidentally revealed a solitary, suspicious lesion in the liver. To complete staging, a whole-body PET/CT scan was obtained, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary lung mass, several mediastinal lymph nodes, and the solitary liver lesion, highly concerning for metastatic disease. An MRI of the brain was performed and showed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass. Pathology confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. The patient has a good performance status (ECOG 1), remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though reports feeling more fatigued than usual. The patient presents today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and establish oncologic care.
