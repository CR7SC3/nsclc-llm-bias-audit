**HPI:**
This is a 58-year-old individual with a history of former tobacco use (quit >1 year ago) who is referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to an outside facility several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was initially treated as a respiratory infection without improvement. Due to worsening symptoms and the development of mild chest discomfort, further evaluation was pursued. The patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of self-care but reports significant fatigue.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a large right upper lobe opacity with associated mediastinal widening. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study characterized a large primary mass in the right upper lobe, with extensive mediastinal and contralateral supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the known right upper lobe primary tumor and in multiple nodal stations, including the right hilar, subcarinal, and contralateral mediastinal and left supraclavicular nodes, consistent with N3 disease. There was no evidence of FDG-avid distant metastatic disease.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was performed as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis and definitive staging. A fine-needle aspiration (FNA) of a subcarinal lymph node was performed. Pathology from this specimen confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive molecular profiling was performed on the biopsy specimen.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was negative.
- Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- Next-generation sequencing for targetable alterations was performed. No actionable driver alteration was identified; results were negative for EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 58-year-old former smoker presenting for initial oncologic evaluation of newly diagnosed, locally advanced, non-metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The definitive diagnosis is Stage IIIB (AJCC 8th Edition) adenocarcinoma of the lung, based on a primary tumor with contralateral mediastinal and supraclavicular nodal involvement and no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling is notable for negative PD-L1 expression, intermediate TMB, and the absence of common actionable driver mutations. The patient is treatment-naive and is here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
