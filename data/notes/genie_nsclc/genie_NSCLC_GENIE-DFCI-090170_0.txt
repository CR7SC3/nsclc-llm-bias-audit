**HPI:**
This is a 61-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and was in a usual state of good health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion began. Symptoms worsened to the point of limiting the ability to walk more than a short distance. An initial evaluation with a primary care provider led to a chest X-ray, which demonstrated a lung opacity and a large pleural effusion. The patient was referred for further diagnostic workup. The patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though is limited by shortness of breath with significant exertion. There is no history of hemoptysis, fevers, or significant weight loss.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified Date]. This revealed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the left lower lobe of the lung. The scan also confirmed a large, loculated left-sided pleural effusion. There was no evidence of contralateral lung nodules, or metastatic disease to the liver, adrenal glands, or bones. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

Given the large, symptomatic pleural effusion, the patient underwent a video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) procedure for pleural biopsy and talc pleurodesis on [De-identified Date]. Pathology from the pleural biopsies confirmed metastatic adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the pleural biopsy tissue specimen.
-   An actionable driver mutation was detected in the form of an ALK gene rearrangement.
-   PD-L1 expression by tumor proportion score (TPS) was reported as ≥50%.
-   Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
-   Testing was negative for pathogenic mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 61-year-old never-smoker who presents with newly diagnosed Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, metastatic to the pleura (cT2a N0 M1a). Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of an ALK rearrangement and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%). The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
