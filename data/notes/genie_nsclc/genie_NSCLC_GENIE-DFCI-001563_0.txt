**REASON FOR CONSULTATION:** Initial evaluation of newly diagnosed non-small cell lung cancer.

**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 55-year-old individual with a history of being in a good state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation. The patient began to experience a persistent, non-productive cough and a gradual onset of mild dyspnea on exertion. These symptoms did not resolve with a course of antibiotics prescribed by a primary care provider, which prompted further investigation.

A chest radiograph was obtained which showed a right-sided pulmonary opacity, leading to a follow-up computed tomography (CT) scan of the chest. This imaging confirmed a primary lung mass and raised suspicion for metastatic disease. The patient was subsequently referred for a tissue diagnosis.

On review of systems today, the patient endorses the persistent cough and some generalized fatigue. There has been an unintentional weight loss of approximately 5-7 pounds over the past three months. The patient denies fevers, chills, night sweats, or significant chest pain. There are no focal neurological symptoms such as headache, vision changes, or weakness. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 1. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
I have personally reviewed the available outside records and imaging reports. The diagnostic workup to date includes:

*   **CT of the Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis with contrast:** This study identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. A distinct 1.5 cm nodule was noted in the contralateral left lower lobe, concerning for a metastatic deposit. Several sub-centimeter mediastinal lymph nodes were also present. The abdominal and pelvic portions of the scan were negative for other evidence of metastatic disease.

*   **PET/CT:** A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the primary right upper lobe mass, the contralateral lung nodule in the left lower lobe, and in mediastinal lymph nodes. No other distant sites of FDG-avid disease were identified.

*   **MRI of the Brain with and without contrast:** Performed for central nervous system staging, this study was negative for intracranial metastases.

*   **CT-guided Core Biopsy:** A biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed on [De-identified Date]. Pathology confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with adenocarcinoma histology.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing and immunohistochemistry were performed on the tumor biopsy specimen. The results are as follows:

*   **Actionable Drivers:** An EGFR uncommon sensitizing mutation was detected.
*   **Negative Markers:** Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, ERBB2, and KRAS.
*   **PD-L1 Expression:** PD-L1 Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) is ≥50%.
*   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB is intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**PROBLEM SUMMARY:**
In summary, this is a 55-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, with metastatic disease to the contralateral lung. The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for an uncommon sensitizing EGFR mutation and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%).
