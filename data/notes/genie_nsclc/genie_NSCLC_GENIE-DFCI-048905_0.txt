**HPI:**
This is a 60-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient reports a history as a former smoker, having quit over one year ago. The diagnosis was initiated after a lung nodule was incidentally discovered on a chest X-ray obtained for an unrelated reason. Subsequent workup led to the current diagnosis. The patient is largely asymptomatic, denying significant cough, shortness of breath, chest pain, or hemoptysis. There has been no unintentional weight loss or decline in appetite. The patient maintains an excellent performance status, is fully ambulatory, and carries out all normal activities without restriction. Today's visit is to review the completed diagnostic workup and discuss the implications of this new diagnosis.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a diagnostic CT of the chest was performed on [De-identified date]. This revealed a 3.5 cm spiculated solid nodule in the right upper lobe, without associated mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy. For staging purposes, a PET/CT scan was subsequently obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity confined to the primary right upper lobe nodule. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the mediastinum, contralateral lung, or any distant sites. A brain MRI with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases. A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass was performed by Interventional Radiology on [De-identified date]. Pathology from this procedure confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma. Based on these findings (T2aN0M0), the patient is staged with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IB non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy. No actionable oncogenic driver alterations were identified; testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 1-49%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2-16 mut/Mb). The molecular profile also detected a loss-of-function mutation in the STK11 gene.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 60-year-old with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, Stage IB (T2aN0M0) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma of the right upper lobe. The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular testing is notable for PD-L1 expression (TPS 1-49%) and an STK11 loss-of-function mutation, with no other identified actionable driver alterations. The purpose of this initial consultation is to establish care and review these findings in detail.
