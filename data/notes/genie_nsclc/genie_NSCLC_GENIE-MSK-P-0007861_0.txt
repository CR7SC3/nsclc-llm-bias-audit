**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 73-year-old who presents for evaluation and management of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to diagnosis, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially attributed to a seasonal illness, but when symptoms failed to resolve, they sought evaluation. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, prompting further investigation. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. They report good functional status, remaining fully independent in all activities of daily living with an ECOG performance status of 1, limited only by occasional shortness of breath on significant exertion. A comprehensive review of systems is otherwise negative for constitutional symptoms such as fevers, night sweats, or unintentional weight loss.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed the presence of a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also revealed extensive ipsilateral and contralateral mediastinal and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, consistent with N3 nodal disease. There was no evidence of distant metastatic disease in the visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis, with no suspicious lesions in the liver or adrenal glands. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis and complete staging, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS). Fine-needle aspiration was performed on the right upper lobe mass and a contralateral mediastinal lymph node (station 4L). Pathology from both sites was consistent with invasive adenocarcinoma.

Based on the combination of a T2 primary tumor with extensive contralateral mediastinal nodal involvement (N3) and no evidence of distant metastases (M0), the patient's disease is classified as Stage IIIB.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the EBUS-guided biopsy.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be in the intermediate range (2–16 mut/Mb).
- PD-L1 expression was not tested, as this was not standard of care at the time of the initial diagnostic sequencing.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 73-year-old, treatment-naive patient with an excellent performance status and a new diagnosis of Stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. Staging workup, including CT imaging and brain MRI, has confirmed locally advanced disease confined to the thorax without evidence of distant metastases. Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor did not identify any actionable driver alterations. The patient is seen today to discuss the diagnosis in detail and to review the implications of these findings.
