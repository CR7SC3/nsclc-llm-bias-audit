**Diagnostic Workup:**
The patient's initial workup began with a chest X-ray that demonstrated a left-sided opacity and a small effusion.

A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study characterized a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe. It also revealed multiple small pulmonary nodules in the right lung, suspicious for contralateral metastatic disease. A moderate left-sided pleural effusion with associated nodular pleural thickening was also identified. No evidence of hepatic, adrenal, or osseous metastases was seen.

A PET/CT scan was then obtained for staging, which confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary left upper lobe mass, the contralateral lung nodules, and the left pleura. No other sites of FDG-avid disease were identified.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the left upper lobe lung mass. The pathology confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.
