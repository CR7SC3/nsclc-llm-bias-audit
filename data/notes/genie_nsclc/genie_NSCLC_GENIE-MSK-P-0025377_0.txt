**HPI:**
The patient is a 73-year-old former smoker who presents for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough. This was initially attributed to a seasonal illness, but it failed to resolve. The patient also notes mild exertional dyspnea and occasional chest discomfort over the past month. Due to the persistence of these symptoms, they presented to their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting a follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This study confirmed the presence of a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, along with prominent right-sided hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or other visualized structures.

A full staging evaluation was subsequently completed. A PET/CT scan demonstrated high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe tumor and in ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes, consistent with locoregional involvement. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the contralateral lung, or at any distant sites. A dedicated MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)-guided fine needle aspiration of the right upper lobe mass and a right paratracheal (4R) lymph node. The patient has tolerated the diagnostic workup well and maintains an excellent performance status, continuing with daily activities with only minimal limitation from the cough.

*   **Actionable Driver Mutation:** KRAS G12C mutation detected.

*   **PD-L1 Expression:** Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) is ≥50%.
