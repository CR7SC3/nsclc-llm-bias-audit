**HPI:**
This is a 68-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who is referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider. Initial evaluation with a chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, leading to further diagnostic workup. The patient reports some associated fatigue but denies fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 1. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which identified a 5.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. This was associated with extensive bulky mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, concerning for locoregionally advanced disease. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A subsequent PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, as well as in the extensive mediastinal and right supraclavicular nodal stations, confirming the findings on the diagnostic CT. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases.

To complete staging and rule out central nervous system involvement, a brain MRI with and without contrast was obtained, which was negative for any intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue acquisition. A fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node (station 7) was performed. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed non-small cell lung cancer with adenocarcinoma histology.

Based on the combination of a primary tumor with extensive N3 nodal involvement (supraclavicular) and no evidence of distant metastasis (M0), the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IIIB disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. Key findings are as follows:
- An activating ERBB2 (HER2) exon 20 insertion was detected.
- Testing was negative for actionable mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and KRAS.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be high (>16 mut/Mb).
- A co-occurring loss-of-function mutation in the KEAP1 gene was also identified.
- PD-L1 expression was not tested, as the sample was sequenced prior to this becoming routine standard of care.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 68-year-old former smoker presenting with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive, Stage IIIB non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. The disease is locoregionally advanced, with no evidence of distant or brain metastases. Molecular profiling is notable for an activating ERBB2 exon 20 insertion, high TMB, and a KEAP1 loss-of-function mutation.
