**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for an 80-year-old who presents for evaluation and management of a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a past medical history notable for a remote smoking history, having quit over one year ago.

The patient was reportedly in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks ago, when they began to experience a persistent dry cough and progressive shortness of breath with exertion. These symptoms prompted an evaluation by their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray was concerning for a left-sided lung mass and an associated pleural effusion.

The patient was referred for further imaging. A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed, which confirmed the presence of a spiculated mass in the left upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated extensive nodular thickening of the left pleura and a moderate-to-large pleural effusion, highly suspicious for malignant involvement. No other suspicious lesions in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones were identified. A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging, which showed intense FDG-avidity in the primary left lung mass and diffuse uptake throughout the left pleura, consistent with metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A diagnostic and therapeutic thoracentesis was performed. Cytological analysis of the pleural fluid confirmed the presence of malignant cells, establishing a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma.

**Pathology:**
