**HPI:**
This is an initial medical oncology consultation for a 66-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a current smoker. The patient presented to an outside facility approximately one month ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and a new, tender lump noted on the upper back. An initial chest X-ray revealed a large right upper lobe lung mass. The patient was subsequently referred for further diagnostic evaluation. The patient reports ongoing cough and intermittent, localized pain related to the back lesion, managed with over-the-counter analgesics. The patient maintains a good performance status, is fully ambulatory, and capable of all self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A staging PET/CT scan was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study demonstrated a hypermetabolic 5.5 cm primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Multiple sites of distant metastatic disease were identified, including a destructive, hypermetabolic lesion in the T7 vertebral body and a 2 cm avid subcutaneous nodule in the right paraspinous region, corresponding to the palpable lesion. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also obtained and was negative for intracranial metastasis.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent an ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy of the accessible right paraspinous subcutaneous nodule. Pathology from this procedure confirmed metastatic carcinoma consistent with a lung primary. The final histology was reported as non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified (NSCLC, nos).

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the subcutaneous nodule biopsy.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- PD-L1 expression was not tested, as this was not standard of care at the time of initial tissue analysis.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 66-year-old current smoker with a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, histology not otherwise specified. Staging workup confirmed metastatic disease involving bone and subcutaneous tissue, with no evidence of central nervous system involvement. Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor did not identify an actionable driver mutation. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis, prognosis, and to consider goals of care.
