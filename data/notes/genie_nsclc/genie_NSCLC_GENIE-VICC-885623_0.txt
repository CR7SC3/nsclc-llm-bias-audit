**HPI:**
This is a 76-year-old individual with a history of former smoking (quit >1 year ago) who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when a persistent, non-productive cough developed. This was followed by progressive dyspnea on exertion and the new onset of a dull, aching pain in the right hip. These symptoms prompted an evaluation in the emergency department. The patient remains ambulatory and capable of self-care but is limited by shortness of breath and pain.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Initial workup included a chest CT scan which revealed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung. The scan also demonstrated a moderate right-sided malignant pleural effusion and multiple lytic lesions concerning for osseous metastases, including a prominent lesion in the right iliac bone corresponding to the patient's site of pain. A staging MRI of the brain was performed and showed no evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary. Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with metastatic disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing and immunohistochemistry were performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy. These studies revealed the following:
- An actionable driver mutation was detected in the Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) gene, specifically an L858R point mutation in exon 21.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be high, at >16 mutations/megabase.
- Testing was negative for ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2 rearrangements or mutations.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 76-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging studies confirm metastatic disease involving the bone and pleura, with no evidence of CNS involvement. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation, high PD-L1 expression, and a high tumor mutational burden. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss these findings and establish care.
