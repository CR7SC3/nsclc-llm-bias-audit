**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 56-year-old individual with a past medical history of prostate cancer, who is referred for initial consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. Symptoms were initially attributed to a viral illness, but due to a lack of resolution and the onset of right-sided pleuritic chest pain, the patient presented for medical evaluation.

An initial chest radiograph revealed a right-sided lung opacity and a moderate pleural effusion. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed the presence of a 2.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, along with multiple enhancing pleural nodules and a large, complex right-sided pleural effusion. The patient was referred to Interventional Radiology. A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass was performed. No FDG-avid locoregional lymphadenopathy or distant metastatic sites were identified. A brain MRI with contrast was also obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. They report a good performance status (ECOG 1), with activity limited only by mild residual dyspnea. They are here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
