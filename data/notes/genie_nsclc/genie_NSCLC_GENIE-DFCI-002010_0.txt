**HPI:**
This is a 72-year-old former smoker who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately 2-3 months ago, when they began to experience a persistent, non-productive cough and increasing dyspnea on exertion. This was associated with generalized fatigue and an unintentional 10-pound weight loss. More recently, over the past month, the patient developed a new, dull, and constant ache in their lower back, which has been partially responsive to over-the-counter analgesics. These progressive symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider, who initiated a diagnostic workup.

The patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all activities of daily living but is limited in strenuous activity due to fatigue and shortness of breath. ECOG performance status is 1. The patient has a remote smoking history but quit over a year ago.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest radiograph revealed a large right-sided lung mass, prompting further imaging.

A CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date], which demonstrated a 5.2 cm spiculated mass in the right lower lobe with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also revealed a suspicious lytic lesion within a lumbar vertebral body.

A subsequent whole-body PET/CT scan on [De-identified Date] confirmed intense FDG-avidity within the primary right lower lobe mass, multiple mediastinal lymph nodes, and the lytic lesion in the L3 vertebra, consistent with distant metastatic disease.

A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lower lobe lung mass. Pathologic review confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue specimen.
- An activating KRAS G12C mutation was identified.
- Testing was negative for genomic alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2-16 mut/Mb).
- PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 72-year-old with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastatic disease to bone. Molecular profiling is notable for the presence of a KRAS G12C mutation. The patient is seen today to discuss these findings in detail.
