**HPI:**
I had the pleasure of evaluating this 73-year-old individual for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and was in their usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially treated as bronchitis without resolution. Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained which demonstrated a right upper lobe opacity. The patient was subsequently referred for further diagnostic evaluation. The patient remains highly functional with an ECOG performance status of 0, denying any weight loss, hemoptysis, or constitutional symptoms.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified Date]. This revealed a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was extensive and bulky mediastinal lymphadenopathy, including involvement of the right paratracheal, precarinal, and subcarinal stations. Critically, there was also enlargement of contralateral mediastinal and left supraclavicular lymph nodes, the largest measuring 2.2 cm. The scan showed no evidence of distant visceral or osseous metastases in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

A subsequent PET/CT scan on [De-identified Date] confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary tumor as well as in the extensive bilateral mediastinal and left supraclavicular nodal stations, consistent with the CT findings. No other sites of FDG-avid disease suggestive of distant metastases were identified.

Given the nodal findings, a brain MRI with and without contrast was performed for staging purposes and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis and staging. Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) was performed on a contralateral mediastinal (station 4L) lymph node. Pathology from this sample confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the EBUS-FNA. The key findings are as follows:
- An actionable driver mutation was detected: EGFR exon 19 deletion (sensitizing).
- The tumor was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 73-year-old, treatment-naive never-smoker with an excellent performance status who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIB (cT2aN3M0) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnosis is based on a right upper lobe primary tumor with extensive contralateral mediastinal and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, confirmed by imaging and EBUS-guided biopsy. Staging workup, including a PET/CT and brain MRI, is negative for distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion.
