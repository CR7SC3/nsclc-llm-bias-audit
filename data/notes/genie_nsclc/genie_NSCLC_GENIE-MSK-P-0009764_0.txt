**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 50-year-old who was recently diagnosed with non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago. The patient initially presented to primary care several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild dyspnea on exertion. An initial chest X-ray was abnormal, prompting further investigation. The patient has a good performance status, ECOG 1, and is here today to establish care and discuss the findings of the diagnostic workup.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following an abnormal chest radiograph, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified]. This study revealed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung. The scan also identified bulky mediastinal lymphadenopathy as well as suspicious contralateral hilar and supraclavicular lymph nodes, concerning for metastatic disease. There was no evidence of liver, adrenal, or bone metastases on the body imaging.

A subsequent PET/CT scan on [De-identified] confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, as well as in the mediastinal, contralateral hilar, and left supraclavicular lymph nodes, consistent with the CT findings.

A staging MRI of the brain was performed on [De-identified] and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent an ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration of a left supraclavicular lymph node. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed metastatic adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

The overall findings are consistent with a clinical stage of T2a N3 M1b, AJCC 8th Edition, Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the lymph node biopsy. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) analysis was conducted and returned as high, at >16 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 50-year-old with an excellent performance status who presents for initial evaluation of a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnostic workup confirmed a primary lung mass with metastatic involvement of distant lymph nodes. Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor did not identify an actionable oncogenic driver but did reveal a high tumor mutational burden. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss this new diagnosis.
