**HPI:**
This is an initial medical oncology consultation for an 80-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker with a past medical history notable for bladder cancer, status post treatment and in remission. The patient presented to their primary care provider several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. This was initially treated as a respiratory infection without improvement. Due to the persistence of symptoms and an associated 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past two months, a chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a right-sided lung opacity, prompting further investigation. The patient remains fully ambulatory and self-caring, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This confirmed the presence of a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also identified several enlarged ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes, with the largest in the subcarinal station (station 7). There was no evidence of contralateral thoracic disease or distant abdominopelvic metastases.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was performed. This demonstrated high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the right-sided mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, consistent with the findings on CT. No other sites of FDG-avid disease suggestive of distant metastases were identified. A brain MRI with and without contrast was also obtained and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis and nodal staging. Fine-needle aspiration was performed on the primary right upper lobe mass and the subcarinal lymph node. Pathology from both sites was consistent with adenocarcinoma. The confirmation of nodal involvement classifies this as Stage IIIA (AJCC 8th edition) non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained via biopsy.
-   **Actionable Mutations:** A KRAS G12C mutation was detected.
-   **Negative Markers:** Testing was negative for targetable alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2.
-   **PD-L1 Expression:** Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 shows a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
-   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is an 80-year-old with an excellent performance status (ECOG 1) who presents with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnostic workup has confirmed locoregionally advanced disease without evidence of distant metastases. Molecular profiling is notable for a KRAS G12C mutation and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%). The patient is here for initial consultation and to establish care.
