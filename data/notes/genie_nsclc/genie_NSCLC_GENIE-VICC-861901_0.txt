**HPI:**
This is a 59-year-old individual with a recent history of quitting smoking (<1 year ago) who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was associated with generalized fatigue, a 10-lb unintentional weight loss, and intermittent, dull right-sided rib pain. These symptoms prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung mass and a pleural effusion, leading to further cross-sectional imaging. The patient remains ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity due to shortness of breath (ECOG Performance Status 1). Review of systems is otherwise negative for fevers, night sweats, hemoptysis, or neurological symptoms such as headache or focal weakness.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A diagnostic CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified]. This confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, extensive mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, a moderate right-sided malignant-appearing pleural effusion, and multiple hypoattenuating lesions throughout the liver consistent with metastases. A subsequent PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, mediastinal nodes, multiple pleural nodules, numerous hepatic lesions, and multiple osseous sites, including the thoracic spine and right ribs, confirming widespread metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathological analysis of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. No actionable driver alteration was identified; the tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2. Consistent with the standard of care at the time of tissue acquisition, PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) analysis was performed and was found to be high at >16 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 59-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastases to the bone, liver, and pleura. Molecular profiling is notable for a high tumor mutational burden (TMB) and the absence of any identified targetable driver mutations. The patient is treatment-naive and presents for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and management options.
