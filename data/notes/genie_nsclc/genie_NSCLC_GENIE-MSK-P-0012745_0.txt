**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 56-year-old patient with an excellent performance status who is referred for evaluation and management of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray was concerning for a right-sided lung mass and a moderate pleural effusion. The patient was subsequently sent for a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This study confirmed the presence of a 4 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, extensive nodular pleural thickening, and a large right-sided pleural effusion. The CT also identified suspicious mediastinal and left supraclavicular lymphadenopathy.

Given these findings, a full staging workup was initiated. A PET/CT scan demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the primary right upper lobe mass, the right-sided pleura, multiple right-sided mediastinal lymph nodes, and a left supraclavicular lymph node, consistent with metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass for definitive diagnosis. They have remained ambulatory and are managing most activities of daily living, though they report fatigue and continued shortness of breath. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. They present today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
