**HPI:**
This is a 73-year-old with a history of tobacco use (former smoker, quit within the last year) who is seen today for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild right-sided chest wall discomfort. These symptoms were initially attributed to a respiratory infection. When the cough failed to resolve, the patient presented to their primary care provider. A chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a mass in the right upper lung.

This prompted a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, which confirmed the presence of a large mass in the right upper lobe, measuring approximately 6.2 cm in greatest dimension and appearing to abut the parietal pleura. No definitive mediastinal or hilar adenopathy was noted. A subsequent PET/CT was performed for staging, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe mass without evidence of hypermetabolic activity in regional lymph nodes or at distant sites. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient was referred for a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass. Pathology returned as squamous cell carcinoma. The patient has an ECOG performance status of 1, remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but restricted in physically strenuous activity by some chest discomfort. The patient is here today to establish care and discuss the diagnosis.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
*   **CT Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis with Contrast ([Date]):** Revealed a 6.2 x 5.5 cm spiculated mass in the posterior aspect of the right upper lobe, with broad abutment of the parietal pleura. No suspicious hilar, mediastinal, or supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. No evidence of metastatic disease in the visualized portions of the abdomen or pelvis.
*   **PET/CT ([Date]):** Intense FDG uptake corresponding to the known right upper lobe mass (SUVmax 14.8). No other sites of abnormal FDG-avidity to suggest regional nodal involvement or distant metastatic disease.
*   **MRI Brain with and without Contrast ([Date]):** No evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.
*   **Pathology ([Date]):** CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass: Squamous cell carcinoma, moderately differentiated.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue.
*   No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
*   Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
*   PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 73-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IIB (cT3N0M0) squamous non-small cell lung cancer of the right upper lobe. The diagnostic workup, including PET/CT and brain MRI, shows no evidence of metastatic disease. Molecular profiling of the tumor is negative for any currently recognized actionable driver alterations. The patient presents for initial consultation to review these findings and discuss management.
