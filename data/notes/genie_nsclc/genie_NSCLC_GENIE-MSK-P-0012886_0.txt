**HPI:**
This is an initial medical oncology consultation for a 66-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is referred for discussion of the diagnosis and management options.

The patient has a history of being a former smoker, having quit within the last year. They were in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. Over the subsequent weeks, they also noted intermittent headaches and a 10-pound unintentional weight loss. Due to worsening headaches and a new episode of mild word-finding difficulty, they presented to an outside emergency department for evaluation.

Initial workup included a chest X-ray which revealed a right-sided lung mass. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which confirmed a large mass in the right lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy and a suspicious left adrenal lesion. Given the neurological symptoms, a brain MRI was obtained, which demonstrated multiple intracranial lesions consistent with metastatic disease. The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary lung mass, which established the diagnosis. The patient is treatment-naive and has not received any prior systemic therapy for this diagnosis. They are seen today to establish care and discuss the findings.
