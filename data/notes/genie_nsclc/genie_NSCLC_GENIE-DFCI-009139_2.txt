**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 72-year-old who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago. The patient has a past medical history notable for prostate cancer, which has been managed separately.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and noted a 10-pound weight loss. Initial evaluation with their primary care provider led to a chest radiograph which demonstrated a left upper lobe opacity.

A follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest confirmed the presence of a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe, with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. For staging purposes, a PET/CT was subsequently performed. This study demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the primary lung mass and mediastinal nodes. It also revealed a new, FDG-avid 2.2 cm lesion in the right lobe of the liver, concerning for distant metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and revealed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient was referred for interventional radiology for tissue diagnosis. A CT-guided core biopsy of the left upper lobe lung mass was performed. Pathology confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. Given the findings of metastatic disease, the patient was referred to medical oncology for discussion of management.

At today's visit, the patient reports mild fatigue but is otherwise active and independent in all activities of daily living. ECOG performance status is 1.
