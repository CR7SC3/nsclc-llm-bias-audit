**HPI:**
This is a 63-year-old who presents for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to diagnosis, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially attributed to a viral illness but failed to resolve. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient noted progressive fatigue and mild shortness of breath with exertion. A chest X-ray ordered by their primary care provider revealed a right-sided lung mass, prompting an urgent referral for further evaluation. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago. They are otherwise active and functionally independent.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study characterized a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also identified multiple suspicious metastatic lesions, including a 1.5 cm nodule in the contralateral left lung and several enlarged, pathologically avid distant lymph nodes, including in the supraclavicular fossa.

A subsequent PET/CT scan confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, the contralateral lung nodule, and the distant nodal sites, consistent with metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe mass on [De-identified Date]. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary. The overall findings are consistent with Stage IV disease at initial presentation.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tumor tissue from the diagnostic biopsy was sent for comprehensive next-generation sequencing. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alteration. Specific testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. The tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested, as this was not standard of care at the time of the initial sequencing panel.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 63-year-old former smoker presenting with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup has confirmed a primary right lung tumor with metastatic disease involving the contralateral lung and distant lymph nodes. Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor did not identify an actionable driver mutation. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
