**HPI:**
This is a 78-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they began to experience new-onset headaches and a feeling of unsteadiness. These symptoms progressed, prompting an evaluation in the emergency department. A brain MRI was performed at that time, which unfortunately revealed multiple lesions suspicious for metastatic disease.

The patient was admitted for further workup. A subsequent staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed which identified a primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The patient was seen by Interventional Pulmonology and underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass to establish a tissue diagnosis.

The patient reports mild fatigue but remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care. They deny cough, shortness of breath, or hemoptysis. Appetite is fair. Performance status is ECOG 1. There is a past history of tobacco use, having quit over one year ago.
