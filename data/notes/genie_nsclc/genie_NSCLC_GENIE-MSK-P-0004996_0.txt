**HPI:**
This is a 54-year-old who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. Over the subsequent weeks, they also noted the onset of dull, constant mid-back pain and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds.

Initial evaluation with a primary care provider led to a chest X-ray which showed a right upper lobe opacity. A follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This revealed a 5.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated mediastinal and hilar adenopathy. The scan also demonstrated multiple hypoattenuating lesions in the liver, the largest measuring 3.1 cm, and a 2.5 cm left adrenal nodule, all suspicious for metastatic disease. Given the patient's report of back pain, a PET/CT was subsequently obtained for comprehensive staging.

The patient is currently symptomatic but maintains a good performance status, estimated at ECOG 1. They are able to perform all self-care and carry out activities of a light nature. The back pain is partially controlled with over-the-counter analgesics. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
- **PET/CT Scan ([De-identified Date]):** Confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe lung mass (SUVmax 14.2), as well as in multiple mediastinal lymph nodes. Widespread metastatic disease was confirmed with avid uptake in multiple hepatic lesions, the left adrenal gland, and numerous osseous sites including the T8 vertebral body and the right iliac crest.
- **MRI Brain ([De-identified Date]):** Performed for staging purposes, this was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.
- **CT-Guided Biopsy ([De-identified Date]):** A core biopsy of one of the accessible liver lesions was performed by Interventional Radiology. Pathology returned as poorly differentiated non-small cell carcinoma with squamous features, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the liver biopsy tissue specimen.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
