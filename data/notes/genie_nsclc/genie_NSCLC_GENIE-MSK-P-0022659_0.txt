**HPI:**
This is a 53-year-old who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a history of being a never-smoker. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they began experiencing persistent headaches, described as dull and generalized, associated with mild word-finding difficulties and a sense of cognitive slowing. These symptoms prompted an evaluation in the emergency department. The patient denies any significant pulmonary symptoms such as cough, shortness of breath, or hemoptysis. There has been no significant weight loss, fevers, or night sweats. The patient maintains an excellent performance status, continuing with all activities of daily living without restriction.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The initial workup began with a brain MRI performed to evaluate the patient's neurological symptoms. This imaging revealed several ring-enhancing lesions in the cerebral hemispheres, highly concerning for metastatic disease.

A subsequent staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed to identify a primary malignancy. This identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. No other sites of distant metastatic disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the left upper lobe lung mass. Pathology from this procedure confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung primary.

Based on the presence of a primary lung tumor with distant metastases to the brain, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IV disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
The lung biopsy tissue was sent for next-generation sequencing and biomarker analysis. Key findings are as follows:
- An activating EGFR exon 19 deletion was detected.
- Testing was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression was negative (Tumor Proportion Score <1%).
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was low at <2 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 53-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV squamous non-small cell lung cancer, metastatic to the brain. The patient is treatment-naive with an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion.
