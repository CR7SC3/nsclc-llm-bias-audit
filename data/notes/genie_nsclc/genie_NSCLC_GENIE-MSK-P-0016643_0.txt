**HPI:**
This is a 79-year-old who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and new-onset dull pain in the right posterior ribs. This was initially managed conservatively, but due to a lack of improvement and increasing fatigue, the patient sought further evaluation. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. They remain ambulatory and are managing all activities of daily living, though they note their energy levels are diminished. They deny any recent fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. They did notice a new, non-tender lump under the skin of their abdomen a few weeks ago but did not initially associate it with their other symptoms.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray performed by the patient's primary care provider was suspicious for a right-sided lung mass. This prompted a contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis on [De-identified date]. Imaging revealed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Additionally, the scan demonstrated multiple lytic lesions concerning for osseous metastases, including in the T8 vertebra and a right-sided rib. A 1.5 cm subcutaneous nodule was also noted in the right lower quadrant of the abdominal wall.

Given these findings, a PET/CT was obtained for comprehensive staging. This confirmed high FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, mediastinal nodes, the previously identified osseous lesions, and the subcutaneous abdominal wall nodule, consistent with metastatic disease. A subsequent MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient ultimately underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic review confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung cancer primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. No actionable oncogenic driver alterations were identified. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1-49% range. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be high (>16 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 79-year-old former smoker presenting with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup confirmed metastatic disease to bone and subcutaneous tissue, with no evidence of brain metastases. Comprehensive molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver mutations but did show a PD-L1 TPS of 1-49% and a high Tumor Mutational Burden.
