**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is an 84-year-old with a past medical history of breast cancer who presents for initial oncology evaluation of a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when a persistent, non-productive cough developed. This was associated with progressive dyspnea on exertion and intermittent right-sided chest wall pain. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, prompting further investigation.

A subsequent CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which identified a large mass in the right upper lobe, multiple suspicious nodules in the contralateral (left) lung, and prominent mediastinal and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. The scan also noted a suspicious lytic lesion in a thoracic vertebra. A PET/CT scan was then obtained for staging, which confirmed high metabolic activity in the primary right lung mass, the contralateral lung nodules, multiple nodal stations including the mediastinum and distant supraclavicular nodes, and the previously noted vertebral body lesion, consistent with widespread metastatic disease.

The patient was referred to Interventional Radiology for a diagnostic tissue biopsy. A CT-guided core biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed without complication. The patient's performance status is ECOG 1, remaining ambulatory and capable of all self-care but restricted in physically strenuous activity. The patient is here today to discuss the biopsy results and establish care.
