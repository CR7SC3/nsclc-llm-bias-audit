The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they developed persistent headaches and a subtle decline in cognitive function, including some word-finding difficulty. This prompted an evaluation in the emergency department at [De-identified] Hospital on [De-identified Date].

An initial non-contrast head CT was concerning for multiple intracranial lesions, and the patient was admitted for further workup. A contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain confirmed the presence of multiple enhancing lesions in the cerebral and cerebellar hemispheres, consistent with metastatic disease. A staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was subsequently performed, which revealed a primary mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with extensive mediastinal and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. A PET/CT scan confirmed high metabolic activity in the primary lung mass, regional lymph nodes, and all sites of distant disease.

To obtain a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of a liver metastasis. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. At present, the patient reports ongoing mild headaches and fatigue but is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care. ECOG performance status is 1. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
