**HPI:**
This is a 51-year-old individual with a history of being a never-smoker, referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially presented to an outside facility approximately six weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough. This was followed by the onset of nagging mid-back pain, which progressively worsened and became sharp.

An initial chest X-ray was abnormal, prompting a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This imaging revealed a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Given the new and persistent back pain, a PET/CT was subsequently performed for staging, which confirmed the FDG-avid primary lung mass and mediastinal nodes. Additionally, the scan demonstrated a new, intensely hypermetabolic lesion in a thoracic vertebral body, highly suspicious for an osseous metastasis. A staging MRI of the brain was also obtained and was negative for intracranial disease.

The patient was referred for a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass, which established the diagnosis. The patient is active and fully ambulatory, with an ECOG performance status of 1, limited only by the intermittent back pain. The patient presents today to discuss the diagnosis in detail and understand the findings.
