**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 43-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside facility approximately one month ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild right-sided chest discomfort. The patient is a former smoker, having quit within the last year. An initial chest radiograph was abnormal, prompting further evaluation. The patient reports good energy levels and has not experienced significant weight loss, fevers, or night sweats. Performance status is excellent.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study revealed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was associated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, with the largest node measuring 2.3 cm in the short axis at the right paratracheal station (4R). No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the right hilar and ipsilateral mediastinal nodal stations. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease in the contralateral lung, contralateral mediastinum, or any distant sites below the diaphragm, consistent with locoregionally confined disease.

A contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient underwent a bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)-guided biopsy of a right paratracheal lymph node. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed metastatic squamous cell carcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tissue specimen. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations. Specifically, testing was negative for mutations or fusions involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 1–49%. The Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 43-year-old, treatment-naive patient with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (cT2aN2M0, AJCC 8th Edition) squamous cell non-small cell lung cancer. The disease is confined to the right hemithorax, with involvement of the right upper lobe and ipsilateral mediastinal lymph nodes. Staging workup, including a brain MRI, is negative for distant metastatic disease. Molecular studies are notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations and a PD-L1 TPS of 1–49%. The patient is here today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis.
