**HPI:**
This is a 45-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit over one year ago) who presents for an initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was initially treated as bronchitis without improvement. Due to worsening symptoms, including some right-sided pleuritic chest discomfort, the patient sought further evaluation. They remain fully ambulatory and continue to manage activities of daily living without assistance, maintaining an excellent performance status.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest radiograph revealed a right-sided lung opacity and a moderate pleural effusion. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, extensive right-sided pleural nodularity, and a large, loculated pleural effusion. No other sites of distant metastatic disease were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A staging PET/CT scan was performed, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass and throughout the right pleura, consistent with malignant involvement. No other suspicious hypermetabolic foci were seen to suggest distant metastatic disease. A brain MRI with contrast was obtained for staging purposes and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed by Interventional Radiology. Pathology confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma with a lepidic and acinar growth pattern, consistent with a lung primary. Cytology from a diagnostic and therapeutic thoracentesis also confirmed malignant cells consistent with adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) of the tumor tissue was performed. No actionable driver alterations were identified; testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be high at >16 mut/Mb. A loss-of-function mutation was detected in the STK11 gene. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on this sample.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 45-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic (Stage IV) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology, with disease confined to the primary right lung mass and ipsilateral pleura. The patient is treatment-naive with an excellent performance status (ECOG 1). Molecular profiling is notable for a high tumor mutational burden and an STK11 loss-of-function mutation, with no identified actionable oncogenic drivers.
