**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 72-year-old former smoker who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive fatigue. This prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider, who ordered a chest radiograph that demonstrated a right-sided lung mass.

Subsequent staging evaluation with a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This confirmed the presence of a large primary mass in the right lung. The scan also revealed a suspicious nodule in the contralateral (left) lung and an enlarged left adrenal gland, concerning for metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases. The patient was referred for thoracic surgery evaluation and subsequently underwent a CT-guided biopsy for definitive diagnosis and molecular characterization. The patient reports a stable performance status, remaining fully ambulatory and capable of self-care, though with some fatigue.
