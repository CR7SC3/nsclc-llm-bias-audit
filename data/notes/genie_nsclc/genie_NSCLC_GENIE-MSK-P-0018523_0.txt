**HPI:**
This is a 57-year-old individual with a history of current tobacco use who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they began to develop a persistent, dry cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was associated with an unintentional 15-pound weight loss over the same period and increasing fatigue. These symptoms prompted a visit to an urgent care center, where a chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity. The patient was referred for further evaluation. The patient remains ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in strenuous activity, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This imaging demonstrated a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated bulky mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. A separate 1.5 cm nodule was also identified in the left lower lobe, suspicious for a synchronous primary or metastatic disease. No other distant disease was identified in the abdomen or pelvis.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe mass. Pathologic examination confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

A staging PET/CT scan was performed, which showed intense FDG avidity in the right upper lobe mass, the mediastinal lymph nodes, and the contralateral left lower lobe nodule. No other sites of hypermetabolic activity suggestive of distant metastases were identified in the neck, abdomen, pelvis, or skeleton. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed to complete staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) of the tumor tissue was performed to assess for targetable alterations. No actionable driver mutations were identified; testing was negative for alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was negative (Tumor Proportion Score <1%). The tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). The molecular profile is also notable for the presence of an STK11 loss-of-function mutation.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 57-year-old, treatment-naive patient with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT2b N2 M1b) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup confirms metastatic disease to the contralateral lung with no evidence of brain metastases. Molecular profiling is significant for the absence of common targetable driver mutations, negative PD-L1 expression, and the presence of an STK11 loss-of-function mutation. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
