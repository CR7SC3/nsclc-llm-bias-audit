**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 78-year-old individual with a history of tobacco use (former smoker, quit >1 year ago) who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when they developed a persistent, dry cough. This was initially attributed to a seasonal illness but did not resolve. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also noted mild, intermittent chest discomfort. Due to the persistence of these symptoms, they presented to their primary care provider for evaluation.

An initial chest X-ray was concerning for a right-sided lung mass, which prompted a follow-up computed tomography (CT) scan of the chest.
