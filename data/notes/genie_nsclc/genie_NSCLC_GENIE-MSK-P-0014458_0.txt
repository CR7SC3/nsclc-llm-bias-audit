**HPI:**
This is an 82-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who is referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months ago, when they noted the onset of progressive shortness of breath with exertion and a persistent dry cough. These symptoms gradually worsened, limiting their ability to perform daily activities. This prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider, which led to initial imaging studies. The patient reports some fatigue but denies fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. Performance status is estimated as ECOG 1, as the patient is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but is restricted in physically strenuous activity.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The patient's initial workup began with a chest X-ray that demonstrated a large left-sided pleural effusion and was suspicious for an underlying parenchymal mass. A subsequent CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study confirmed the presence of a 4 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe with associated ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. A large, complex left pleural effusion was again noted, with nodular pleural thickening concerning for malignant involvement. The scan did not reveal evidence of metastatic disease in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided thoracentesis of the left-sided pleural effusion. Cytologic analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells, confirming a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, not otherwise specified (NOS).

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the cell block from the malignant pleural fluid specimen. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations in *EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET* exon 14, *RET, NTRK, KRAS,* and *ERBB2*. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed, as testing was not standard of care at the time the sample was obtained.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is an 82-year-old former smoker presenting for initial evaluation of a new diagnosis of Stage IV (M1a) non-small cell lung cancer, histology not otherwise specified. The stage is defined by the presence of a malignant pleural effusion in the absence of distant organ or brain metastases. Molecular studies are notable for an absence of targetable driver mutations and an intermediate tumor mutational burden. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and establish a plan of care.
