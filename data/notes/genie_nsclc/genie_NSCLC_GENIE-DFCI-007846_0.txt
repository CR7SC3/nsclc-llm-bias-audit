**HPI:**
This is a 69-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, referred for initial oncologic consultation. The patient presented to primary care approximately one month ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and mild fatigue. There was no associated hemoptysis, chest pain, or significant weight loss. An initial chest radiograph revealed a right-sided pulmonary opacity, prompting further investigation. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. Despite the new diagnosis, the patient reports a good energy level and maintains an excellent performance status, continuing with all usual activities of daily living without significant limitation.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also revealed evidence of distant metastatic disease, including a 1.5 cm nodule in the contralateral left lower lobe and a 2.1 cm enhancing mass in the left adrenal gland. No other sites of distant disease were identified in the chest or abdomen. A staging MRI of the brain was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Histopathologic examination of the tissue specimen confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue from the lung biopsy. The analysis identified an actionable driver mutation in the form of an ALK gene rearrangement. Further molecular testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
