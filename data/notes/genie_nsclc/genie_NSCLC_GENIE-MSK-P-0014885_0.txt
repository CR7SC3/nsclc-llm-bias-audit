**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 53-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit over one year ago) who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was followed by the onset of new, constant headaches and right-sided rib pain.

The patient was evaluated in the emergency department, where a chest X-ray revealed a left-sided lung mass and a moderate pleural effusion. A subsequent CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis confirmed a large, spiculated mass in the left upper lobe, a malignant-appearing pleural effusion, and a lytic lesion in a thoracic vertebral body. Given the new headaches, a brain MRI was performed, which demonstrated multiple intracranial lesions.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the left lung mass for definitive diagnosis. Pathology confirmed adenocarcinoma. The patient is treatment-naive and has not received any prior systemic therapy, radiation, or surgery for this diagnosis. They report moderate fatigue but remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**

A 2.1 cm lytic lesion was noted in the T8 vertebral body. No other sites of visceral metastatic disease were identified in the abdomen or pelvis.
*   **MRI Brain with and without contrast [Date De-identified]:** Demonstrated three enhancing parenchymal lesions in the bilateral cerebral and cerebellar hemispheres, the largest measuring 1.2 cm in the right frontal lobe, consistent with metastatic disease.
*   **Pathology [Date De-identified]:** CT-guided core biopsy of the left upper lobe lung mass showed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin.
*   **Staging:** Based on the above findings of a primary lung tumor with metastases to the brain, bone, and pleura, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing and immunohistochemistry were performed on the tumor tissue with the following results:
*   **Actionable Driver Mutation:** An EGFR uncommon sensitizing mutation was detected.
*   **Other Molecular Markers:** Testing was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
*   **PD-L1 Expression:** Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) is ≥50%.
*   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Low (<2 mut/Mb).

**PROBLEM SUMMARY:**
In summary, this is a 53-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to brain, bone, and pleura. Molecular profiling is notable for an EGFR uncommon sensitizing mutation and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%). The patient presents with a good performance status for initial discussion of diagnosis and management options.
