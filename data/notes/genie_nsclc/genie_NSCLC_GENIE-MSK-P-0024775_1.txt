**HPI:**
This is a 76-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit more than one year ago. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they began to develop a persistent, non-productive cough and mild dyspnea on exertion. This prompted an evaluation by their primary care provider. An initial chest radiograph revealed a right-sided lung opacity, leading to further cross-sectional imaging. The patient reports a stable appetite and denies hemoptysis, fevers, or significant weight loss. Performance status is excellent; the patient is fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A diagnostic CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified date]. This study identified a 4.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was significant associated ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, consistent with locoregional nodal involvement. The visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis were negative for metastatic disease.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was obtained. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in multiple right-sided mediastinal lymph node stations (N2), consistent with the CT findings. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for any intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass on [De-identified date]. Histopathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma. Based on the integrated imaging and pathology findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Results were negative for mutations or fusions involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was performed, and the result was negative (Tumor Proportion Score <1%).

Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 76-year-old with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (AJCC 8th ed.) squamous non-small cell lung cancer of the right lung. The disease is confined to the chest with mediastinal nodal involvement and no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling is negative for common actionable driver alterations and PD-L1 expression.
