**HPI:**
This is an initial medical oncology consultation for a 78-year-old individual referred for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and was in their usual state of good health until approximately six weeks ago, when they began to experience progressive shortness of breath with exertion and a persistent, non-productive cough. Symptoms worsened over the subsequent weeks, leading to a visit with their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray revealed a large left-sided pleural effusion. The patient was subsequently sent for further imaging and evaluation. They report some associated fatigue but deny fevers, chills, hemoptysis, or significant weight loss. Performance status is excellent, with the patient remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though limited in strenuous activity by dyspnea.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified]. This study identified a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe, extensive mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, and a large, loculated left pleural effusion. No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, or osseous lesions were identified.

A subsequent MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed for staging purposes and was negative for intracranial metastatic disease.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent a left-sided thoracentesis with cytology performed on the pleural fluid. Pathology confirmed the presence of malignant cells consistent with adenocarcinoma, favoring a lung primary.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 78-year-old never-smoker who presents for an initial consultation regarding a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with malignant pleural effusion (cT2a N2 M1a). The patient is treatment-naive. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation as well as high-level MET amplification. The patient is here today to review these diagnostic findings and to discuss the implications of this diagnosis.
