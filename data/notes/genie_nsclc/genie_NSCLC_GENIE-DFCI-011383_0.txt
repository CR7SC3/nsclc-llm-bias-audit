**History of Present Illness**

This is an 83-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit over one year ago) who is referred for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they began to experience progressive shortness of breath with exertion and a persistent, non-productive cough. This was associated with mild, pleuritic-type right-sided chest discomfort. The patient initially attributed these symptoms to age, but sought evaluation with their primary care physician when the dyspnea began to limit daily activities.

An initial chest X-ray was performed which revealed a right-sided lung mass and a moderate pleural effusion. This prompted a referral to a pulmonologist for further evaluation. The patient reports being generally functional, though fatigues more easily. They are able to perform all self-care activities but are restricted in physically strenuous activity. The patient's performance status is estimated to be ECOG 1.

**Diagnostic Workup**

A staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This confirmed the presence of a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right lower lobe of the lung. There was an associated large, loculated right-sided pleural effusion with nodular pleural thickening, concerning for malignant involvement. No other distant metastatic disease was identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis. A brain MRI was performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To obtain a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) with talc pleurodesis on [De-identified Date]. Biopsies of the pleura were obtained and fluid was drained. Surgical pathology from the pleural biopsy specimens demonstrated infiltration by adenocarcinoma. Cytology from the pleural fluid was also positive for malignant cells. The morphology was consistent with a primary lung adenocarcinoma.

These findings confirm a diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer (adenocarcinoma).

**Molecular Studies**

Next-generation sequencing was performed on the pleural biopsy tissue to identify molecular drivers and biomarkers. The results are as follows:
- An actionable driver mutation was detected in **KRAS (G12C)**.
- Testing was negative for targetable alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary**

In summary, this is an 83-year-old former smoker presenting for initial oncologic evaluation of a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to the pleura. The diagnosis was confirmed via pleural biopsy. The patient is treatment-naive with an ECOG performance status of 1. Molecular profiling is notable for a KRAS G12C mutation.
