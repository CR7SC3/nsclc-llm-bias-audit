**HPI:**
This is an 80-year-old who presents for initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent dry cough. This was initially evaluated by a primary care provider and did not resolve with a course of antibiotics. Due to the persistence of the cough and the subsequent development of mild dyspnea on exertion, a chest X-ray was performed, which demonstrated a right-sided lung mass.

The patient was referred for further imaging and evaluation. A CT scan of the chest confirmed the presence of a right upper lobe lung mass with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided biopsy of the lung mass for definitive diagnosis.

Review of systems is notable for some fatigue and an approximately 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past several months. The patient denies hemoptysis, fevers, or significant chest pain. They remain fully ambulatory and self-sufficient in all activities of daily living, with an estimated ECOG performance status of 1. Past medical history is notable for hypertension. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

In summary, this is an 80-year-old former smoker with an excellent performance status (ECOG 1) who presents with a new diagnosis of non-metastatic, locally advanced, Stage IIIA (cT2bN2M0, AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer of squamous histology.
