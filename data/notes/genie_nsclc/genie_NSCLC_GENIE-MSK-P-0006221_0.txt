**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 46-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive right-sided chest wall pain. Over the subsequent weeks, they noted increasing dyspnea on exertion and the onset of new, localized mid-back pain, which was constant and worsened at night. These symptoms prompted a visit to an urgent care center, which led to an emergency department evaluation.

Initial workup in the emergency department included a chest X-ray that demonstrated a right-sided lung mass and a pleural effusion. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed for staging. This confirmed the presence of a primary lung mass, a large pleural effusion, and identified a suspicious osseous lesion in the thoracic spine. The patient was admitted for further evaluation and management, including a thoracentesis for symptomatic relief and diagnostic sampling. An interventional radiology-guided core biopsy of the lung mass was also performed.

The patient is now seen in clinic for discussion of the diagnosis and to establish care. On review of systems, they report ongoing fatigue and intermittent pleuritic chest pain, managed with over-the-counter analgesics. Their appetite is fair with a recent unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds. Their ECOG performance status is 1; they are fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care but are restricted in physically strenuous activity.
