**HPI:**
This 44-year-old is referred for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. Over the ensuing weeks, this was followed by the onset of progressive dyspnea on exertion and intermittent, sharp right-sided pleuritic chest pain. An initial course of antibiotics prescribed by their primary care provider for suspected pneumonia did not result in improvement. Due to worsening symptoms, the patient presented to an outside emergency department on [De-identified].

The patient reports a fair appetite with an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the past two months. They remain fully ambulatory and able to perform all activities of daily living, though their exercise tolerance is limited by dyspnea. They deny fevers, chills, night sweats, or hemoptysis. ECOG performance status is 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Initial workup in the emergency department included a chest radiograph which revealed a large right upper lobe mass and a moderate right-sided pleural effusion. A staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was subsequently performed. This confirmed a 5.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Additionally, the scan demonstrated multiple small nodules in the contralateral (left) lung and nodular pleural thickening, consistent with metastatic disease. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed for staging and was negative for intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic analysis of the tissue revealed invasive adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor specimen.
- **Actionable driver mutation:** An ALK gene rearrangement was detected.
- **Other markers:** The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- **PD-L1:** This was not tested.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (2–16 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 44-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. Staging studies confirmed metastatic disease to the contralateral lung and pleura. Molecular testing of the tumor is notable for the presence of an ALK rearrangement. The patient presents today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis in detail and establish oncologic care.
