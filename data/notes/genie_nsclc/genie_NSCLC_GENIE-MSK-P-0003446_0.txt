**HPI:**
This is an initial medical oncology consultation for a 34-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago. The patient was in a usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild dyspnea on exertion. An initial course of antibiotics from their primary care provider did not resolve the symptoms, prompting a chest X-ray which revealed a right upper lobe opacity. The patient was subsequently referred for further diagnostic evaluation. The patient reports some fatigue but is fully ambulatory and carrying out all activities of daily living.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed. This demonstrated a large, spiculated 5.5 cm mass in the right upper lobe. There was extensive mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, including bulky subcarinal and contralateral mediastinal nodes, consistent with N3 disease. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A PET/CT scan was performed for staging, which confirmed high FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass as well as in the extensive right hilar and mediastinal nodal stations, including contralateral mediastinal nodes. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease below the diaphragm or elsewhere to suggest distant metastatic spread.

Given the extent of mediastinal involvement, a brain MRI with and without contrast was obtained to rule out central nervous system metastases, and this study was negative for any intracranial disease.

The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue acquisition. A fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node was performed. Pathology from this specimen confirmed poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the EBUS-guided biopsy.
- An ALK gene rearrangement was detected.
- No pathogenic mutations were identified in EGFR, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, or ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was low at <2 mut/Mb.
- PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 34-year-old former smoker who presents for initial evaluation of a newly diagnosed, unresectable Stage IIIB (cT3N3M0) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma subtype. The diagnostic workup has confirmed disease confined to the chest, with extensive contralateral mediastinal nodal involvement but no evidence of distant metastatic disease. The patient is symptomatic but maintains a good performance status. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of an ALK gene rearrangement. The diagnosis and its implications were discussed.
