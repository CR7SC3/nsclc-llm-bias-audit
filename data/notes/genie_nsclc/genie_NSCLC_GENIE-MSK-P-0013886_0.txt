**HPI:**
This is a 33-year-old who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and was in their usual state of good health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they began experiencing persistent headaches and new-onset mid-back pain. The headaches were not relieved by over-the-counter analgesics. Due to the persistence of symptoms, the patient presented to an outside emergency department for evaluation.

An initial workup was initiated. A CT of the head revealed several intracranial lesions concerning for metastatic disease. This prompted further staging imaging with a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, which identified a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, mediastinal lymphadenopathy, and a destructive lesion in the T7 vertebral body. The patient was admitted for expedited workup. An MRI of the brain was subsequently performed, which confirmed the presence of multiple, scattered enhancing cerebral metastases.

The patient was referred to Interventional Radiology for a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass to establish a tissue diagnosis. The patient has remained hemodynamically stable and has a good performance status, remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care. The patient is now referred to medical oncology to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis [De-identified Date]:** Revealed a 3.5 cm right upper lobe spiculated mass with associated mediastinal adenopathy. A lytic lesion was noted in the T7 vertebral body, suspicious for a bone metastasis. No other sites of visceral metastatic disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.
- **MRI Brain with and without contrast [De-identified Date]:** Confirmed the presence of at least five distinct enhancing lesions in the cerebral hemispheres, consistent with brain metastases.
- **Pathology [De-identified Date]:** CT-guided core biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung malignancy.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry were performed on the tumor tissue with the following results:
- **Actionable Driver Mutations:** RET fusion detected.
- **Negative for:** EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF V600E, MET exon 14 skipping, NTRK fusion, KRAS, ERBB2 (HER2).
- **PD-L1 Tumor Proportion Score (TPS):** Negative (0%).
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Low (<2 mutations/Megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 33-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The diagnostic workup confirmed a primary right upper lobe lung mass with metastatic disease to the brain and bone. Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a RET fusion.
