**HPI:**
This is a 61-year-old individual with a history of current tobacco use who is referred for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside facility approximately one month ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. An initial chest radiograph was abnormal, prompting further investigation. The patient reports some associated fatigue but denies fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. Performance status is good, with the patient remaining ambulatory and capable of all self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This revealed a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated bulky contralateral mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, concerning for extensive nodal involvement. There were no suspicious lesions identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the extensive mediastinal and supraclavicular nodal stations, consistent with the CT findings. No evidence of distant metastatic disease below the diaphragm was identified.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was obtained for central nervous system staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent an endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) with transbronchial needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed invasive, poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alterations were identified. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was performed, revealing a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.

Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was characterized as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 61-year-old current smoker with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, unresectable, locally advanced Stage IIIB non-small cell lung cancer, squamous cell histology. Staging studies including PET/CT and brain MRI confirm disease is confined to the chest and supraclavicular nodes, with no evidence of distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and the absence of identified targetable driver mutations. The patient is seen today to discuss these findings and establish care.
