**REASON FOR CONSULTATION:**
Initial evaluation of newly diagnosed metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.

**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 58-year-old who presents for an initial oncology consultation. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to evaluation, when they began to experience progressive dyspnea on exertion and a persistent, non-productive cough. This was associated with right-sided pleuritic chest discomfort. An initial evaluation led to a chest X-ray which demonstrated a large right-sided pleural effusion.

The patient was subsequently referred for further diagnostic workup. A CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which confirmed the large right pleural effusion and also identified a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung. Given these findings, the patient underwent a CT-guided thoracentesis for both therapeutic and diagnostic purposes, with removal of 1.5 liters of serosanguinous fluid, which provided symptomatic relief. A core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was also performed.

Pathology from both the pleural fluid and the lung mass confirmed a diagnosis of metastatic lung adenocarcinoma. A staging MRI of the brain was negative for intracranial metastasis. The patient reports feeling better since the thoracentesis but still has some baseline fatigue and mild dyspnea with significant exertion. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care. Performance status is ECOG 1. The patient is a current smoker.
