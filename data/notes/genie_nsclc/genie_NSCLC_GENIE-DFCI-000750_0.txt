**HPI:**
This is a 68-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, referred for initial oncology consultation and discussion of management. The patient is a former smoker. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a mild, persistent, non-productive cough. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider, who ordered initial imaging.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a suspicious nodule in the right lung. A follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed on [De-identified Date], which confirmed the presence of a 2.8 cm spiculated solid nodule in the right upper lobe. There was no associated mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy.

For definitive diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe nodule. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed non-small cell lung cancer, with adenocarcinoma histology.

Staging evaluation was completed with a PET/CT scan, which demonstrated intense FDG-avidity localized to the primary right upper lobe nodule. There was no evidence of hypermetabolic activity in the mediastinal lymph nodes or at any distant sites to suggest metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. An actionable KRAS G12C mutation was detected. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2. PD-L1 was not tested. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 68-year-old, treatment-naive former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IB non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma of the right upper lobe. Molecular testing is notable for the presence of a KRAS G12C mutation. The patient has been staged with PET/CT and brain MRI, with no evidence of regional or distant metastatic disease.
