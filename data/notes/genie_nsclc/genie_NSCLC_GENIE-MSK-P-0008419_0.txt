**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 74-year-old individual referred for consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and new-onset, dull low back pain. The back pain was progressive and not relieved by over-the-counter analgesics. This prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, leading to a follow-up CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This imaging confirmed a large mass in the right lung and identified a suspicious lytic lesion in the lumbar spine. The patient has a remote history of smoking but quit over one year ago. They remain fully ambulatory and independent with all activities of daily living, though report some fatigue and discomfort from the back pain. Their ECOG performance status is 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive staging workup was undertaken.
*   **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis:** Revealed a 5.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. A lytic lesion was noted in the L4 vertebral body, suspicious for metastatic disease. No other sites of distant disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.
*   **PET/CT Scan:** Demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass, multiple mediastinal lymph nodes, and confirmed high metabolic activity in the L4 vertebral body lesion, consistent with osseous metastasis.
*   **Brain MRI:** Performed for staging, this was negative for intracranial metastases.
*   **Biopsy:** A CT-guided core biopsy of the right lung mass was performed. Pathology confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer with features most consistent with an adenocarcinoma histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the lung biopsy.
*   No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, and NTRK. KRAS and ERBB2 were also wild-type.
*   PD-L1 expression was not tested on this sample.
*   Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as high (>16 mutations/megabase).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 74-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV non-small cell adenocarcinoma of the lung, metastatic to bone. The patient is treatment-naive and maintains a good performance status (ECOG 1). Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor did not identify an actionable oncogenic driver mutation but did reveal a high tumor mutational burden.
