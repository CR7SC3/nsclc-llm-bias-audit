**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 59-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presents to clinic today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.

The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and a sense of mild chest tightness. These symptoms were initially attributed to a viral illness but did not resolve. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient noted progressive dyspnea on exertion, particularly when climbing stairs. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider, who ordered initial imaging. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. Past medical history is notable for a prior diagnosis of melanoma, which has been managed with surveillance and has no evidence of active disease. The patient otherwise feels well, with a good appetite and stable weight, and maintains an excellent performance status (ECOG 1).

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A chest X-ray performed on [De-identified Date] revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further investigation.

A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest confirmed the presence of a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated enlarged ipsilateral mediastinal (station 4R) and subcarinal (station 7) lymph nodes, concerning for regional metastatic involvement.

A whole-body PET/CT scan was performed on [De-identified Date] for staging. This study showed intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the known right upper lobe primary tumor and the mediastinal and subcarinal lymph nodes. Crucially, no other sites of FDG-avid disease were identified to suggest distant metastatic spread.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was obtained for central nervous system staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)-guided transbronchial needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathological analysis of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry were performed on the biopsy tissue. The key findings are as follows:
*   **Actionable Driver Mutation:** A sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion was detected.
*   **Other Markers:** Testing was negative for genomic alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
*   **PD-L1 Expression:** Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) is ≥50%.
*   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 59-year-old lifetime never-smoker with an excellent performance status who presents for initial consultation for a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (cT2aN2M0, AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%).
