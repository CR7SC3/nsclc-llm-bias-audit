**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 55-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. After an initial course of antibiotics from their primary care provider failed to provide relief, a chest X-ray was obtained. This revealed a right-sided pulmonary opacity, prompting further investigation.

A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest confirmed the presence of a solid mass in the right upper lobe. To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was performed, which demonstrated FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe tumor with no evidence of distant metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was also obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient then underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathology from this procedure established the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer with adenocarcinoma histology. The patient has had some mild fatigue but maintains an excellent performance status (ECOG 0), continuing with all activities of daily living without restriction. They are here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
