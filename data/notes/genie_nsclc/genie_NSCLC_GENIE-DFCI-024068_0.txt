**HPI:**
This is a 54-year-old with a history of smoking (quit <1 year ago) who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive fatigue. An initial course of antibiotics from their primary care provider did not lead to improvement. Due to worsening dyspnea on exertion and an unintentional 10-pound weight loss, a chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a right-sided lung mass and a pleural effusion.

The patient was subsequently referred for a contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis for further characterization and staging. This imaging confirmed a large primary mass in the right lung, a contralateral pulmonary nodule, a left adrenal nodule, and a moderate right-sided pleural effusion, all suspicious for malignancy. The patient has remained fully ambulatory and capable of self-care but is restricted from strenuous activity due to symptoms. ECOG performance status is 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive staging evaluation has been completed.

- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis with contrast:** Revealed a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. A separate 1.5 cm nodule was identified in the left lower lobe. A 2.5 cm enhancing nodule was noted in the left adrenal gland. A moderate right-sided pleural effusion with associated pleural nodularity was also present. No other sites of distant disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

- **MRI Brain with contrast:** Performed for central nervous system staging, this was negative for intracranial metastases.

- **Biopsy:** The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathology confirmed invasive, poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma.

- **Thoracentesis:** A sample of the right-sided pleural fluid was obtained. Cytology was positive for malignant cells, consistent with adenocarcinoma.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV (cT2a N0 M1c) NSCLC.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tissue from the lung biopsy was sent for next-generation sequencing and PD-L1 immunohistochemistry. The results are as follows:

- **Driver Mutations:** No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- **PD-L1 Expression:** Negative, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of <1%.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (reported as 9 mutations/megabase, range 2–16 mut/Mb).
- **Other Findings:** A loss-of-function mutation was detected in the STK11 gene.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 54-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. Staging workup confirmed metastatic disease involving the contralateral lung, left adrenal gland, and pleura. Molecular profiling is notable for a PD-L1 TPS <1% and an STK11 loss-of-function mutation, with no actionable oncogenic drivers identified. The patient is here today to discuss the implications of this diagnosis.
