**HPI:**
This is a 70-year-old individual with a history of current tobacco use who presents for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they began to develop a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive exertional dyspnea. This was associated with mild chest discomfort and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the past three months. An initial evaluation with their primary care physician led to a chest X-ray that revealed a right-sided lung mass, prompting further diagnostic workup. The patient remains fully ambulatory and self-caring, but is limited in strenuous activity due to respiratory symptoms, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified date], which demonstrated a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. This was associated with significant bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, with a subcarinal lymph node conglomerate measuring up to 3.5 cm. No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, or other distant visceral lesions were identified.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT was subsequently performed. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe mass (SUVmax 14.2) and in the multiple right hilar and mediastinal nodal stations (SUVmax 11.8), consistent with the CT findings. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere to suggest distant metastases. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also obtained and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient underwent a bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (TBNA) of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung primary.

The combination of these findings establishes a diagnosis of clinical Stage IIIA (cT2aN2M0) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma subtype.

**Molecular Studies:**
Tumor tissue from the EBUS-TBNA procedure was sent for comprehensive molecular profiling via next-generation sequencing. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specifically, the tumor was found to be negative for mutations or rearrangements involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was performed, which showed a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of <1%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) analysis was also completed and was found to be high at >16 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 70-year-old current smoker with a new diagnosis of locally advanced, non-metastatic Stage IIIA (cT2aN2M0) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. The patient is symptomatic with an ECOG performance status of 1. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of any identified targetable driver mutations, a PD-L1 TPS of <1%, and a high Tumor Mutational Burden. The patient is here today for an initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and its implications.
