**HPI:**
This is a 65-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, presenting for initial oncology consultation. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago. The patient has a prior history of Non Small Cell Lung Cancer. The patient was in a usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea developed. These symptoms prompted an evaluation with a primary care provider, which led to initial imaging. The patient otherwise feels well, maintains an excellent performance status (ECOG 0), and is fully independent with all activities of daily living. There is no history of significant weight loss, hemoptysis, or focal neurologic symptoms.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A chest CT with contrast was performed which identified a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also revealed prominent ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy, with the largest node measuring 2.2 cm in short axis. No suspicious contralateral lung nodules, pleural effusions, or chest wall invasion were noted.

Staging was completed with a PET/CT scan, which demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the corresponding right mediastinal and subcarinal nodal stations, consistent with the findings on chest CT. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases. Specifically, the liver, adrenal glands, and bones were negative for metastatic involvement.

A brain MRI with and without contrast was performed for central nervous system staging and was negative for intracranial metastases.

A definitive diagnosis was established via endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) with fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal lymph node (station 7). Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

Based on the combined findings of a primary tumor with ipsilateral mediastinal nodal involvement and no evidence of distant metastatic disease (T2bN2M0), the patient is diagnosed with Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the biopsy specimen.
- Actionable driver mutation detected: KRAS G12C.
- Additional immunotherapy resistance biomarker detected: STK11 loss-of-function mutation.
- Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- PD-L1 expression was not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 65-year-old with an excellent performance status and a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA, KRAS G12C-mutated, STK11-mutated non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. The patient is treatment-naive and presents today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
