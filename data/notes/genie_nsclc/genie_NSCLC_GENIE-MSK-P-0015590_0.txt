**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is an 84-year-old with an excellent performance status (ECOG 1) who presents for initial evaluation of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially attributed to a seasonal illness, but when symptoms failed to resolve and were accompanied by mild dyspnea on exertion, they sought evaluation with their primary care provider.

The patient is a former smoker, having quit over a decade ago, with an estimated 20-pack-year history. They deny any hemoptysis, fevers, chills, or significant unintentional weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and independent with all activities of daily living.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting a follow-up CT scan of the chest. The CT confirmed the presence of a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar adenopathy, concerning for locoregional involvement.

For staging purposes, a PET/CT was performed. This demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in right-sided mediastinal lymph nodes (stations 4R and 7), consistent with nodal metastases. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases. A subsequent MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

To obtain a tissue diagnosis and confirm staging, the patient underwent an endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) with transbronchial needle aspiration (TBNA) of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis.

**Pathology:**
- EBUS-TBNA of subcarinal lymph node: Invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung primary.

**Staging:**
- Based on the combination of imaging and pathology, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer.
