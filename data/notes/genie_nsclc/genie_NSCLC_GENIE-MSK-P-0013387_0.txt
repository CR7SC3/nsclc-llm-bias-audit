**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 53-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, referred for consultation and discussion of management.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive fatigue. Over the subsequent weeks, they began experiencing intermittent headaches and a dull ache in the right hip. Due to the persistence of these symptoms, they presented to an outside hospital for evaluation.

Initial workup included a chest CT scan which revealed a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, associated with mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, as well as several nodules in the contralateral (left) lung. Given the neurologic symptoms, a brain MRI with contrast was performed, which demonstrated multiple enhancing parenchymal lesions consistent with metastatic disease.

For definitive staging, a PET/CT scan was obtained. This study confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, mediastinal lymph nodes, contralateral lung nodules, and identified additional sites of metastatic disease including multiple osseous lesions (notably in the pelvis and thoracic spine), distant nodal involvement, and a hypermetabolic subcutaneous nodule in the left flank.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was subsequently performed. Pathology confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. The patient has had some symptomatic progression with worsening fatigue but remains fully ambulatory and self-caring. They are seen today to discuss these findings and establish a plan of care.
