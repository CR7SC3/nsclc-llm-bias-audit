**HPI:**
I had the pleasure of meeting this 75-year-old for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months ago, when they began to experience a persistent, dry cough and progressive dyspnea with exertion. Over the past month, these symptoms were accompanied by the onset of left-sided pleuritic chest pain and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds. This prompted a visit to an urgent care center, which led to a chest radiograph and subsequent referral for further evaluation. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and maintains an excellent performance status, remaining fully ambulatory and independent in all activities of daily living.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The initial chest X-ray demonstrated a large left-sided lung mass with an associated pleural effusion. A follow-up contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed for staging. This study confirmed the presence of a large primary mass in the left upper lobe. The scan also revealed multiple nodules in the contralateral (right) lung and extensive nodular pleural thickening, consistent with metastatic disease. There was no evidence of metastatic involvement in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones. A staging MRI of the brain was subsequently obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the left upper lobe mass. Histopathologic examination of the tissue confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin. Based on the findings of a primary lung mass with contralateral lung and pleural metastases, the patient's disease is classified as AJCC Stage IV.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor biopsy specimen. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver mutations. Specifically, the tumor was negative for alterations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. As sequencing was performed prior to 2017, PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed as part of the standard panel at that time. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be low (<2 mutations/megabase).
