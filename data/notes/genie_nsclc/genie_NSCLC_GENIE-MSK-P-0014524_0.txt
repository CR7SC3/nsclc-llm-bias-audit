**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 66-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately three months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and mild exertional dyspnea. These symptoms progressed, prompting a visit to their primary care provider. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, which led to further cross-sectional imaging. The patient denies hemoptysis, fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. They remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though symptoms are present with strenuous activity, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1. They are a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study identified a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also revealed bulky mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, including prominent right paratracheal and subcarinal nodes, the largest measuring 2.5 cm in short axis. No suspicious liver, adrenal, or bone lesions were identified.

To complete staging, a whole-body PET/CT was obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the right hilar, right paratracheal, and subcarinal lymph nodes, consistent with nodal metastases. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease below the diaphragm or in the contralateral hemithorax to suggest distant metastatic disease.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis and definitive nodal staging. A biopsy of the primary mass and fine-needle aspiration of a subcarinal (station 7) lymph node were performed. Pathology from both sites returned as invasive adenocarcinoma, confirming N2 nodal involvement.

Based on this workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained via biopsy.
-   No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or fusions in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
-   PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%.
-   Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 66-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (non-metastatic) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) with no identified actionable driver mutations. The patient presents with a good performance status for consideration of definitive therapy.
