**HPI:**
This is a 67-year-old with a past medical history notable for breast cancer, now in remission, who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they began to experience a persistent, dry cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was associated with generalized fatigue and an unintentional 10-pound weight loss over two months. The patient also noted a new, firm, non-tender lump under the skin on their chest wall. Due to these worsening symptoms, they presented to their primary care provider who initiated a diagnostic workup. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over 10 years ago. They remain ambulatory and capable of all self-care but are limited in strenuous activity, consistent with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a left-sided lung mass and a moderate pleural effusion. A subsequent staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified date]. This imaging confirmed a 5.2 cm spiculated mass in the left upper lobe with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. It also revealed multiple hypodense lesions throughout the liver consistent with metastatic deposits, the largest measuring 3.1 cm. A moderate left-sided malignant pleural effusion was confirmed, along with a 2.0 cm subcutaneous nodule in the right anterior abdominal wall, corresponding to the patient's palpable finding.

Given the findings suggestive of metastatic disease, a dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was obtained, which showed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, an ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy of the accessible subcutaneous abdominal wall nodule was performed. Pathology from this specimen returned adenocarcinoma. Immunohistochemical staining was positive for TTF-1 and Napsin-A, confirming a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the biopsy specimen to assess for targetable alterations. No actionable driver alteration was identified; testing was negative for mutations or fusions involving EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of 1–49%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
