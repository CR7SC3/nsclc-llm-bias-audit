**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for an 80-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and was in their usual state of good health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they began to notice progressively worsening dyspnea on exertion and a persistent, non-productive cough. Symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray revealed a large left-sided pleural effusion. The patient was subsequently sent for a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis for further characterization and staging. This imaging confirmed a primary mass in the left lung with an associated large effusion and some pleural nodularity, but no other clear sites of distant metastatic disease in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

The patient was referred to Interventional Radiology for a diagnostic and therapeutic thoracentesis with image-guided pleural biopsy. Approximately 1.5 liters of serosanguinous fluid were removed, with significant improvement in dyspnea. Pathologic analysis of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma. Staging was completed with an MRI of the brain, which was negative for intracranial metastases. The patient is now seen in clinic for formal consultation. They report feeling much better since the thoracentesis, with only mild residual dyspnea. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis with contrast, [De-identified Date]:** Revealed a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the left lower lobe. There was an associated large, complex left pleural effusion with nodular pleural thickening. No suspicious mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy was identified. The liver, adrenal glands, and visualized bones were without evidence of metastatic lesions.
- **MRI Brain with and without contrast, [De-identified Date]:** Negative for intracranial metastatic disease.
- **Ultrasound-Guided Thoracentesis and Pleural Biopsy, [De-identified Date]:** Biopsy of a thickened pleural site was performed. Pathology returned as poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, with immunohistochemical stains (TTF-1 positive, Napsin-A positive) supporting a lung primary. Cytology from the pleural fluid was also positive for malignant cells.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing performed on the pleural biopsy specimen revealed the following:
- No actionable driver alteration identified. Testing was negative for mutations, fusions, or amplifications in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry is positive.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) is intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is an 80-year-old never-smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with malignant pleural effusion (cT2a N0 M1a). The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of targetable driver alterations, positive PD-L1 expression, and an intermediate tumor mutational burden. The patient is here for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
