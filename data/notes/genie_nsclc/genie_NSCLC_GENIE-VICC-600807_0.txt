**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 64-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a significant history of tobacco use and is a current smoker. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately four weeks prior to presentation, when they began experiencing persistent, dull headaches and intermittent dizziness. These symptoms culminated in a witnessed seizure, which prompted an evaluation in the emergency department at an outside hospital. At that time, the patient also endorsed a non-productive cough that had been present for several months, along with mild exertional dyspnea and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the past three months. The patient has an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
In the emergency department, an MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed to evaluate the new-onset seizure. This imaging revealed multiple ring-enhancing lesions in the cerebral hemispheres, highly suspicious for metastatic disease.

A subsequent staging CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was obtained to search for a primary malignancy. This study identified a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. No other sites of thoracic or abdominopelvic metastatic disease were identified.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Histopathologic examination of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, moderately differentiated.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver alterations; results were negative for mutations or rearrangements in *EGFR*, *ALK*, *ROS1*, *BRAF*, *MET* exon 14, *RET*, *KRAS*, and *ERBB2*. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be high at >16 mutations/megabase. PD-L1 expression analysis was not performed.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 64-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT2b N2 M1b) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastases to the brain. The patient is treatment-naive. Molecular profiling is notable for a high tumor mutational burden, with no targetable driver mutations identified. The patient is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis in detail.
