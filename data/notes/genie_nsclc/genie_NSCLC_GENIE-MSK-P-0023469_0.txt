**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 74-year-old individual who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation to discuss a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough. This was initially attributed to a seasonal illness, but due to lack of resolution, they sought evaluation from their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray revealed a suspicious opacity in the right lung. The patient denies any associated hemoptysis, chest pain, fevers, chills, or significant weight loss. They report good baseline functional status, remaining fully independent with all activities of daily living and ambulating without assistance. Their ECOG performance status is estimated to be 0-1. They are a current smoker.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest was performed. This study identified a 5.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. No definitive mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy was appreciated.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT was subsequently obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the known right upper lobe mass. There was no evidence of hypermetabolic activity in regional lymph nodes or in any distant sites to suggest metastatic disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic examination of the biopsy specimen confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

**MOLECULAR STUDIES:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. This analysis did not identify any actionable driver alterations; results were negative for mutations or rearrangements in *EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET* exon 14, *RET, NTRK, KRAS,* and *ERBB2*.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mutations/megabase).

**PROBLEM SUMMARY:**
In summary, this is a 74-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, clinical Stage IIB (cT3N0M0) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. The diagnostic workup is consistent with localized disease confined to the right upper lobe. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of an actionable driver mutation and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%). The patient presents today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
