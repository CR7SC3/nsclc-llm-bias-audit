**HPI:**
This is a 54-year-old individual with a history of being a never-smoker who is seen for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside facility approximately three weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was associated with several weeks of increasing fatigue and vague right-sided back pain. An initial chest X-ray was concerning for a lung mass, prompting further investigation. The patient has no prior history of malignancy and has been otherwise in a good state of health. ECOG performance status is estimated at 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This imaging revealed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also identified multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules, several hypoattenuating lesions in the liver consistent with metastases, a 2 cm right adrenal nodule, and a lytic lesion in the T8 vertebral body.

To complete staging, a dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was obtained, which was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of one of the liver lesions was subsequently performed by Interventional Radiology on [De-identified date]. Histopathologic examination of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of a poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, with an immunoprofile consistent with a lung primary.

Based on this workup, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive next-generation sequencing was performed on the biopsy specimen. The results are notable for the detection of an actionable sensitizing mutation, specifically an EGFR exon 19 deletion.

Additional molecular analysis was negative for other driver mutations or rearrangements, including ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was performed, and the result was negative (Tumor Proportion Score < 1%). Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 54-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV NSCLC, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging studies confirmed a primary right lung mass with metastatic disease involving the contralateral lung, liver, bone, and adrenal gland, with no evidence of CNS involvement. The patient is treatment-naive. Molecular profiling is critically important, revealing an EGFR exon 19 deletion. PD-L1 expression is negative. The patient is seen today to discuss these findings in detail and to review the implications for management.
