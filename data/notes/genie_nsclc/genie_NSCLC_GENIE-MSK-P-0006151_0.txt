**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 53-year-old individual with an excellent performance status (ECOG 0) who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation to discuss a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough. This was initially attributed to a viral illness but did not resolve. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also noted the onset of mild right-sided chest discomfort and slight shortness of breath with significant exertion. Due to the persistence of these symptoms, they presented to their primary care provider, who ordered initial imaging.

The patient's review of systems is otherwise negative. They deny fevers, chills, night sweats, or unintentional weight loss. They remain fully active and continue to work without limitation.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, which prompted a follow-up contrast-enhanced CT of the chest. This study characterized a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated enlarged ipsilateral mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, concerning for regional involvement.

To complete staging, a full-body PET/CT was performed. This confirmed high FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe mass and in multiple right-sided mediastinal lymph nodes (paratracheal and subcarinal stations). Crucially, the PET/CT showed no evidence of FDG-avid disease below the diaphragm or in the contralateral hemithorax, consistent with locoregionally confined disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

The patient subsequently underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS)-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (TBNA) of a right paratracheal (4R) lymph node for definitive diagnosis and staging.

Pathology from the lymph node biopsy confirmed metastatic adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry were performed on the biopsy specimen with the following results:
*   **Actionable Driver Mutation:** An **EGFR L858R point mutation** was detected.
*   **Other Markers:** Testing was **negative** for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
*   **PD-L1 Expression:** PD-L1 was **negative** (Tumor Proportion Score < 1%).
*   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was **intermediate** (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 53-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, non-metastatic **Stage IIIA non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma** (AJCC 8th Edition). Staging workup has confirmed locoregionally advanced disease without evidence of distant metastases. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation and negative PD-L1 expression. The patient presents today with an excellent performance status to discuss the implications of this diagnosis and review the findings in detail.
