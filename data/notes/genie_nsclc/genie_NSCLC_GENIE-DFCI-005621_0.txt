**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 70-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit over one year ago) who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient initially presented to their primary care provider approximately two months ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and increasing fatigue. An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe lung opacity, prompting further investigation. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest confirmed the presence of a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe and also identified prominent ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy.

To complete staging, a PET/CT scan was performed. This study demonstrated intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the right upper lobe lung mass and the mediastinal lymph nodes, consistent with malignancy. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases. A staging MRI of the brain was also obtained and was negative for any intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right lung mass, which established the diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma. The patient is here today to discuss these findings. The patient reports an ECOG performance status of 1, remaining fully ambulatory and capable of self-care but restricted in physically strenuous activity by fatigue.
