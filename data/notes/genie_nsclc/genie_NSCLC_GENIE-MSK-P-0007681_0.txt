**History of Present Illness**

This is a 71-year-old individual referred for initial oncology evaluation of newly diagnosed non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a new, persistent, non-productive cough. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also began experiencing intermittent headaches and a sense of imbalance. These symptoms prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider, who ordered initial imaging.

A chest CT revealed a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, along with prominent mediastinal and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy.
