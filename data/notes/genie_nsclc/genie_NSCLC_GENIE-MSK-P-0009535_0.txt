**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 58-year-old individual with an excellent performance status who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they developed persistent headaches and a single episode of focal seizure. This prompted an evaluation in the emergency department, where an urgent MRI of the brain was performed. This imaging revealed multiple enhancing intracranial lesions, highly suspicious for metastatic disease.

A subsequent staging workup was initiated. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which identified a primary spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, measuring approximately 3.5 cm, with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also revealed a destructive lesion in the left iliac bone, consistent with an osseous metastasis.

Given the imaging findings, the patient was referred for a tissue diagnosis.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed on [De-identified Date]. Pathologic examination of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung malignancy.

Staging imaging, including the aforementioned MRI of the brain and CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, confirms widespread disease. The MRI of the brain demonstrated at least three distinct cerebellar and cerebral enhancing nodules. The CT scan confirmed the primary lung mass, mediastinal nodal involvement, and a lytic lesion in the left ilium. A subsequent PET/CT scan confirmed high FDG-avidity at all known sites of disease and did not identify additional areas of metastatic involvement.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue specimen.
- An actionable driver mutation was identified: a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion.
- Testing was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry revealed a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
