**HPI:**
This is a 57-year-old individual with a history of being a never-smoker who is seen today for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough. This was initially attributed to a viral illness, but when symptoms failed to resolve, the patient sought evaluation from their primary care provider. The patient denies hemoptysis, fevers, chills, or significant weight loss but does endorse some mild fatigue and dyspnea with significant exertion. The patient maintains an excellent performance status, ECOG 0, and continues to work and perform all activities of daily living without restriction.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further investigation. A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study characterized a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated enlarged contralateral mediastinal and supraclavicular lymph nodes, suspicious for metastatic involvement. No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, or osseous lesions were identified.

To complete staging, a PET/CT was obtained. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe lung mass as well as in multiple contralateral mediastinal and left supraclavicular lymph nodes, consistent with M1b disease. A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and revealed no evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

On [De-identified Date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe lung mass. Pathologic review of the tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. An activating *ERBB2* (HER2) exon 20 insertion was detected. Testing was negative for actionable mutations in *EGFR*, *ALK*, *ROS1*, *BRAF*, *MET* exon 14, *RET*, *NTRK*, and *KRAS*.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was performed, revealing a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 57-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT2a N3 M1b) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to distant (contralateral mediastinal and supraclavicular) lymph nodes. The patient is asymptomatic from their disease with an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling is notable for an activating *ERBB2* exon 20 insertion and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%). Brain imaging is negative for metastatic disease. The patient is treatment-naive.
