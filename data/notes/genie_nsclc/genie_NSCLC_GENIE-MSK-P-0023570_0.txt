**HPI:**
This is a 67-year-old individual with a history of tobacco use who presents for an initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of lung cancer. The patient presented to their primary care provider approximately six weeks ago with a new, persistent cough and increasing shortness of breath on exertion. An initial chest X-ray revealed a left-sided lung mass, which prompted further investigation. The patient reports a 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past three months but otherwise denies fevers, chills, or significant chest pain. They remain fully ambulatory and independent with all activities of daily living. The patient is a current smoker.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This demonstrated a large, 6.5 cm centrally located mass in the left upper lobe. The imaging also revealed extensive and bulky mediastinal and left supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, concerning for significant regional spread. No discrete liver, adrenal, or other distant visceral metastases were identified.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was obtained for staging, which confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary left upper lobe mass and the extensive mediastinal and supraclavicular nodal stations. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease below the diaphragm or in the skeleton to suggest distant metastasis.

A CT-guided core biopsy of the left upper lobe lung mass was performed. Pathology confirmed a diagnosis of invasive, poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

To complete staging, a brain MRI with and without contrast was performed, which was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specific testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was strongly positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) analysis was performed and found to be high at >16 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 67-year-old individual presenting with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive Stage IIIB (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer of squamous cell histology. Staging workup confirms locoregionally advanced disease without evidence of distant metastases. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and high Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB), with no identified actionable oncogenic drivers.
