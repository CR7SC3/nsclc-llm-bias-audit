**HPI:**
This is a 68-year-old individual with a history of former tobacco use who presents for initial oncology consultation. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to evaluation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. An initial course of antibiotics from their primary care provider did not resolve the symptoms. Due to the persistence of the cough, a chest radiograph was obtained which revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting further evaluation. The patient reports a 10-pound unintentional weight loss over the past three months but denies fevers, chills, or hemoptysis. Performance status is good; the patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though is limited in strenuous activity by dyspnea.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed following the abnormal chest x-ray. Imaging revealed a 4 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was also prominent ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy noted, with the largest node measuring 2.5 cm. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or other visceral organs.

The patient subsequently underwent a PET/CT scan for staging purposes. This study confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass and in the mediastinal and subcarinal lymph nodes, consistent with the CT findings. Importantly, there was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere to suggest distant metastases.

A diagnostic bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) was performed. Fine-needle aspiration of the right upper lobe mass and a subcarinal lymph node (station 7) were successfully performed. Pathologic review of both specimens confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was completed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastasis.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tissue obtained from the EBUS-guided biopsy. The results showed no actionable driver alteration. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on this specimen. The tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 68-year-old former smoker who presents for initial evaluation of a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, non-metastatic Stage IIIA (cT2aN2M0) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma subtype. Staging workup including PET/CT and brain MRI is negative for distant metastatic disease. Molecular profiling of the tumor is negative for common actionable driver mutations. The patient is here to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
