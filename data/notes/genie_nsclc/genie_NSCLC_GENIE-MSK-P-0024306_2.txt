**History of Present Illness:**
This is a 48-year-old individual with a past medical history of melanoma, who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and new-onset right-sided chest wall pain. The symptoms did not resolve with a course of antibiotics prescribed by their primary care provider. Due to the persistence of symptoms, a chest X-ray was obtained which revealed a right upper lobe lung mass.

This prompted a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis, which confirmed a large mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. The scan also demonstrated multiple suspicious nodules in the contralateral lung, several hypoattenuating lesions in the liver, and lytic lesions in the thoracic spine and ribs, concerning for widespread metastatic disease.

The patient is a current smoker. They report the aforementioned cough and intermittent bone pain, but deny hemoptysis, fevers, or significant weight loss. Their energy level is slightly decreased but they remain fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care. ECOG performance status is 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive staging evaluation was completed prior to this consultation.

**Imaging:**
- A PET/CT scan was performed for staging, which demonstrated a large, intensely hypermetabolic primary mass in the right upper lobe. It confirmed widespread metastatic disease, including:
    - Hypermetabolic mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy.
    - Multiple hypermetabolic lesions throughout the liver.
    - Numerous hypermetabolic osseous lesions involving the ribs and thoracic spine.
    - A hypermetabolic nodule in the left lower lobe, consistent with contralateral lung metastasis.
- A brain MRI with and without contrast was performed as part of the standard staging workup and was negative for intracranial metastases.

**Pathology:**
- A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed.
- Final pathology confirmed: Invasive Adenocarcinoma, non-small cell type.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry (IHC) was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1–49% range.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
- A pathogenic loss-of-function mutation was identified in the KEAP1 gene.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 48-year-old current smoker presenting with a new diagnosis of treatment-naive, Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging studies confirm metastatic disease involving the bone, liver, contralateral lung, and distant lymph nodes, with no evidence of brain metastases. Molecular profiling is notable for the absence of targetable driver mutations, a PD-L1 TPS of 1-49%, and a KEAP1 loss-of-function mutation. The patient has a good performance status (ECOG 1) and is here today to discuss the diagnosis and management options.
