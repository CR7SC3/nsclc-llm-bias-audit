**HPI:**
This is a 66-year-old individual with an excellent performance status (ECOG 0) who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation when they developed a mild, persistent, non-productive cough. This prompted an evaluation by their primary care provider, which led to a chest radiograph revealing a right upper lobe pulmonary nodule. The patient denies any fevers, chills, night sweats, hemoptysis, or unintentional weight loss. They remain fully active and without constitutional symptoms.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive diagnostic evaluation was pursued following the initial abnormal chest x-ray.

I have personally reviewed the relevant imaging studies. A CT scan of the chest confirmed a 1.8 cm solid, spiculated nodule in the apical segment of the right upper lobe. There was no evidence of mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity localized only to the known right upper lobe nodule, with a max SUV of 8.2. No other sites of abnormal FDG uptake were identified in the thorax or elsewhere in the body to suggest nodal or distant metastatic disease.

A staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was obtained and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe nodule was performed on [De-identified Date]. Histopathologic examination of the tissue specimen revealed invasive adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue.
- Actionable driver mutation: A RET fusion was detected.
- Additional molecular profiling was negative for pathogenic mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression showed a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) in the 1-49% range.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was low at <2 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 66-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IA non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. The disease is characterized by a RET fusion and a PD-L1 TPS of 1-49%. The patient is treatment-naive and presents today to discuss the diagnosis and potential management options.
