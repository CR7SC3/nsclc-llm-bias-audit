**HPI:**
This is a 65-year-old individual with a history of being a never-smoker who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation following a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent dry cough and progressive fatigue. They subsequently noted a new, non-tender lump under the skin of their chest wall. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray was suspicious for a lung mass, leading to further cross-sectional imaging. The patient reports some intermittent, dull pain in the mid-back, but is otherwise maintaining their weight and appetite. Performance status is excellent; the patient remains fully ambulatory, independent in all activities of daily living, and continues to work.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A diagnostic CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed on [De-identified date]. This study revealed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Multiple osseous lesions consistent with metastases were identified in the thoracic spine and pelvis. The scan also confirmed a 2 cm subcutaneous nodule in the left anterior chest wall, consistent with the patient's palpable finding.

A subsequent staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe lung mass. Pathology from this procedure confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma with features consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue from the lung biopsy. Molecular analysis identified an actionable sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion. The tumor tested negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 was performed, and the tumor was found to be negative (Tumor Proportion Score <1%). Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was assessed and found to be in the intermediate range (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 65-year-old never-smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging studies confirm metastatic disease involving bone and subcutaneous tissue, with no evidence of brain metastases. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion. The patient has an excellent performance status and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
