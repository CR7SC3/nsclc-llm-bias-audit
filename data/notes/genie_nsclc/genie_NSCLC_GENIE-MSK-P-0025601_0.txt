**HPI:**
This is a 59-year-old individual with a recent diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, referred for initial oncology consultation. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months ago, when a persistent, non-productive cough developed, accompanied by progressive dyspnea on exertion and intermittent right-sided chest wall pain. Over the past month, the patient also noted the onset of persistent headaches and dull, constant low back pain. An initial evaluation with a primary care provider led to outpatient imaging. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and has no significant personal or family history of malignancy. Performance status is good, though with some fatigue related to symptoms; the patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of self-care.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A chest CT scan was performed on [De-identified date], which revealed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules, consistent with contralateral metastatic disease, and mediastinal lymphadenopathy.

Given the clinical presentation including back pain and headaches, further staging studies were pursued. A whole-body PET/CT scan confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, mediastinal nodes, and multiple contralateral lung nodules. Additionally, the scan identified a hypermetabolic focus in the L3 vertebral body and a 3 cm avid mass in the left adrenal gland.

An MRI of the brain with and without contrast was subsequently performed to evaluate the patient's headaches. This study revealed multiple, small, ring-enhancing lesions in the bilateral cerebral hemispheres, consistent with brain metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the primary right upper lobe lung mass was performed on [De-identified date]. Histopathologic examination confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue.
- **Actionable driver mutation:** An activating EGFR exon 19 deletion was detected.
- **Negative markers:** The tumor was negative for rearrangements or mutations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- **Immunotherapy markers:** Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 demonstrated a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of ≥50%. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 59-year-old never-smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, with metastatic disease to the brain, bone, contralateral lung, and adrenal gland. The tumor is characterized by a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion and high PD-L1 expression. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation.
