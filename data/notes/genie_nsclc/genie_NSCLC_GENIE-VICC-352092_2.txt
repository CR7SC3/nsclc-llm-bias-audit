**HPI:**
This is a 61-year-old individual with a history of smoking (quit >1 year ago) who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient initially sought evaluation with their primary care provider for a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion over the past several months. This was associated with mild fatigue but no significant weight loss, hemoptysis, or chest pain. An initial chest radiograph was abnormal, prompting further diagnostic evaluation. The patient has no prior history of malignancy and has received no cancer-directed therapy to date.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. Imaging revealed a spiculated 4.8 cm mass in the right upper lobe. Multiple small, solid nodules were noted in the contralateral left lung, the largest measuring 1.1 cm, concerning for metastatic disease. Several enlarged mediastinal and right hilar lymph nodes were also identified. There was no evidence of adrenal, hepatic, or osseous metastases on the abdominal and pelvic portions of the study.

A subsequent PET/CT scan on [De-identified Date] demonstrated intense FDG avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass, the mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, and the contralateral pulmonary nodules, consistent with the CT findings. No other distant sites of FDG-avid disease were identified.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was performed for staging purposes and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, with adenocarcinoma histology.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor biopsy specimen. No actionable driver alteration was identified; the tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was not performed on this specimen. The tumor mutational burden (TMB) was found to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 61-year-old former smoker with newly diagnosed, treatment-naive Stage IV (M1a) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. The disease is characterized by a primary right lung mass with contralateral pulmonary metastases. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver mutations.
