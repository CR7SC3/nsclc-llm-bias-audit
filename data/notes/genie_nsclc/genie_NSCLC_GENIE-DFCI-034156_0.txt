**HPI:**
This is a 65-year-old individual with a history of hypertension and hyperlipidemia who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit approximately 5 years ago after a 30-pack-year history.

The patient initially presented to an urgent care center approximately six weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. Over the preceding two months, the patient also noted an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds, generalized fatigue, and new-onset dull pain in the mid-thoracic back. More recently, the patient began experiencing intermittent headaches and a sense of imbalance, which prompted presentation to the emergency department at [De-identified] Hospital.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
In the emergency department, a CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed. This imaging revealed a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated bulky mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also demonstrated multiple bilateral pulmonary nodules consistent with contralateral lung metastases, three discrete hypodense lesions in the liver suspicious for metastatic deposits, and a lytic lesion within the T8 vertebral body, corresponding to the patient's reported back pain.

Given the new-onset neurologic symptoms, a brain MRI with and without contrast was subsequently obtained. This study confirmed the presence of multiple enhancing lesions in the bilateral cerebral and cerebellar hemispheres, the largest measuring 1.8 cm in the left cerebellum, consistent with widespread brain metastases.

To confirm the diagnosis and obtain tissue for molecular analysis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of one of the accessible liver lesions. Pathologic examination of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma. Immunohistochemical stains were positive for TTF-1 and Napsin-A, supporting a lung primary.

The cumulative findings of a primary right lung mass with metastases to the contralateral lung, liver, bone, and brain are consistent with a clinical stage of T2b N2 M1c, AJCC 8th Edition, Stage IVB non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a baseline ECOG performance status of 1, being fully ambulatory but restricted in physically strenuous activity.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the liver biopsy specimen. No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was found to be negative for mutations, fusions, or amplifications in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

PD-L1 expression was not tested, consistent with the institutional standards at the time the sequencing was ordered (pre-2017 panel). Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate at 11 mutations/megabase (mut/Mb) (laboratory reference range for intermediate: 2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 65-year-old, treatment-naive individual with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma with widespread metastatic disease involving the brain, bone, liver, and contralateral lung. Molecular profiling of the tumor did not identify any targetable driver mutations. The patient is symptomatic from the disease burden but remains fully ambulatory.
