**HPI:**
This is a 63-year-old individual who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was initially attributed to a seasonal illness, but symptoms worsened, prompting a visit to an urgent care center. A chest X-ray performed at that time was abnormal, showing a mass in the right lung.

The patient was referred for further evaluation, which included a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This confirmed a large primary mass in the right upper lobe, along with findings suspicious for metastatic disease, including a malignant pleural effusion and enlarged contralateral lymph nodes. During this diagnostic period, the patient also began reporting intermittent headaches and mild word-finding difficulty, which prompted a dedicated brain MRI. The patient's functional status remains high, with an ECOG performance status of 1, limited only by mild respiratory symptoms.
