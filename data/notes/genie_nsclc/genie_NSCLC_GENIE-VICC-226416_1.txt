**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 69-year-old individual with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, referred for initial consultation. The patient presented to their primary care provider several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This was initially treated as bronchitis without improvement.

A subsequent chest X-ray revealed a right upper lobe opacity, prompting a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest. This study demonstrated a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe measuring approximately 5 cm, associated with extensive bulky ipsilateral mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy.
