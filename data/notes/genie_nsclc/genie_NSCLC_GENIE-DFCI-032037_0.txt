**HPI:**
This is an initial consultation for an 80-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to an outside hospital several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough. A chest X-ray was performed which revealed a right upper lobe lung nodule. The patient is a former smoker, having quit approximately 6 months ago after a many-pack-year history. The patient reports good baseline functional status, remaining active with daily activities, and denies constitutional symptoms such as fevers, night sweats, or unintentional weight loss. Following the initial imaging, the patient was referred for further diagnostic evaluation which has led to this consultation.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A comprehensive staging workup was completed prior to this visit.

- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis with contrast:** Revealed a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was no evidence of mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy. The visualized portions of the abdomen and pelvis, including the liver and adrenal glands, were unremarkable and without evidence of metastatic disease.

- **PET/CT:** Performed for staging, this demonstrated intense FDG avidity localized to the known right upper lobe mass. There was no abnormal FDG uptake in the mediastinum, contralateral lung, or any distant sites to suggest nodal or metastatic involvement.

- **MRI Brain with and without contrast:** Negative for any intracranial metastatic disease.

- **CT-guided core biopsy of the right upper lobe mass:** Pathology confirmed invasive, moderately differentiated squamous cell carcinoma.

Based on the completed workup, the patient has clinical Stage IB (T2aN0M0) disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing and PD-L1 immunohistochemistry were performed on the biopsy specimen.
- **PD-L1:** Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) is ≥50%.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (10 mutations/megabase).
- **Driver Mutations:** No actionable driver alteration was identified. Testing was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.

**Problem Summary:**
This is an 80-year-old former smoker with an excellent performance status (ECOG 0-1) who presents for an initial oncology consultation. The patient has a new diagnosis of Stage IB (T2aN0M0) squamous non-small cell lung cancer of the right upper lobe. Molecular profiling is notable for high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%) and the absence of any identified actionable driver mutations. The patient is treatment-naive and is here today to discuss the diagnosis in detail.
