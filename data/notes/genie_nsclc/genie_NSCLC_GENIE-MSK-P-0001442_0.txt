**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 56-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifelong never-smoker and was in a usual state of good health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when symptoms of a persistent, non-productive cough began. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient also developed progressive, nagging right-sided rib pain and mid-back pain, which prompted a visit to an urgent care center.

An initial chest X-ray was performed which showed a suspicious opacity in the right lung. This led to a referral for a contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. The CT scan confirmed the presence of a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, along with bulky mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Given these findings, the patient was referred for a full staging workup and tissue diagnosis. The patient has a good performance status, ECOG 0-1, and is managing daily activities with minimal assistance, though the back pain is a significant new issue.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A PET/CT was performed for staging on [De-identified date]. This study demonstrated a hypermetabolic primary mass in the right upper lobe measuring approximately 4.2 cm. There was intense FDG uptake in multiple mediastinal and hilar lymph node stations. The scan also revealed multiple, distinct foci of hypermetabolic activity in the skeleton, including in the T7 vertebral body and the right posterior 5th rib, confirming distant metastatic disease. No other sites of distant metastatic disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

On [De-identified date], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Histopathologic examination of the tissue specimen confirmed a diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung origin. Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IV disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue from the lung biopsy. The results are as follows:
-   **Actionable Driver Mutation:** A sensitizing EGFR L858R point mutation was detected.
-   **Other Markers:** Testing was negative for fusions or mutations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
-   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).
-   **PD-L1 Expression:** Not tested.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 56-year-old never-smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, metastatic to bone. The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation. The case was reviewed, and the findings and implications were discussed in detail.
