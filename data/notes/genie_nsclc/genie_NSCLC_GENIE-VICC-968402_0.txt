**HPI:**
This is an 81-year-old individual with a history of current tobacco use who is referred for initial oncologic consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient presented to their primary care provider several weeks ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. An initial chest radiograph demonstrated a right-sided lung opacity, which prompted further investigation. The patient has remained functional, with an ECOG performance status of 1, but notes increasing fatigue. There is no reported hemoptysis, fevers, or significant weight loss.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest x-ray, a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified]. This imaging revealed a large, centrally located mass in the right upper lobe measuring approximately 5.5 cm. The scan also demonstrated bulky mediastinal and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy, raising suspicion for locally advanced disease. No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, or osseous lesions were identified.

For definitive staging, a PET/CT scan was subsequently obtained. This study confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in multiple mediastinal and right supraclavicular nodal stations. Importantly, the PET scan showed no evidence of FDG-avid disease below the diaphragm or in the contralateral lung, consistent with locoregionally confined disease. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastasis.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and fine-needle aspiration (FNA) of a subcarinal lymph node. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of poorly differentiated squamous cell carcinoma, consistent with a non-small cell lung cancer primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the EBUS-FNA. This comprehensive genomic profiling identified a high-level MET amplification. The panel was negative for other actionable driver mutations or rearrangements, including EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is an 81-year-old current smoker who presents for initial evaluation of newly diagnosed, treatment-naive, Stage IIIB (AJCC 8th Edition) non-small cell lung cancer of squamous cell histology. The disease is locally advanced with extensive nodal involvement but without evidence of distant metastatic disease. Molecular analysis of the tumor is notable for a high-level MET amplification. The patient is here to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
