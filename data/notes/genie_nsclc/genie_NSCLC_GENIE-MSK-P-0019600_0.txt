**HPI:**
This is a 70-year-old individual with a history of hypertension and a greater than 30-pack-year smoking history (quit over one year ago) who is referred for initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they began experiencing persistent headaches, intermittent dizziness, and a subtle decline in balance. These symptoms prompted an evaluation in the emergency department. An initial non-contrast CT of the head revealed multiple intracranial lesions concerning for metastatic disease. A subsequent MRI of the brain with and without contrast confirmed the presence of at least three discrete enhancing cerebral lesions, the largest in the left parietal lobe.

Given the high suspicion for a primary malignancy, a CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast was performed. This identified a spiculated 3.8 cm mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. No other sites of definite metastatic disease were identified in the abdomen or pelvis. The patient has noted a mild, non-productive cough over the past few months but denied hemoptysis, chest pain, or significant shortness of breath. There has been an unintentional weight loss of approximately 10 pounds over the last three months. The patient remains fully ambulatory and self-caring, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
- **MRI Brain [Date]:** Revealed three enhancing parenchymal lesions in the bilateral cerebral hemispheres consistent with metastatic deposits. The largest lesion measures 1.5 cm in the left parietal lobe with surrounding vasogenic edema.

- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis [Date]:** Showed a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe. Prominent right hilar and subcarinal lymph nodes were noted. No discrete liver, adrenal, or osseous metastases were identified.

- **PET/CT [Date]:** Demonstrated intense hypermetabolic activity in the right upper lobe lung mass (SUVmax 14.2) and in the right hilar and mediastinal lymph nodes. Avid uptake was also confirmed in the previously identified cerebral lesions. No other distant hypermetabolic foci were seen.

- **CT-guided Core Biopsy of Right Upper Lobe Mass [Date]:** Pathology returned poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma with features consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue from the lung biopsy.
- **Actionable Driver Mutation:** An activating ERBB2 (HER2) exon 20 insertion was detected.
- **Other Drivers:** Testing was negative for pathogenic mutations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, and KRAS.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (reported as 11 mutations/megabase).
- **PD-L1 Expression:** Not tested, as this was not standard of care at the time of the initial tissue analysis.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 70-year-old former smoker who presents for initial oncology evaluation. The patient has a new diagnosis of Stage IV (cT2a N2 M1b) non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastatic disease to the brain. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for an ERBB2 exon 20 insertion mutation. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today to discuss the diagnosis and establish a plan of care.
