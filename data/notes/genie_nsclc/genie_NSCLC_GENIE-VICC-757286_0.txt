**HPI:**
This is a 67-year-old individual with a history of former tobacco use who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately three months ago, when they developed a persistent, dry cough. This did not resolve with a course of antibiotics, which prompted further investigation by their primary care provider.

An initial chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, leading to further cross-sectional imaging. The patient reports the cough but denies hemoptysis, chest pain, fevers, or chills. There has been no significant weight loss. Appetite is reported as good. They remain fully active and independent in all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 0. Review of systems is otherwise negative, specifically for any headaches, focal weakness, or new-onset bone pain.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a diagnostic CT of the chest with contrast was performed on [De-identified]. This study characterized a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the apical segment of the right upper lobe. There was no associated mediastinal or hilar lymphadenopathy.

For staging purposes, a whole-body PET/CT scan was subsequently obtained. This demonstrated intense FDG avidity within the known right upper lobe mass, consistent with malignancy. Importantly, there was no evidence of hypermetabolic activity in any regional lymph node stations (hilar, mediastinal, supraclavicular) or at any distant sites.

Given the histology, a staging MRI of the brain with and without contrast was also performed, which was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe mass. Pathologic review of the specimen confirmed a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy. This analysis revealed the presence of a sensitizing EGFR L858R point mutation. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was found to be high at >16 mutations/megabase. The tumor was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested on this specimen.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 67-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of clinical Stage IB (cT2aN0M0) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The patient has an excellent performance status. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation and high tumor mutational burden. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis in detail.
