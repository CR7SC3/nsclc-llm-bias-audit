**HPI:**
This is a 66-year-old individual referred for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago. The patient initially presented to their primary care provider several months ago with a persistent, non-productive cough and worsening dyspnea on exertion. More recently, the patient developed new, sharp right-sided rib pain, which prompted an initial workup. Despite these symptoms, the patient remains fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, though strenuous activity is limited by shortness of breath. The patient has no significant neurologic symptoms such as headache, vision changes, or focal weakness.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest radiograph revealed a suspicious right upper lobe mass. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study confirmed a large, spiculated mass in the right upper lobe measuring approximately 5 cm, with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. The scan also revealed multiple hypodense lesions throughout the liver, consistent with metastatic disease. Additionally, a lytic lesion was identified in the right seventh rib, corresponding to the patient's area of focal pain.

To complete staging, an MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed and showed no evidence of intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass on [De-identified Date]. Pathology from this procedure confirmed the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Pathology review of the lung mass biopsy specimen confirmed squamous cell carcinoma. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue. No actionable driver alteration was identified. Specifically, the tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. PD-L1 expression was not tested. Tumor mutational burden (TMB) was determined to be intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 66-year-old former smoker with a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, squamous cell histology, with metastatic disease to the liver and bone. Molecular profiling did not identify any actionable driver mutations. The patient is treatment-naive and is seen today for initial consultation to discuss the diagnosis.
