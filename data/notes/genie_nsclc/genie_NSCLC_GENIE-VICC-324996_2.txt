**HPI:**
The patient is a 57-year-old former smoker who presents for an initial medical oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and progressive fatigue. An initial course of antibiotics for presumed bronchitis did not provide relief. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient noted the onset of dull, aching pain in the right posterior ribs and a 10-pound unintentional weight loss.

Due to the persistence of symptoms, the patient presented to their primary care provider. A chest X-ray was obtained which demonstrated a right upper lobe opacity. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed. This scan confirmed a spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, along with mediastinal lymphadenopathy, a left adrenal nodule, and a suspicious lytic lesion in a posterior rib.

The patient was referred for further diagnostic evaluation. A PET/CT scan was performed for staging, which demonstrated intense FDG uptake in the primary right lung mass, multiple mediastinal lymph nodes, the left adrenal gland, and multiple osseous sites, confirming metastatic disease. A staging MRI of the brain was also obtained and was negative for intracranial metastases. The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided core biopsy of the right lung mass for definitive diagnosis and molecular testing. The patient has an ECOG performance status of 1 and presents today to discuss the diagnosis.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 57-year-old with a new diagnosis of metastatic (Stage IV) non-small cell lung cancer of adenocarcinoma histology. The patient is treatment-naive.
