**HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:**
This is a 46-year-old who presents to the medical oncology clinic for an initial consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately one month prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive exertional dyspnea. Over the subsequent weeks, they also noted the onset of intermittent headaches and a subtle feeling of imbalance. This prompted an evaluation in the emergency department at an outside hospital.

The patient is treatment-naive and has not received any prior systemic therapy, radiation, or surgical intervention for this malignancy. They report a good functional status prior to the onset of these symptoms but have noted a decline in their ability to perform strenuous activities over the past month.

**DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP:**
An initial chest CT scan performed during the hospital evaluation revealed a 3.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung, with associated bulky mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy.

Given the patient’s reported neurologic symptoms, a contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain was performed. This study demonstrated multiple enhancing lesions in the cerebral hemispheres, consistent with metastatic disease.

A subsequent PET/CT scan for systemic staging confirmed high FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe lung mass and the mediastinal lymph nodes. The previously identified intracranial lesions were noted. No other sites of FDG-avid metastatic disease were identified in the neck, abdomen, or pelvis.

The patient underwent a CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Histopathologic examination of the tissue specimen confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.
