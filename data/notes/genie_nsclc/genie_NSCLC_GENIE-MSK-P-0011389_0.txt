**HPI:**
This is a 74-year-old who presents to the medical oncology clinic for an initial consultation regarding a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of good health until approximately six weeks ago, when they began to experience a progressive, non-productive cough and increasing shortness of breath with exertion. These symptoms prompted an evaluation with their primary care physician, leading to initial imaging studies. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker. They report a good overall functional status, limited only by dyspnea, with an estimated ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a large right-sided pleural effusion. A subsequent contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed on [De-identified Date]. This study confirmed the presence of a 3.2 cm primary mass in the right lower lobe, along with extensive pleural nodularity and a large, complex right-sided pleural effusion. No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, or osseous lesions were identified. A dedicated MRI of the brain with contrast was subsequently performed and was negative for intracranial metastases.

To obtain a tissue diagnosis and for therapeutic relief, the patient underwent a thoracentesis. Cytological analysis of the pleural fluid was positive for malignant cells. A subsequent video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) was performed for pleural biopsy and talc pleurodesis. The pathology from the pleural tissue confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary. Based on the presence of a malignant pleural effusion, the patient carries a diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV (cT2a N0 M1a) disease.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the pleural biopsy. This analysis revealed an activating somatic mutation in the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) gene, specifically an L858R point mutation in exon 21, which is known to be a sensitizing mutation.

The tumor was found to be negative for other targetable driver alterations, including rearrangements in ALK and ROS1, and for mutations in BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2. Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb). PD-L1 expression was not tested, consistent with the standard of care at the time of the initial tissue acquisition.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 74-year-old never-smoker who presents for initial oncologic evaluation of a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to the pleura. The tumor is molecularly characterized by a sensitizing EGFR L858R mutation.
