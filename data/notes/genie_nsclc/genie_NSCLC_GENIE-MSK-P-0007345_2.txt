**HPI:**
This is an initial medical oncology consultation for an 82-year-old who presents for evaluation of a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a past medical history significant for prostate cancer, previously treated, and is a former smoker, having quit over one year ago.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This prompted an evaluation with their primary care provider, which included a chest radiograph revealing a right upper lobe opacity. The patient was subsequently referred for further diagnostic imaging. The patient remains fully ambulatory and independent with all activities of daily living, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed, which characterized a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the posterior segment of the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated prominent right hilar and ipsilateral mediastinal lymphadenopathy, with the largest node in the paratracheal station measuring 2.5 cm in short axis. No suspicious hepatic, adrenal, or other distant visceral lesions were identified.

For definitive staging, a PET/CT was obtained. This study confirmed intense FDG-avidity within the primary right upper lobe mass and in multiple right-sided mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes, consistent with nodal metastases. There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastasis. A staging MRI of the brain was also performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was subsequently performed by Interventional Radiology. Pathology from this specimen confirmed a diagnosis of poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma, consistent with a primary lung malignancy.

Based on this workup, the patient is diagnosed with clinical Stage IIIA (cT2b N2 M0) non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Comprehensive next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy.
- No actionable driver alteration was identified. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (9 mutations/megabase).
- PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was not performed, as this was not standard of care at the time the initial molecular profiling was ordered.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is an 82-year-old with a history of prostate cancer and a former smoking history who presents with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA (cT2b N2 M0) non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma of the right lung. The patient has a good performance status. Molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for an intermediate tumor mutational burden and the absence of any identified targetable driver mutations. The patient is here today to discuss the diagnosis and establish care.
