**HPI:**
This is a 71-year-old with a history of smoking (quit over a decade ago) who presents for initial oncology consultation for a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, non-productive cough and progressive, nagging low back pain. The back pain was initially attributed to arthritis but did not respond to over-the-counter analgesics and began to interfere with sleep. Over the past month, the patient also noted increasing fatigue and an unintentional weight loss of approximately 15 pounds. These symptoms prompted a visit to their primary care physician, who ordered initial imaging.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
An initial chest X-ray revealed a suspicious opacity in the right lung, prompting further evaluation with a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This identified a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe, extensive mediastinal lymphadenopathy, and a destructive lesion in the L4 vertebral body concerning for metastatic disease.

A subsequent whole-body PET/CT scan was performed for staging. This confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass and in multiple mediastinal lymph nodes. It also demonstrated multiple discrete foci of intense FDG uptake in the skeleton, including the previously noted L4 vertebra and new sites in the right iliac crest and a left rib, consistent with widespread osseous metastases. No other sites of distant metastatic disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis. A staging MRI of the brain was performed and was negative for any intracranial metastases.

A CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass was performed by Interventional Radiology. Pathology confirmed invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary. The diagnosis was communicated to the patient, and referral was made to medical oncology for discussion of management.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue specimen. An actionable driver mutation was identified in KRAS G12C. The tumor was negative for other targetable alterations, including EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, and ERBB2.

Immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 expression was performed, revealing a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of <1%, which is considered negative. The Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was low at <2 mutations/megabase. Further genomic analysis also revealed co-occurring loss-of-function mutations in STK11 and KEAP1, which are known biomarkers for potential primary resistance to immunotherapy.

**Problem Summary:**
In summary, this is a 71-year-old, treatment-naive patient with a new diagnosis of AJCC Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology, with metastases to bone. The tumor is characterized by a KRAS G12C mutation, negative PD-L1 expression (TPS <1%), and the presence of STK11 and KEAP1 co-mutations. The patient is here for initial evaluation and to establish care.
