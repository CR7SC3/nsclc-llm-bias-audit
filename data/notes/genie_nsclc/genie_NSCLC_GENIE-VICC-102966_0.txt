**History of Presenting Illness:**
This is a 63-year-old with a history of smoking (quit over one year ago) who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately six weeks prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and progressive shortness of breath with exertion. Over the past month, they also noted a new, firm, non-tender lump on their anterior chest wall. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider, who ordered initial imaging.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A chest X-ray revealed a large right-sided lung mass, leading to a staging CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with contrast.

Imaging studies were reviewed. The CT chest demonstrated a 4.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. There was an associated moderate-sized malignant pleural effusion. Multiple enlarged mediastinal, hilar, and supraclavicular lymph nodes were identified, consistent with metastatic involvement. A 1.5 cm subcutaneous nodule was noted in the left anterior chest wall, corresponding to the patient's palpable finding. The CT of the abdomen and pelvis revealed a 3.1 cm enhancing nodule in the left adrenal gland, highly suspicious for a metastasis.

Given the findings of metastatic disease, a brain MRI with and without contrast was obtained for staging, which was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastases.

To establish a tissue diagnosis, the patient underwent an interventional radiology-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass. Pathology from this procedure confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the biopsy. Key findings are as follows:
*   An actionable driver alteration was detected: **MET amplification (high-level)**.
*   The tumor was negative for pathogenic mutations in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
