**HPI:**
This is a 62-year-old, former smoker, who presents for an initial oncology consultation regarding a new diagnosis of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer. The patient was in a usual state of good health until approximately six weeks ago, when they began experiencing persistent headaches and a sense of imbalance. This culminated in a witnessed seizure, prompting an evaluation in the emergency department at [De-identified] Hospital on [De-identified Date].

An initial non-contrast head CT revealed several intracranial lesions, and a subsequent brain MRI with contrast confirmed the presence of multiple enhancing masses consistent with metastatic disease. This finding prompted a broader oncologic workup. The patient reports ongoing mild headaches, managed with scheduled dexamethasone, and denies any focal weakness, sensory changes, or further seizure activity. They also endorse new-onset mid-back pain over the past month, which they had previously attributed to muscle strain. Review of systems is otherwise negative for cough, dyspnea, or hemoptysis. The patient remains fully ambulatory and self-caring, with an ECOG performance status of 1.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
The diagnostic evaluation began with imaging initiated after the patient's neurologic presentation.
- **Brain MRI ([De-identified Date]):** Demonstrated multiple, well-circumscribed, enhancing intraparenchymal lesions in the bilateral cerebral hemispheres, consistent with metastatic deposits.
- **CT Chest/Abdomen/Pelvis with contrast ([De-identified Date]):** Revealed a 3.8 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe of the lung with associated mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy. Additionally, a lytic lesion was identified in the T8 vertebral body, concerning for osseous metastasis. No other sites of distant disease were identified in the chest, abdomen, or pelvis.
- **PET/CT ([De-identified Date]):** Performed for staging, this study confirmed intense FDG-avidity in the right upper lobe primary mass, mediastinal lymph nodes, the previously identified vertebral lesion, and the known cerebral metastases.
- **CT-guided core needle biopsy of the right upper lobe lung mass ([De-identified Date]):** Pathology from this procedure confirmed the diagnosis. The final pathology report established a diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

Based on these findings, the patient is diagnosed with AJCC 8th Edition Stage IV non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing (NGS) and immunohistochemistry (IHC) were performed on the lung biopsy tissue.
- **Somatic Mutation Analysis:** An actionable driver was detected, specifically a sensitizing **EGFR exon 19 deletion**. The panel was negative for alterations in ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- **PD-L1 Expression:** Immunohistochemistry was positive, with a Tumor Proportion Score (TPS) of **≥50%**.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** TMB was determined to be low at <2 mutations/megabase.

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 62-year-old with an excellent performance status and a new diagnosis of Stage IV non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma, metastatic to the brain and bone. The patient is treatment-naive. Comprehensive molecular profiling of the tumor is notable for the presence of a sensitizing EGFR exon 19 deletion and high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%).
