**HPI:**
This is an initial oncology consultation for a 79-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient has a past medical history significant for bladder cancer, for which treatment was completed several years ago with no evidence of recurrence. The patient is a current smoker.

The patient was in their usual state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when they developed a persistent, dry cough and progressive dyspnea on exertion. This prompted a visit to their primary care provider, who ordered a chest radiograph which revealed a right-sided lung opacity. The patient reports feeling otherwise well, with stable appetite and no significant weight loss. Performance status is excellent; the patient remains fully independent and active.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
Following the abnormal chest X-ray, a diagnostic CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis with intravenous contrast was performed. This study characterized a 4.2 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. It also demonstrated enlarged right-sided mediastinal and subcarinal lymph nodes, concerning for regional metastatic involvement. No suspicious lesions were identified in the liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

For definitive staging, a whole-body PET/CT was obtained. This showed intense FDG-avidity corresponding to the primary right upper lobe mass and the mediastinal lymphadenopathy (ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal stations). There was no evidence of FDG-avid disease elsewhere in the body to suggest distant metastases. A subsequent brain MRI with and without contrast was also performed and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

On [De-identified], the patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) for tissue diagnosis and staging. Fine-needle aspiration was performed on a subcarinal lymph node. Cytopathology and subsequent cell block analysis confirmed the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, consistent with a lung primary.

The cumulative findings of a primary lung tumor with ipsilateral mediastinal nodal involvement and no evidence of distant metastases (M0) are consistent with a clinical Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer.

**Molecular Studies:**
Next-generation sequencing was performed on the tumor tissue obtained from the EBUS biopsy. The results are as follows:
- No actionable driver alteration was identified on molecular profiling. The tumor was negative for mutations or rearrangements in EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, and ERBB2.
- PD-L1 expression was not tested.
- Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB) was reported as intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 79-year-old current smoker with a history of bladder cancer, presenting for initial consultation as a treatment-naive patient with a new diagnosis of Stage IIIA non-small cell lung cancer, adenocarcinoma histology. Staging workup including PET/CT and brain MRI confirms locoregionally advanced, non-metastatic disease. Molecular analysis of the tumor did not identify any actionable driver mutations.
