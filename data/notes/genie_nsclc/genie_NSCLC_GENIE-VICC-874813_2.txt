**History of Present Illness:**
This is the initial oncology consultation for a 60-year-old with a new diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer. The patient is a lifetime never-smoker and was in an excellent state of health until approximately two months prior to presentation, when a persistent, non-productive cough developed. This was initially attributed to a viral illness but failed to resolve. Over the subsequent weeks, the patient noted the onset of mild dyspnea on exertion and a sense of chest fullness. An outpatient chest X-ray revealed a right-sided lung opacity, prompting a referral for further evaluation. The patient reports no significant weight loss, fevers, or hemoptysis. Performance status is excellent, ECOG 0-1, with the patient remaining fully ambulatory and capable of all self-care, limited only by mild respiratory symptoms.

**Diagnostic Workup:**
A contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was performed as part of the initial workup. This confirmed the presence of a 4.5 cm spiculated mass in the right upper lobe. The scan also demonstrated prominent ipsilateral mediastinal and subcarinal lymphadenopathy, with the largest node measuring 2.8 cm in the short axis. There were no suspicious lesions identified in the contralateral lung, liver, adrenal glands, or bones.

A subsequent PET/CT scan was performed for staging purposes. This demonstrated intense FDG-avidity in the primary right upper lobe mass as well as in the right paratracheal and subcarinal nodal stations, consistent with the CT findings. No other sites of FDG-avid disease suggestive of distant metastases were identified.

A dedicated MRI of the brain with and without contrast was performed to complete staging and was negative for any evidence of intracranial metastatic disease.

The patient underwent bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) and transbronchial needle aspiration (TBNA) of a subcarinal (station 7) lymph node. Pathologic analysis of the specimen confirmed the diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma.

**Molecular Studies:**

- **Actionable driver mutation:** An ALK gene rearrangement was detected.
- **Other markers:** No pathogenic mutations were identified in EGFR, ROS1, BRAF, MET exon 14, RET, NTRK, KRAS, or ERBB2.
- **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Intermediate (2–16 mut/Mb).

**Problem Summary:**
This is a 60-year-old never-smoker who presents with a new diagnosis of ALK-rearranged non-small cell lung adenocarcinoma. Staging workup, including PET/CT and brain MRI, has confirmed locoregionally advanced disease with involvement of mediastinal lymph nodes but no evidence of distant metastases, consistent with AJCC Stage IIIA disease. The patient has an excellent performance status and is now seen for discussion of findings and to consider therapeutic options.
